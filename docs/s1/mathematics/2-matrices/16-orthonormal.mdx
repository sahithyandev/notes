## Orthonormal Vectors

A set of $n$ column vectors is orthonormal **iff**:

- They are pairwise orthogonal **AND**
- $\forall i \in \{1,\dots,n\},\; \|v_i\| = 1$

## Orthonormal Matrix

A matrix $Q$ whose columns are orthonormal. For real matrices: $Q^T Q = I$ (orthogonal matrix). For complex matrices: $Q^* Q = I$ (unitary matrix).

Key property: $Q^{-1} = Q^T$ (real) or $Q^{-1} = Q^*$ (complex).

## Why Orthonormal Bases

- Preserve lengths and angles under transformation: $\|Qx\| = \|x\|$.
- Coordinates in an orthonormal basis are computed by a simple dot product, no matrix inversion needed.
- Numerically stable: no accumulation of rounding errors across operations.

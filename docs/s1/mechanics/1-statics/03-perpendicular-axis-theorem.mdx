Applies only to flat (planar) objects lying in the $xy$-plane. For any 2 perpendicular axes $x$, $y$ in the plane of the object and a third axis $z$ perpendicular to the plane:

$$
I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}
$$

The same holds for any other pair of in-plane perpendicular axes $m$, $n$ sharing the same $z$:

$$
I_{zz} = I_{mm} + I_{nn}
$$

If $I_{xx}$ is at maximum, $I_{yy}$ is at minimum (their sum is fixed).

## Derivation

For a mass element $dm$ at position $(x, y)$:

$$
I_{zz} = \int r^2 \,\text{d}m = \int (x^2 + y^2)\,\text{d}m = \int y^2\,\text{d}m + \int x^2\,\text{d}m = I_{xx} + I_{yy}
$$

## Example

Thin disk of radius $R$ and mass $M$: $I_{xx} = I_{yy} = \dfrac{MR^2}{4}$ by symmetry.

$$
I_{zz} = \frac{MR^2}{4} + \frac{MR^2}{4} = \frac{MR^2}{2}
$$

<Note>

Perpendicular axis theorem does not apply to 3D solids. Use the [parallel axis theorem](/s1/mechanics/statics/parallel-axis-theorem) for shifting axes, not for combining them.

</Note>

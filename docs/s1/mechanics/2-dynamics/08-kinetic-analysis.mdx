## D'Alembert's Principle

By this principle, a particle or a system of particles moving with a constant
acceleration is known as in dynamic equilibrium.

### For a Particle

$$
F - ma = 0
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="F">resultant applied force</Sym>
  <Sym s="m">mass of the particle</Sym>
  <Sym s="a">acceleration of the particle</Sym>
</Symbols>

$ma$ is known as the inertia force which is fictious.

### For a System of Particles

The equation can be rewritten as:

$$
F = \sum m_ia_i = F = ma_G
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="F">resultant applied force</Sym>
  <Sym s="m_i">mass of a particle</Sym>
  <Sym s="a_i">acceleration of a particle</Sym>
  <Sym s="m">total mass</Sym>
  <Sym s="a_G">acceleration of the center of mass</Sym>
</Symbols>

## Kinetics of a Rigid Body

Both translational and rotational aspects of the motion must be considered.

$$
F=ma
\;\;\;
\text{and}
\;\;\;
M=I\alpha
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="F">resultant applied force</Sym>
  <Sym s="m">mass of the body</Sym>
  <Sym s="a">acceleration of the body</Sym>
  <Sym s="M">moment of the resultant force</Sym>
  <Sym s="I">mass moment of inertia</Sym>
  <Sym s="\alpha">angular acceleration of the body</Sym>
</Symbols>

## General Plane Motion

$$
\sum M_G = I_G \alpha
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="M_G">moment about center of mass</Sym>
  <Sym s="I_G">mass moment of inertia about center of mass</Sym>
  <Sym s="\alpha">angular acceleration of the body</Sym>
</Symbols>

Let $O$ be a point other than $G$.

$$
\sum M_O = I_G \alpha + ma_G r_{OG}
$$

### Kinetic Energy

$$
T = \text{translational kinetic energy} + \text{rotational kinetic energy}
$$

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}mv_G^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_G\omega^2
$$

## Center of Percussion

The point on an object attached to a pivot where a perpendicular impact will
produce no reactive shock at the pivot.

Translational and rotational motions cancel at the pivot when an impulsive blow
is struck at the center of percussion.

$$
l_{\text{P/G}} = \frac{k_\text{G}^2}{\text{OG}}
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="\text{P}">Center of percussion</Sym>
  <Sym s="\text{G}">Center of gravity</Sym>
  <Sym s="\text{O}">Pivot</Sym>
</Symbols>

For a uniform rod:

$$
l_{\text{P/G}} = \frac{L}{6}
$$

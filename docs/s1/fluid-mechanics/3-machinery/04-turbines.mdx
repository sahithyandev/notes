Used to generate electricity.

### Runner

A wheel with buckets attached. Mounted on a shaft.

## Types of Turbines

### Reaction Turbines

<Aka>pressure turbines</Aka>. Similar to pumps; but operating in reverse
direction (direction of fluid flow and energy transfer). Guide vanes are placed
to guide fluid flow onto the runner.

3 types of reaction turbines based on the direction of fluid flow.

#### Axial Flow

<Aka>Kaplan turbine</Aka>. Commonly used to get a head output of 3 to 70m.

#### Radial Flow

<Aka>Francis turbine</Aka>. Commonly used to get a head output of 30 to 500m.

### Impulse Turbines

<Aka>velocity turbines</Aka>. Used for high heads. Highly efficient. High
velocity jet is focused on the buckets of the runner.

Efficiency of an impulse turbine is given by:

$$
\mu = \frac{1}{v_1^2}(2u)(v_1 - u)(1+k\cos\beta)
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="v_1">velocity of the jet of fluid</Sym>
  <Sym s="u">velocity of the bucket</Sym>
  <Sym s="k">loss coefficient (a little less than 1)</Sym>
  <Sym s="\beta">angle of deflection of fluid inside the bucket</Sym>
</Symbols>

$\mu$ can be considered as a function of $u$. And from that, the turbine works
at maximum efficiency when $2u=v_1$.

Multiphase material made from 2 or more distinct constituent materials. Designed to combine properties that no single material provides.

Each composite has 2 phases:

- Matrix  
  Continuous phase. Holds the reinforcement in place and transfers load.
- Reinforcement  
  Dispersed phase. Provides the primary strength or stiffness.

## Classification

- Particle-reinforced  
  Matrix with dispersed particles (e.g. concrete: cement matrix + aggregate particles).
- Fiber-reinforced  
  Matrix with embedded fibers (e.g. GFRP, CFRP). Fibers carry most of the load.
- Structural  
  Layers of different materials bonded together (e.g. plywood, honeycomb panels).

## Rule of Mixtures

Longitudinal elastic modulus of a fiber-reinforced composite:

$$
E_c = V_m E_m + V_f E_f
$$

Here:

- $V_m$, $V_f$: volume fractions of matrix and fiber
- $E_m$, $E_f$: elastic moduli of matrix and fiber

## Examples

- Concrete  
  Cement matrix + aggregate. High compressive strength.
- GFRP (glass fiber reinforced polymer)  
  Glass fiber + polymer matrix. Not brittle.
- CFRP (carbon fiber reinforced polymer)  
  Carbon fiber + epoxy. Very high strength-to-weight ratio.

<Note>

Glass fiber is a shape, not a separate material. Glass is drawn into thin fibers to increase flexibility before embedding in the polymer matrix.

</Note>

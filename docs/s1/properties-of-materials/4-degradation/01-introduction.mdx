Loss of functional properties of a material due to environmental interaction.

## Types

- Corrosion  
  Electrochemical degradation of metals. Requires an electrolyte and electron transfer.
- Oxidation  
  Reaction with oxygen. Forms a surface oxide layer. Common in metals at high temperatures.
- Swelling  
  Polymer absorbs a solvent and expands. Weakens the structure.
- Dissolution  
  Gradual dissolving of the material in a solvent.

## Rate-Affecting Factors

- Temperature  
  Higher temperature accelerates all degradation mechanisms.
- Concentration of reactant  
  Higher concentration increases reaction rate.
- Surface area exposed  
  Larger area means more reaction sites.
- pH  
  Acidic or alkaline environments accelerate corrosion in metals.

## Resistance of Ceramics

Ceramics have ionic or covalent bonds with no free electrons. Electrochemical corrosion requires free electrons, so it does not occur. Ceramics are also chemically stable in most solvents. That's why glass is used to contain liquids and store reactive chemicals.

<Note>

Degradation of metals (corrosion, oxidation) and polymers (swelling, UV degradation) are the primary focus of this section. Ceramics are only degraded by extreme chemical or thermal attack.

</Note>

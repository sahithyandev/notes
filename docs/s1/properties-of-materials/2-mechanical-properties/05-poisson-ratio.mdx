A tensile stress in a particular direction causes extension ($\epsilon_z$) in that direction and contraction in the other 2 directions ($\epsilon_x$, $\epsilon_y$). For isotropic materials:

$$
\nu = -\frac{\epsilon_x}{\epsilon_z} = -\frac{\epsilon_y}{\epsilon_z}
$$

For metals, $\nu = 0.34$ if not given. Maximum possible value is $\nu = 0.5$.

## Isotropic Materials

Homogeneous materials where properties are direction-independent. $\epsilon_x = \epsilon_y$ under uniaxial stress.

## Relation to Elastic Moduli

$$
G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)} \qquad K = \frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)}
$$

Here:

- $G$: shear modulus
- $K$: bulk modulus
- $E$: Young's modulus

## Biaxial Stress State

When stresses $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_y$ both act in the $xy$-plane, the strain in $z$ is:

$$
\epsilon_z = -\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)
$$

## Why Rubber Has $\nu = 0.5$

Rubber is nearly incompressible: volume does not change under stress. Volume conservation requires $\nu = 0.5$ exactly. From the bulk modulus relation, $K \to \infty$ as $\nu \to 0.5$, which corresponds to a perfectly incompressible material.

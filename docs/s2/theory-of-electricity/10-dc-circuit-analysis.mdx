## Node Voltage Method

Applies KCL at each non-reference node to find all node voltages.

Procedure:

1. Select 1 node as reference (ground, $V = 0$).
2. Assign voltages $V_1, V_2, \ldots$ to the remaining nodes.
3. Write KCL at each non-reference node: sum of currents leaving = 0.
4. Express each current as $(V_i - V_j)/R$.
5. Solve the linear system.

<Note>

When a voltage source connects 2 nodes directly, treat them as a supernode: write KVL across the source and one combined KCL equation.

</Note>

## Mesh Current Method

Applies KVL around each independent loop.

Procedure:

1. Identify all meshes (innermost loops of a planar circuit).
2. Assign a clockwise mesh current $I_1, I_2, \ldots$ to each.
3. Write KVL for each mesh: $\sum V_{\text{drops}} = 0$.
4. Solve for mesh currents; branch currents are algebraic sums of contributing mesh currents.

<Note>

When a current source lies on the boundary between 2 meshes, form a supermesh: write 1 KVL equation around both combined, plus 1 KCL constraint relating the 2 mesh currents to the source.

</Note>

## Superposition Theorem

The response (voltage or current) at any element equals the sum of responses due to each independent source acting alone.

To isolate a source:

- Replace all other voltage sources with short circuits.
- Replace all other current sources with open circuits.

<Note>

Superposition does not apply to power. $P = I^2R$ is non-linear, so individual power contributions cannot be summed.

</Note>

## Thevenin's Theorem

Any linear 2-terminal network can be replaced by a voltage source $V_{th}$ in series with a resistance $R_{th}$.

$$
V_{th} = V_{oc}
$$

$V_{oc}$: open-circuit voltage at the terminals.

$$
R_{th} = \frac{V_{oc}}{I_{sc}}
$$

$I_{sc}$: short-circuit current at the terminals.

Alternatively: deactivate all independent sources and compute the equivalent resistance seen at the terminals.

## Norton's Theorem

Dual of Thevenin's theorem. Any linear 2-terminal network can be replaced by a current source $I_N$ in parallel with $R_N$.

$$
I_N = I_{sc}
$$

$$
R_N = R_{th}
$$

The 2 forms are interconvertible:

$$
V_{th} = I_N R_N
$$

## DC Time Constant

Time for a capacitor to charge through a resistor from $0\,\text{V}$ to $63.2\%$ of the applied DC voltage.

Alternatively: time to discharge to $36.8\%$ of the initial charge voltage.

$$
\tau_{RC} = RC
$$

$$
\tau_{RL} = \frac{L}{R}
$$

Here:

- $R$: resistance (ohms)
- $C$: capacitance (farads)
- $L$: inductance (henries)

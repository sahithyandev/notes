$$
F(\omega) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\, e^{-j\omega t}\,\text{d}t
$$

$F(\omega)$ is a continuous function of $\omega$ called the **spectrum** of $f(t)$.

Used for aperiodic signals. Applied in telecommunications and transient circuit analysis where circuit parameters are frequency-dependent.

## Inverse Transform

$$
f(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\omega)\, e^{j\omega t}\,\text{d}\omega
$$

## Properties

- Linearity  
  $\mathcal{F}\{af(t) + bg(t)\} = aF(\omega) + bG(\omega)$

- Time shift  
  $\mathcal{F}\{f(t - t_0)\} = e^{-j\omega t_0} F(\omega)$

- Differentiation  
  $\mathcal{F}\{f'(t)\} = j\omega\, F(\omega)$

- Time scaling  
  $\mathcal{F}\{f(at)\} = \dfrac{1}{|a|} F\!\left(\dfrac{\omega}{a}\right)$

- Convolution  
  $\mathcal{F}\{(f * g)(t)\} = 2\pi\, F(\omega)\, G(\omega)$

<Note>

The factor of $2\pi$ in the convolution property follows from the $\frac{1}{2\pi}$ in the forward transform definition. With the symmetric convention $F(\omega) = \int f(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,\text{d}t$, convolution becomes $F(\omega)G(\omega)$ without the factor.

</Note>

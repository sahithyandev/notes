The LUP decomposition of an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is the triple $(L, U, P)$ satisfying

$$
PA = LU
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="L">
    unit lower triangular, $n \times n$ with all diagonal entries $1$
  </Sym>
  <Sym s="U">upper triangular, $n \times n$</Sym>
  <Sym s="P">permutation matrix, $n \times n$</Sym>
</Symbols>

## Properties

- $P$ reorders the rows of $A$ as in [partial pivoting](/s5/numerical-methods/gaussian-elimination#partial-pivoting).
- Exists for any nonsingular $A$.
- Not unique.
- More robust than LU without pivoting, at about the same cost.

## Comparison with LU Decomposition

The [LU decomposition](/s5/numerical-methods/lu-decomposition) factors $A$ as $A = LU$, without row interchanges. LUP selects the pivot row by size.

- Existence  
  A zero pivot stops the elimination. LUP always factors a nonsingular $A$.
- Stability  
  A small pivot gives large multipliers and amplifies rounding error. Partial pivoting keeps every multiplier at most $1$ in size.
- Cost  
  Factorization costs $O(n^3)$ for both. Each solve costs $O(n^2)$. LUP adds the row swaps and the permutation of $\vec{b}$.

### When to Use Which

LUP is the default for a general dense matrix. The pivot sizes are unknown in advance.

Use LU without pivoting when the pivots are safe by construction.

- Diagonally dominant $A$  
  Every pivot is nonzero without a swap.
- Symmetric positive definite $A$  
  Every leading principal minor is positive.
- Banded or sparse $A$  
  Row interchanges would add fill-in.

## Solving $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$

$PA\vec{x} = P\vec{b}$ becomes $LU\vec{x} = P\vec{b}$.

- Solve $L\vec{y} = P\vec{b}$ by forward substitution.
- Solve $U\vec{x} = \vec{y}$ by [back substitution](/s5/numerical-methods/gaussian-elimination#back-substitution).

## Recursive Leading-Row-Column Algorithm

Partition an $n \times n$ matrix into blocks

$$
\begin{pmatrix} \bar{a}_{11} & \bar{a}_{12}^T \\ \bar{a}_{21} & \bar{A}_{22} \end{pmatrix}
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="\bar{a}_{11}">$1 \times 1$ pivot</Sym>
  <Sym s="\bar{a}_{12}^T">$1 \times (n-1)$ rest of the pivot row</Sym>
  <Sym s="\bar{a}_{21}">$(n-1) \times 1$ rest of the pivot column</Sym>
  <Sym s="\bar{A}_{22}">$(n-1) \times (n-1)$ remaining block</Sym>
</Symbols>

The Schur complement of $\bar{a}_{11}$ is

$$
A' = \bar{A}_{22} - \frac{\bar{a}_{21}\bar{a}_{12}^T}{\bar{a}_{11}}
$$

Base case $n = 1$ gives $L = (1)$, $U = (a)$, $P = (1)$.

Recursive step

- Pick row $i$ with the largest $|a_{i1}|$. Let $P_1$ swap row $i$ with row $1$.
- Let $\bar{A} = P_1 A$ and partition it as above.
- Compute the multipliers $\bar{a}_{21}/\bar{a}_{11}$ and the Schur complement $A'$.
- Recurse on $A'$ to get $P_2 A' = L_2 U_2$.
- Combine

$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \vec{0}^T \\ \vec{0} & P_2 \end{pmatrix} P_1, \quad
L = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \vec{0}^T \\ P_2\bar{a}_{21}/\bar{a}_{11} & L_2 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
U = \begin{pmatrix} \bar{a}_{11} & \bar{a}_{12}^T \\ \vec{0} & U_2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Find $L$, $U$, $P$ for

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 5 & 5 \\ 2 & 9 & 0 \\ 6 & 8 & 8 \end{pmatrix}
$$

<Solution>

Level 1, $n = 3$. Column $1$ is $(0, 2, 6)^T$, so the pivot is $6$ in row $3$. $P_1$ swaps rows $1$ and $3$.

$$
\bar{A} = P_1 A = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 8 & 8 \\ 2 & 9 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 5 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\frac{\bar{a}_{21}}{\bar{a}_{11}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
A' = \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 0 \\ 5 & 5 \end{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{6}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}(8 \enspace 8) = \begin{pmatrix} 19/3 & -8/3 \\ 5 & 5 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Level 2, $n = 2$. Column $1$ of $A'$ is $(19/3, 5)^T$, so the pivot $19/3$ is already in row $1$ and $P_2 = I_2$.

$$
\frac{5}{19/3} = \frac{15}{19}, \quad
A'' = 5 - \frac{5 \cdot (-8/3)}{19/3} = \frac{135}{19}
$$

Level 3, $n = 1$. $L = (1)$, $U = (135/19)$, $P = (1)$.

Combining back up

$$
L_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 15/19 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad U_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 19/3 & -8/3 \\ 0 & 135/19 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
L = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1/3 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 15/19 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
U = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 8 & 8 \\ 0 & 19/3 & -8/3 \\ 0 & 0 & 135/19 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Check, row 3 of $LU$

$$
\tfrac{15}{19}\left(0, \tfrac{19}{3}, -\tfrac{8}{3}\right) + \left(0, 0, \tfrac{135}{19}\right) = (0, 5, 5)
$$

</Solution>

### Example 2

Using $P$, $L$, $U$ from Example 1, solve $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ for $\vec{b} = (10, 11, 22)^T$.

<Solution>

$$
P\vec{b} = (22, 11, 10)^T
$$

Forward substitution, $L\vec{y} = P\vec{b}$

$$
y_1 = 22
$$

$$
y_2 = 11 - \frac{1}{3}(22) = \frac{11}{3}
$$

$$
y_3 = 10 - \frac{15}{19}\cdot\frac{11}{3} = \frac{135}{19}
$$

Back substitution, $U\vec{x} = \vec{y}$

$$
x_3 = \frac{135/19}{135/19} = 1
$$

$$
x_2 = \frac{11/3 + (8/3)(1)}{19/3} = 1
$$

$$
x_1 = \frac{22 - 8(1) - 8(1)}{6} = 1
$$

$$
\vec{x} = (1, 1, 1)^T
$$

</Solution>

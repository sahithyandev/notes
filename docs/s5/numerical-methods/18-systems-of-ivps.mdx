An $m$-dimensional system of first-order [IVPs](/s5/numerical-methods/initial-value-problem) is

<Formula>

$$
\mathbf{u}'(t) = \mathbf{f}(t, \mathbf{u}(t)), \qquad \mathbf{u}(a) = \boldsymbol{\alpha}
$$

</Formula>

where $\mathbf{u} = (u_1, \ldots, u_m)^T$, $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_m)^T$, and $\mathbf{f} = (f_1, \ldots, f_m)^T$. The goal is to approximate $\mathbf{u}(t)$ for $a \leq t \leq b$.

<Note>

Vector notation expresses every component equation at once. It makes the existence result and numerical methods apply to systems of any dimension without repeating component indices.

</Note>

[The Lipschitz condition](/s5/numerical-methods/initial-value-problem#lipschitz-condition) applies unchanged. Replace the scalar variables with vectors and absolute values with a vector norm:

<Formula>

$$
\lVert \mathbf{f}(t, \mathbf{u}) - \mathbf{f}(t, \mathbf{z}) \rVert \leq L \lVert \mathbf{u} - \mathbf{z} \rVert
$$

</Formula>

for all $(t, \mathbf{u}), (t, \mathbf{z}) \in D \subseteq \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^m$.

The [existence and uniqueness result](/s5/numerical-methods/initial-value-problem#existence-and-uniqueness) also applies with this condition. A continuous, bounded Jacobian of $\mathbf{f}$ is sufficient.

[Order 4 Runge-Kutta](/s5/numerical-methods/runge-kutta-methods#order-4-runge-kutta) applies unchanged. Use $\mathbf{w}_i$, $\mathbf{k}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{k}_4$, and $\mathbf{f}$ in place of $w_i$, $k_1, \ldots, k_4$, and $f$. Start with $\mathbf{w}_0 = \boldsymbol{\alpha}$. Then $\mathbf{u}(t_i) \approx \mathbf{w}_i$.

## Reducing a Higher-Order IVP

An $m$th-order IVP

<Formula>

$$
y^{(m)}(t) = f\big(t, y, y', \ldots, y^{(m-1)}\big), \quad a \leq t \leq b
$$

</Formula>

with $y(a) = \alpha_1, y'(a) = \alpha_2, \ldots, y^{(m-1)}(a) = \alpha_m$ becomes a first-order system by setting $\mathbf{u} = (y, y', \ldots, y^{(m-1)})^T$

<Formula>

$$
\mathbf{u}' = \begin{pmatrix}
u_2 \\
u_3 \\
\vdots \\
u_m \\
f(t, u_1, \ldots, u_m)
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{u}(a) = \begin{pmatrix}
\alpha_1 \\
\alpha_2 \\
\vdots \\
\alpha_m
\end{pmatrix}
$$

</Formula>

Solving the system by order 4 Runge-Kutta, the first component of $\mathbf{w}_i$ approximates $y(t_i)$.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Reduce

<Formula>

$$
y'' - 2y' + 2y = e^{2t}\sin t, \quad 0 \leq t \leq 1, \quad y(0) = -0.4,\ y'(0) = -0.6
$$

</Formula>

to a first-order system, then take 1 step of order 4 Runge-Kutta with $h = 0.5$.

<Solution>

Set $\mathbf{u} = (y, y')^T$.

$$
\mathbf{u}' = \mathbf{f}(t, \mathbf{u}) =
\begin{pmatrix}
u_2 \\
2u_2 - 2u_1 + e^{2t}\sin t
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf{u}(0) = \begin{pmatrix} -0.4 \\ -0.6 \end{pmatrix}
$$

For $h = 0.5$:

$$
\mathbf{k}_1 = 0.5\, \mathbf{f}\left(0, \begin{pmatrix} -0.4 \\ -0.6 \end{pmatrix}\right)
= \begin{pmatrix} -0.3 \\ -0.2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{k}_2 = 0.5\, \mathbf{f}\left(0.25, \begin{pmatrix} -0.55 \\ -0.7 \end{pmatrix}\right)
= \begin{pmatrix} -0.35 \\ 0.0540 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{k}_3 = 0.5\, \mathbf{f}\left(0.25, \begin{pmatrix} -0.575 \\ -0.5730 \end{pmatrix}\right)
= \begin{pmatrix} -0.2865 \\ 0.2059 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{k}_4 = 0.5\, \mathbf{f}\left(0.5, \begin{pmatrix} -0.6865 \\ -0.3941 \end{pmatrix}\right)
= \begin{pmatrix} -0.1970 \\ 0.9440 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{w}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -0.4 \\ -0.6 \end{pmatrix}
+ \tfrac{1}{6}\left(
\begin{pmatrix} -0.3 \\ -0.2 \end{pmatrix}
+ 2\begin{pmatrix} -0.35 \\ 0.0540 \end{pmatrix}
+ 2\begin{pmatrix} -0.2865 \\ 0.2059 \end{pmatrix}
+ \begin{pmatrix} -0.1970 \\ 0.9440 \end{pmatrix}
\right)
= \begin{pmatrix} -0.6950 \\ -0.3894 \end{pmatrix}
$$

The first component gives $y(0.5) \approx -0.6950$. The exact value is $-0.6936$.

</Solution>

### Example 2

Write $y''' = y'' - 2y' + y + t$ with $y(0) = 1$, $y'(0) = 0$, $y''(0) = 2$ as a first-order system.

<Solution>

Set $\mathbf{u} = (y, y', y'')^T$.

$$
\mathbf{u}' = \begin{pmatrix}
u_2 \\
u_3 \\
u_3 - 2u_2 + u_1 + t
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf{u}(0) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

</Solution>

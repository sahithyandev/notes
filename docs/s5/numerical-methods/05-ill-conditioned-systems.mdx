A problem is ill-conditioned **iff** a small change in any of its elements causes a large change in its solution. Otherwise it's well-conditioned.

For a matrix, the degree of ill-conditioning is measured by a condition number, defined using the norms of the matrix and its inverse.

<Note>

Round-off error effectively perturbs the entries of $A$ and $\vec{b}$. If the system is ill-conditioned, this produces large errors in the solution. Ill-conditioned systems are inherently difficult to handle in the presence of rounding errors, and the usual row reduction algorithm must be modified when they arise.

</Note>

Examples:

$$
\begin{cases} x + y = 2 \\ x + 1.001y = 2 \end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases} x + y = 2 \\ x + 1.001y = 2.001 \end{cases}
$$

The left system has solution $x = 2$, $y = 0$. The right system, obtained by a $0.001$ change in one constant, has solution $x = 1$, $y = 1$. The coefficient matrix is ill-conditioned, with condition number $4004$.

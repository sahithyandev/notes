A [boundary value problem](/s5/numerical-methods/boundary-value-problem) is nonlinear **iff** its differential equation cannot be written as

<Formula>

$$
y'' = p(x)y' + q(x)y + r(x)
$$

</Formula>

for functions $p$, $q$, and $r$.

## Nonlinear Shooting Method

Choose the initial slope $t=y'(a)$ so that the IVP solution satisfies $y(b)=\beta$. For a trial value $t$, solve

<Formula>

$$
y'' = f(x,y,y'), \quad y(a) = \alpha,\ y'(a) = t
$$

</Formula>

Define $z(x,t) = \dfrac{\partial y(x,t)}{\partial t}$. Solve the sensitivity IVP with the original IVP:

<Formula>

$$
z'' = f_y(x,y,y')z + f_{y'}(x,y,y')z', \quad z(a) = 0,\ z'(a) = 1
$$

</Formula>

Use [Newton's method](/s5/numerical-methods/newtons-method) on $F(t)=y(b,t)-\beta$:

<Formula>

$$
t_k = t_{k-1} - \frac{y(b,t_{k-1}) - \beta}{z(b,t_{k-1})}
$$

</Formula>

- Choose an initial guess $t_0$.
- Solve both IVPs with [order 4 Runge-Kutta](/s5/numerical-methods/systems-of-ivps).
- Update $t$ until $|F(t)|$ is sufficiently small.

<Note>

Reduce both IVPs to first-order systems before applying Runge-Kutta.

</Note>

## Nonlinear Finite-Difference Method

Replace derivatives with centered differences. Solve the resulting nonlinear system with Newton's method.

A unique solution exists **iff**

- $f$, $f_y$, and $f_{y'}$ are continuous for $x \in [a,b]$ and $y, y' \in (-\infty, \infty)$
- $f_y(x,y,y') \geq \delta$ for some $\delta > 0$
- A constant $k$ exists with $k = \max\big(|f_y(x,y,y')|,\ |f_{y'}(x,y,y')|\big)$

For mesh points $x_0, \ldots, x_{N+1}$, let $y(x_i) \approx w_i$. Centered differences give

<Formula>

$$
w_0 = \alpha, \quad w_{N+1} = \beta, \quad -\frac{w_{i+1}-2w_i+w_{i-1}}{h^2} + f\left(x_i,w_i,\frac{w_{i+1}-w_{i-1}}{2h}\right) = 0
$$

</Formula>

for $i = 1, \ldots, N$, with truncation error $O(h^2)$.

The resulting $N$ equations are

<Formula>

$$
2w_1-w_2+h^2f\left(x_1,w_1,\frac{w_2-\alpha}{2h}\right)-\alpha=0
\\[8pt]
-w_{i-1}+2w_i-w_{i+1}+h^2f\left(x_i,w_i,\frac{w_{i+1}-w_{i-1}}{2h}\right)=0, \quad i=2,\ldots,N-1
\\[8pt]
-w_{N-1}+2w_N+h^2f\left(x_N,w_N,\frac{\beta-w_{N-1}}{2h}\right)-\beta=0
$$

</Formula>

Solve with [Newton's method](/s5/numerical-methods/newtons-method). A unique solution exists whenever $h < \dfrac{2}{L}$.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Use the nonlinear shooting method to solve

$$
y''=(y')^2, \quad 0 \leq x \leq 1, \quad y(0)=0, \quad y(1)=1
$$

Start with $t_0=y'(0)=0.5$. Use order 4 Runge-Kutta on the mesh $x=0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1$. Stop when the endpoint residual $|y(1,t_k)-1|$ is less than $10^{-5}$.

<Solution>

For a trial initial slope $t$, set

$$
v=y', \qquad z=\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}, \qquad s=z'
$$

The IVP system for $y$ and its sensitivity $z$ is

$$
y'=v, \qquad v'=v^2, \qquad z'=s, \qquad s'=2vs
$$

with $y(0)=0$, $v(0)=t$, $z(0)=0$, and $s(0)=1$. Use order 4 Runge-Kutta with $h=0.25$ to solve both IVPs numerically.

For each trial slope, define the endpoint residual $F(t)=y(1,t)-1$. The Newton update is

$$
t_{k+1}=t_k-\frac{F(t_k)}{z(1,t_k)}
$$

The first RK4 integration gives

$$
y(1,t_0)\approx0.6930885, \qquad F(t_0)\approx-0.3069115, \qquad z(1,t_0)\approx1.9990825
$$

Thus,

$$
t_1=0.5-\frac{-0.3069115}{1.9990825}\approx0.6535262
$$

Repeating the integrations and Newton updates gives:

| $k$ |       $t_k$ |            $F(t_k)$ |  $z(1,t_k)$ |   $t_{k+1}$ |
| --- | ----------: | ------------------: | ----------: | ----------: |
| $0$ | $0.5000000$ |        $-0.3069115$ | $1.9990825$ | $0.6535262$ |
| $1$ | $0.6535262$ |         $0.0593241$ | $2.8765003$ | $0.6329025$ |
| $2$ | $0.6329025$ |         $0.0016746$ | $2.7170748$ | $0.6322862$ |
| $3$ | $0.6322862$ |  $1.4\times10^{-6}$ | $2.7125768$ | $0.6322857$ |
| $4$ | $0.6322857$ | $9.5\times10^{-13}$ | $2.7125731$ |          -- |

At $t_4$, $|F(t_4)|\approx9.5\times10^{-13}<10^{-5}$. Hence,

$$
y'(0)\approx0.6322857
$$

</Solution>

### Example 2

Use the nonlinear shooting method to solve

$$
y''=y^2, \quad 0 \leq x \leq 1, \quad y(0)=0, \quad y(1)=1
$$

Start with $t_0=y'(0)=0.5$. Use order 4 Runge-Kutta with step size $h=0.25$. Stop when $|y(1,t_k)-1|$ is less than $10^{-5}$.

<Solution>

For a trial initial slope $t$, set $v=y'$, $z=\partial y/\partial t$, and $s=z'$. The IVP system is

$$
y'=v, \qquad v'=y^2, \qquad z'=s, \qquad s'=2yz
$$

with $y(0)=0$, $v(0)=t$, $z(0)=0$, and $s(0)=1$. Define $F(t)=y(1,t)-1$. The Newton update is

$$
t_{k+1}=t_k-\frac{F(t_k)}{z(1,t_k)}
$$

The RK4 integrations and Newton updates are:

| $k$ |       $t_k$ |     $F(t_k)$ |  $z(1,t_k)$ |   $t_{k+1}$ |
| --- | ----------: | -----------: | ----------: | ----------: |
| $0$ | $0.5000000$ | $-0.4786646$ | $1.0863657$ | $0.9406110$ |
| $1$ | $0.9406110$ |  $0.0177426$ | $1.1677594$ | $0.9254173$ |
| $2$ | $0.9254173$ |  $0.0000220$ | $1.1648661$ | $0.9253984$ |
| $3$ | $0.9253984$ |   $<10^{-5}$ | $1.1648625$ |          -- |

At $t_3$, $|F(t_3)|<10^{-5}$. Therefore,

$$
y'(0)=t_3\approx0.9253984
$$

</Solution>

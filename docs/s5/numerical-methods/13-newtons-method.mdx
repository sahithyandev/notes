Newton's method for nonlinear systems generalizes the single-variable iteration $g(x) = x - \phi(x)f(x)$, $\phi(x) = 1/f'(x)$, to $n$ dimensions by replacing $\phi(x)$ with the inverse of a matrix $A(\vec{x})$

$$
G(\vec{x}) = \vec{x} - A(\vec{x})^{-1}F(\vec{x})
$$

Quadratic convergence of this [fixed-point iteration](/s5/numerical-methods/fixed-point-method) to $\vec{p}$ requires $\dfrac{\partial g_i(\vec{p})}{\partial x_k} = 0$ for each $i, k$, which holds by choosing $A(\vec{x})$ to be the [Jacobian matrix](/s5/numerical-methods/jacobian-matrix) $J(\vec{x})$ of $F$.

## Iteration

$$
G(\vec{x}) = \vec{x} - J(\vec{x})^{-1}F(\vec{x})
$$

$$
\vec{x}^{(k)} = \vec{x}^{(k-1)} - J(\vec{x}^{(k-1)})^{-1}F(\vec{x}^{(k-1)}), \quad k \geq 1
$$

<Note>

Inverting $J(\vec{x}^{(k-1)})$ explicitly is avoided in practice. Instead, solve the linear system

$$
J(\vec{x}^{(k-1)})\vec{y}^{(k-1)} = -F(\vec{x}^{(k-1)})
$$

for $\vec{y}^{(k-1)}$, for example by [Gaussian elimination](/s5/numerical-methods/gaussian-elimination), then set $\vec{x}^{(k)} = \vec{x}^{(k-1)} + \vec{y}^{(k-1)}$.

</Note>

Convergence is quadratic, provided a sufficiently accurate starting value $\vec{x}^{(0)}$ is known and $J(\vec{p})^{-1}$ exists.

## Example

Apply Newton's method to the [nonlinear system](/s5/numerical-methods/non-linear-system-of-equations#example) with its [Jacobian](/s5/numerical-methods/jacobian-matrix#example), starting from $\vec{x}^{(0)} = (0.1, 0.1, -0.1)^T$.

$$
F(\vec{x}^{(0)}) \approx (-1.199950, -2.269833, 8.462025)^T
$$

$$
J(\vec{x}^{(0)}) \approx
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & 0.001000 & -0.001000 \\
0.200000 & -32.400000 & 0.995004 \\
-0.099005 & -0.099005 & 20.000000
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Solving $J(\vec{x}^{(0)})\vec{y}^{(0)} = -F(\vec{x}^{(0)})$ gives

$$
\vec{y}^{(0)} \approx (0.399870, -0.080533, -0.421520)^T
$$

$$
\vec{x}^{(1)} = \vec{x}^{(0)} + \vec{y}^{(0)} \approx (0.499870, 0.019467, -0.521520)^T
$$

Repeating converges to $\vec{x}^{(5)} \approx (0.500000, 0.000000, -0.523599)^T$, matching the exact solution $\vec{p} = (0.5, 0, -\pi/6)$.

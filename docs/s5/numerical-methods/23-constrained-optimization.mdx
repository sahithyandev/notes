Constrained optimization minimizes or maximizes $f(x)$ subject to restrictions on the design variables $x$.

## General Form

<Formula>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_x \quad & f(x) \\
\text{subject to} \quad & g_i(x) = c_i, \quad i = 1, \ldots, n \\
& h_j(x) \leq d_j, \quad j = 1, \ldots, m
\end{aligned}
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="g_i(x) = c_i">equality constraints</Sym>
  <Sym s="h_j(x) \leq d_j">inequality constraints</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

<Note>

When $f$ and every constraint are linear, the problem is a linear programming problem.

</Note>

## Linear Programming

Linear programming is constrained optimization where the objective function and every constraint are linear.

### Standard Form

Objective function

<Formula>

$$
z = f(x) = a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + \cdots + a_n x_n
$$

</Formula>

Constraints

<Formula>

$$
a_{i1} x_1 + a_{i2} x_2 + \cdots + a_{in} x_n \leq b_i
$$

</Formula>

The constraint may use $\geq$ in place of $\leq$.

### Graphical Solution

For 2 design variables:

- Plot each constraint as a boundary line.
- Shade the feasible region satisfying every constraint.
- Move the objective-function line $z = \text{constant}$ across the feasible region.
- Evaluate the objective function at each vertex of the feasible region.

The optimum occurs at a vertex of the feasible region.

### Worked Examples

#### Example 1

Minimize $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ subject to $x + y = 2$ and $x \leq 1.5$. Then repeat with $x \geq 1.5$ in place of $x \leq 1.5$.

<Solution>

In the general form, $g(x, y) = x + y$ with $c = 2$.

The equality gives $y = 2 - x$.

$$
f = x^2 + (2 - x)^2 = 2x^2 - 4x + 4
$$

$$
\frac{\text{d}f}{\text{d}x} = 4x - 4 = 0 \implies x = 1, \quad y = 1, \quad f = 2
$$

With $x \leq 1.5$

$x = 1$ satisfies the inequality, so the constraint is inactive.

$$
(x, y) = (1, 1), \quad f_{\min} = 2
$$

With $x \geq 1.5$

The unconstrained optimum $x = 1$ violates the inequality. $f = 2x^2 - 4x + 4$ increases for $x > 1$, so the optimum lies on the boundary and the constraint is active.

$$
x = 1.5, \quad y = 0.5, \quad f_{\min} = 2.25 + 0.25 = 2.5
$$

</Solution>

#### Example 2

A factory produces heaters of types $S$ and $L$, sold wholesale at \$40 and \$88 respectively. Machine $M_1$ needs 2 minutes per $S$ heater and 8 minutes per $L$ heater. Machine $M_2$ needs 5 minutes per $S$ heater and 2 minutes per $L$ heater. Both machines run 60 minutes per hour. Find the hourly production rates $x_1$ ($S$) and $x_2$ ($L$) maximizing hourly revenue.

<Solution>

Objective function

$$
z = f(x) = 40x_1 + 88x_2
$$

Constraints

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x_1 + 8x_2 &\leq 60 \\
5x_1 + 2x_2 &\leq 60 \\
x_1 &\geq 0 \\
x_2 &\geq 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The constraint intersections in the first quadrant are $(0, 0)$, $(12, 0)$, $(0, 7.5)$ and $(10, 5)$.

| Vertex     | Revenue |
| ---------- | ------- |
| $(0, 0)$   | $0$     |
| $(12, 0)$  | $480$   |
| $(0, 7.5)$ | $660$   |
| $(10, 5)$  | $840$   |

The optimum is $(x_1, x_2) = (10, 5)$.

$$
z_{\max} = 40(10) + 88(5) = 840
$$

</Solution>

#### Example 3

Maximize $z = 3x_1 + 2x_2$ subject to $x_1 + x_2 \leq 4$, $x_1 \leq 3$, $x_1, x_2 \geq 0$.

<Solution>

The feasible region has vertices $(0, 0)$, $(3, 0)$, $(3, 1)$ and $(0, 4)$.

| Vertex   | $z = 3x_1 + 2x_2$ |
| -------- | ----------------- |
| $(0, 0)$ | $0$               |
| $(3, 0)$ | $9$               |
| $(3, 1)$ | $11$              |
| $(0, 4)$ | $8$               |

The maximum is $z = 11$ at $(3, 1)$.

</Solution>

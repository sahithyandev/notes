3 operations on the equations of a linear system are called elementary operations:

- Interchange  
  Swap the $i$-th and $j$-th equations.
- Scale  
  Multiply the $k$-th equation by $c \neq 0$.
- Replace  
  Replace the $k$-th equation by itself plus $c$ times the $j$-th equation.

Each has an inverse operation: interchange is its own inverse, scaling by $c$ is undone by dividing by $c$, and replacing $R_k$ by $R_k + cR_j$ is undone by replacing $R_k$ by $R_k - cR_j$.

2 linear systems are equivalent **iff** one is obtained from the other by a finite number of elementary operations.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Show a single elementary operation preserves the solution set (case by case on the 3 operation types)
- Extend to a finite chain of elementary operations by induction

</Note>

Two equivalent systems have the same set of solutions.

## Elementary row operations

Applying elementary operations to the augmented matrix $[A \mid \vec{b}]$ instead of the equations gives elementary row operations:

- Interchange, $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$
- Scale, $cR_k \to R_k$ for $c \neq 0$
- Replace, $R_k + cR_j \to R_k$

<Note>

Interchanging rows to place a useful entry in the pivot position is called **pivoting**. The pivot element of a column is the entry used to eliminate the other entries in that column.

</Note>

2 matrices are row equivalent **iff** one is obtained from the other by a finite number of elementary row operations.

Examples:

- $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 6 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$ are row equivalent.

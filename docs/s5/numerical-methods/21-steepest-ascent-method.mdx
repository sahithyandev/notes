The steepest ascent method finds a local maximum of $f(x, y)$ by repeatedly moving in the direction of the gradient.

## Gradient

The gradient of $f(x,y)$ is

$$
\nabla f(x, y) = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\vec{i} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\vec{j}
$$

$f(x,y)$ increases fastest in the direction of $\nabla f$ and decreases fastest in the opposite direction.

## Procedure

Given a starting point $(x_0, y_0)$

- Evaluate the gradient $\nabla f(x_i, y_i)$
- Search along the gradient direction for the $h_i$ that maximizes

$$
g(h_i) = f\left(x_i + h_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x_i,y_i),\ y_i + h_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x_i,y_i)\right)
$$

- Set $g'(h_i) = 0$ and solve for $h_i$
- Update

$$
x_{i+1} = x_i + h_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x_i,y_i), \quad y_{i+1} = y_i + h_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x_i,y_i)
$$

- Repeat until convergence

<Note>

Finding the minimum of $f(x,y)$ follows the same steps applied to $-f(x,y)$. This variant is the steepest descent method.

</Note>

## Worked Example

Maximize

$$
f(x,y) = 2xy + 2x - x^2 - 2y^2
$$

with initial guess $x_0 = -1$, $y_0 = 1$. The partial derivatives are

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2y + 2 - 2x, \quad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 2x - 4y
$$

Iteration 1

$$
\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|_{(-1,1)} = 6, \quad \left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right|_{(-1,1)} = -6
$$

$$
g(h_0) = f(-1 + 6h_0,\ 1 - 6h_0) = -180h_0^2 + 72h_0 - 7
$$

$$
g'(h_0) = -360h_0 + 72 = 0 \implies h_0 = 0.2
$$

$$
x_1 = -1 + 6(0.2) = 0.2, \quad y_1 = 1 - 6(0.2) = -0.2
$$

Iteration 2

$$
\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|_{(0.2,-0.2)} = 1.2, \quad \left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right|_{(0.2,-0.2)} = 1.2
$$

$$
g(h_1) = f(0.2 + 1.2h_1,\ -0.2 + 1.2h_1) = -1.44h_1^2 + 2.88h_1 + 0.2
$$

$$
g'(h_1) = -2.88h_1 + 2.88 = 0 \implies h_1 = 1
$$

$$
x_2 = 0.2 + 1.2(1) = 1.4, \quad y_2 = -0.2 + 1.2(1) = 1
$$

Iteration 3

$$
\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|_{(1.4,1)} = 1.2, \quad \left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right|_{(1.4,1)} = -1.2
$$

$$
g(h_2) = f(1.4 + 1.2h_2,\ 1 - 1.2h_2) = -7.2h_2^2 + 2.88h_2 + 1.64
$$

$$
g'(h_2) = -14.4h_2 + 2.88 = 0 \implies h_2 = 0.2
$$

$$
x_3 = 1.4 + 1.2(0.2) = 1.64, \quad y_3 = 1 - 1.2(0.2) = 0.76
$$

The iterations continue toward the maximum at $(2, 1)$, where $f(2,1) = 2$.

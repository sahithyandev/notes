A boundary value problem (BVP) is a differential equation with conditions specified at 2 distinct points, called boundary conditions.

### Standard Form

$$
y'' = f(x, y, y'), \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad y(a) = \alpha,\ y(b) = \beta
$$

### Linear and Nonlinear BVPs

The BVP is linear if $f$ has the form

$$
f(x,y,y') = p(x)y' + q(x)y + r(x)
$$

for some functions $p$, $q$, $r$, giving

$$
y'' = p(x)y' + q(x)y + r(x), \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad y(a) = \alpha,\ y(b) = \beta
$$

Otherwise, the BVP is nonlinear.

## Existence and Uniqueness

The BVP has a unique solution on $[a,b]$ **iff**

- $f$, $f_y$, and $f_{y'}$ are continuous on $D = \{(x,y,y') \mid a \leq x \leq b,\ y, y' \in \mathbb{R}\}$
- $f_y(x,y,y') > 0$ for all $(x,y,y') \in D$
- a constant $M > 0$ exists with $|f_{y'}(x,y,y')| \leq M$ for all $(x,y,y') \in D$

Examples:

- $y'' + e^{-xy} + \sin(y') = 0$, $0 \leq x \leq 1$, $y(0) = y(1) = 0$ has a unique solution, since $f_y = xe^{-xy} > 0$ for all $x,y$ and $|f_{y'}| = |\cos(y')| \leq 1$ for all $y'$.

A linear BVP is solved by the [linear shooting method](#linear-shooting-method) or a [linear finite-difference method](#linear-finite-difference-method). A nonlinear BVP is solved by the [nonlinear shooting method](#nonlinear-shooting-method) or a [nonlinear finite-difference method](#nonlinear-finite-difference-method).

## Linear Shooting Method

The linear shooting method solves a [linear BVP](#linear-and-nonlinear-bvps) by combining solutions to 2 initial value problems.

Applying the [general existence and uniqueness theorem](#existence-and-uniqueness) to $f(x,y,y') = p(x)y' + q(x)y + r(x)$ gives $f_y = q(x)$ and $f_{y'} = p(x)$, so a unique solution exists **iff**

- $p$, $q$, $r$ are continuous on $[a,b]$
- $q(x) > 0$ for $x \in [a,b]$

### Superposition Idea

The ODE is linear, so if $y_1$ solves

$$
y_1'' = p(x)y_1' + q(x)y_1 + r(x)
$$

and $y_2$ solves the homogeneous equation

$$
y_2'' = p(x)y_2' + q(x)y_2
$$

then $y = y_1 + c\,y_2$ solves the same ODE for any constant $c$.

### Auxiliary IVPs

Fix

$$
y_1(a) = \alpha,\ y_1'(a) = 0, \qquad y_2(a) = 0,\ y_2'(a) = 1
$$

so that $y(a) = y_1(a) + c\,y_2(a) = \alpha$ for any $c$. Solve

$$
y_1'' = p(x)y_1' + q(x)y_1 + r(x), \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad y_1(a) = \alpha,\ y_1'(a) = 0
$$

$$
y_2'' = p(x)y_2' + q(x)y_2, \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad y_2(a) = 0,\ y_2'(a) = 1
$$

by reducing each to a first-order system and applying [order 4 Runge-Kutta](/s5/numerical-methods/systems-of-ivps). $q(x) > 0$ guarantees $y_2(b) \neq 0$ whenever $y_2$ is non-constant.

### Combining Solutions

$c$ is chosen so that $y(b) = \beta$

$$
y_1(b) + c\,y_2(b) = \beta \implies c = \frac{\beta - y_1(b)}{y_2(b)}
$$

$$
y(x) = y_1(x) + \frac{\beta - y_1(b)}{y_2(b)}\, y_2(x)
$$

is the unique solution to the BVP.

## Nonlinear Shooting Method

The nonlinear shooting method solves a nonlinear BVP by approximating the initial slope $y'(a)$ that makes an initial value problem's solution hit the far boundary condition.

### Idea

For a guess $t$, let $y(x,t)$ solve the IVP

$$
y'' = f(x,y,y'), \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad y(a) = \alpha,\ y'(a) = t
$$

The method finds $t$ such that $y(b,t) - \beta = 0$, using [Newton's method](/s5/numerical-methods/newtons-method).

### Newton Iteration

$$
t_k = t_{k-1} - \frac{y(b,t_{k-1}) - \beta}{y'(b,t_{k-1})}
$$

$y'(b,t_{k-1})$ can't be obtained analytically since $y(b,t_{k-1})$ is only a point approximation. Define $z(x,t) = \dfrac{\partial y(x,t)}{\partial t}$. Differentiating the IVP with respect to $t$ gives

$$
z'' = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y,y')\,z + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y'}(x,y,y')\,z', \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad z(a) = 1,\ z'(a) = 0
$$

Substituting $z(b,t_{k-1})$ for $y'(b,t_{k-1})$

$$
t_k = t_{k-1} - \frac{y(b,t_{k-1}) - \beta}{z(b,t_{k-1})}
$$

### Procedure

- Choose an initial guess $t_0$
- Repeat for $k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ until $|y(b,t_k) - \beta|$ is sufficiently small
  - Approximate $y_k(x) = y(x,t_k)$ from

    $$
    y'' = f(x,y,y'), \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad y(a) = \alpha,\ y'(a) = t_k
    $$

  - Approximate $z_k(x) = z(x,t_k)$ from

    $$
    z'' = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y_k,y_k')\,z + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y'}(x,y_k,y_k')\,z', \quad a \leq x \leq b, \quad z(a) = 1,\ z'(a) = 0
    $$

  - Update

    $$
    t_{k+1} = t_k - \frac{y_k(b) - \beta}{z_k(b)}
    $$

<Note>

Both IVPs are reduced to first-order systems and solved with [order 4 Runge-Kutta](/s5/numerical-methods/systems-of-ivps).

</Note>

## Linear Finite-Difference Method

The finite-difference method approximates the solution of a linear BVP by replacing derivatives with centered difference formulas at mesh points and solving the resulting linear system.

### Mesh Points

Partition $[a,b]$ into $N+1$ subintervals of length $h = \dfrac{b-a}{N+1}$. Mesh points are

$$
x_i = a + ih, \quad i = 0, 1, \ldots, N+1
$$

### Centered Difference Formulas

$$
y''(x_i) = \frac{1}{h^2}\big(y(x_{i+1}) - 2y(x_i) + y(x_{i-1})\big) - \frac{h^2}{12}y^{(4)}(\xi_i)
$$

$$
y'(x_i) = \frac{1}{2h}\big(y(x_{i+1}) - y(x_{i-1})\big) - \frac{h^2}{6}y'''(\eta_i)
$$

for some $\xi_i, \eta_i \in (x_{i-1}, x_{i+1})$.

### Linear Difference Equation

Substituting the centered difference formulas into the linear BVP and dropping the $O(h^2)$ error term, $y(x_i) \approx w_i$ satisfies

$$
w_0 = \alpha, \quad w_{N+1} = \beta
$$

$$
-\left(1 + \frac{h}{2}p(x_i)\right)w_{i-1} + \big(2 + h^2 q(x_i)\big)w_i - \left(1 - \frac{h}{2}p(x_i)\right)w_{i+1} = -h^2 r(x_i)
$$

for $i = 1, \ldots, N$, with truncation error $O(h^2)$.

### Matrix Form

$$
Aw = b
$$

with $A$ tridiagonal, using

$$
a_i = -1 - \frac{h}{2}p(x_i), \quad d_i = 2 + h^2 q(x_i), \quad c_i = -1 + \frac{h}{2}p(x_i)
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
d_1 & c_1 & & \\
a_2 & d_2 & c_2 & \\
& \ddots & \ddots & \ddots \\
& & a_N & d_N
\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
w = \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \\ \vdots \\ w_{N-1} \\ w_N \end{pmatrix}, \quad
b = \begin{pmatrix}
-h^2r(x_1) - a_1\alpha \\
-h^2r(x_2) \\
\vdots \\
-h^2r(x_{N-1}) \\
-h^2r(x_N) - c_N\beta
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Solve for $w$ with [LU decomposition](/s5/numerical-methods/lu-decomposition) or another linear solver.

If $p$, $q$, $r$ are continuous on $[a,b]$ and $q(x) \geq 0$ on $[a,b]$, the linear system has a unique solution whenever $h < \dfrac{2}{L}$, where $L = \max_{a \leq x \leq b} |p(x)|$.

## Nonlinear Finite-Difference Method

The finite-difference method for a nonlinear BVP replaces derivatives with centered difference formulas and solves the resulting nonlinear system with Newton's method.

A unique solution exists **iff**

- $f$, $f_y$, $f_{y'}$ are continuous for $x \in [a,b]$ and $y, y' \in (-\infty, \infty)$
- $f_y(x,y,y') \geq \delta$ for some $\delta > 0$
- a constant $k$ exists with $k = \max\big(|f_y(x,y,y')|,\ |f_{y'}(x,y,y')|\big)$

### Nonlinear Difference Equation

Using the [centered difference formulas](#centered-difference-formulas) at mesh points $x_0, \ldots, x_{N+1}$ on $[a,b]$, $y(x_i) \approx w_i$ satisfies

$$
w_0 = \alpha, \quad w_{N+1} = \beta
$$

$$
-\frac{w_{i+1} - 2w_i + w_{i-1}}{h^2} + f\left(x_i, w_i, \frac{w_{i+1}-w_{i-1}}{2h}\right) = 0
$$

for $i = 1, \ldots, N$, with truncation error $O(h^2)$.

### Nonlinear System

$$
\begin{aligned}
2w_1 - w_2 + h^2 f\left(x_1, w_1, \frac{w_2 - \alpha}{2h}\right) - \alpha &= 0 \\
-w_1 + 2w_2 - w_3 + h^2 f\left(x_2, w_2, \frac{w_3 - w_1}{2h}\right) &= 0 \\
&\ \ \vdots \\
-w_{N-2} + 2w_{N-1} - w_N + h^2 f\left(x_{N-1}, w_{N-1}, \frac{w_N - w_{N-2}}{2h}\right) &= 0 \\
-w_{N-1} + 2w_N + h^2 f\left(x_N, w_N, \frac{\beta - w_{N-1}}{2h}\right) - \beta &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

This is a system of $N$ nonlinear equations in $N$ unknowns, with a unique solution whenever $h < \dfrac{2}{L}$. Solve with [Newton's method](/s5/numerical-methods/newtons-method).

Gaussian elimination reduces $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ to [echelon form](/s1/mathematics/matrices/echelon-form) by [row operations](/s5/numerical-methods/linear-system-of-equations#equivalent-systems), then solves it by back substitution.

## Forward Elimination

The pivot element of a column is the entry used to eliminate the other entries in that column.

Provided $a_{11} \neq 0$, eliminate $x_1$ from equations $2$ through $n$ using

$$
R_j - \frac{a_{j1}}{a_{11}} R_1 \to R_j, \quad j = 2, 3, \ldots, n
$$

More generally, eliminate $x_i$ below row $i$, for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$, using

$$
R_j - \frac{a_{ji}^{(i)}}{a_{ii}^{(i)}} R_i \to R_j, \quad j = i+1, i+2, \ldots, n
$$

provided $a_{ii}^{(i)} \neq 0$. This reduces $[A \mid \vec{b}]$ to upper triangular form

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
a_{11} & a_{12} & \cdots & a_{1n} & b_1^{(1)} \\
0 & a_{22}^{(1)} & \cdots & a_{2n} & b_2^{(2)} \\
\vdots & & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \cdots & a_{nn}^{(n-1)} & b_n^{(n-1)}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

## Back Substitution

Once $[A \mid \vec{b}]$ is upper triangular, solve from the last row upward. The last row has only $x_n$

$$
x_n = \frac{b_n^{(n-1)}}{a_{nn}^{(n-1)}}
$$

Then substitute the solved unknowns into each row above

$$
x_i = \frac{b_i^{(i-1)} - \sum\limits_{j=i+1}^{n} a_{ij}^{(i-1)}x_j}{a_{ii}^{(i-1)}}, \quad i = n-1, n-2, \ldots, 1
$$

## Pivoting

Pivoting is interchanging rows or columns to place a larger entry in the pivot position. The system stays equivalent.

### Partial Pivoting

At stage $i$, find the row $p \geq i$ maximizing $|a_{ji}^{(i)}|$ for $j \geq i$, then swap $R_i \leftrightarrow R_p$ before eliminating. This keeps every multiplier at or below $1$ in magnitude.

### Scaled Partial Pivoting

Partial pivoting can pick a poor pivot when an entry is large only because its row is large. Scaled partial pivoting compares entries relative to their row. Before elimination begins, compute the scale factor of each row

$$
s_j = \max_{1 \leq k \leq n} |a_{jk}|
$$

At stage $i$, pick the first row $p \geq i$ maximizing $\frac{|a_{ji}^{(i)}|}{s_j}$ and swap $R_i \leftrightarrow R_p$. The scale factors $s_j$ are computed once and carried along with their rows during swaps.

<Note>

If $s_j = 0$, row $j$ is all $0$ and the system has no unique solution.

</Note>

### Complete Pivoting

At stage $i$, search the entire remaining submatrix for the entry maximizing $|a_{jk}^{(i)}|$ over $j \geq i$ and $k \geq i$. Swap its row into row $i$ and its column into column $i$ before eliminating.

<Note>

Swapping columns reorders the unknowns $x_i$, so the column permutation must be tracked and undone after back substitution. Complete pivoting costs $O(n^3)$ comparisons and is rarely needed when partial pivoting suffices.

</Note>

## Pitfalls

### Zero Pivot

If $a_{ii}^{(i)} = 0$, the multiplier divides by zero even when the system has a unique solution. Swap in a row below with a non-zero entry in that column.

### Small Pivot

A small pivot inflates the multipliers $\frac{a_{ji}^{(i)}}{a_{ii}^{(i)}}$, which amplifies rounding error through every later stage.

### Ill Conditioning

A problem is ill-conditioned **iff** a small change in any of its elements causes a large change in its solution. Otherwise the problem is well-conditioned.

Round-off errors slightly change $A$ and $\vec{b}$. In an ill-conditioned system this gives large errors in the solution, and pivoting cannot prevent it.

Consider

$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1.0001 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}2\\2.0001\end{pmatrix}
$$

with solution $x = 1, y = 1$. Changing $b_2$ from $2.0001$ to $2.0002$, a change of $0.0001$, gives

$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1.0001 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}2\\2.0002\end{pmatrix}
$$

with solution $x = 0, y = 2$. A $0.0001$ change in $b_2$ moved $x$ and $y$ by $1$ each. The coefficient matrix is nearly singular, $\det = 0.0001$.

#### Condition Number

A measure of the degree of ill-conditioning. Denoted as $\kappa(A)$. Defined as:

$$
\kappa(A) = \|A\| \, \|A^{-1}\|
$$

A large $\kappa(A)$ means $A$ is close to singular.

$$
\begin{cases} x + y = 2 \\ x + 1.001y = 2 \end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases} x + y = 2 \\ x + 1.001y = 2.001 \end{cases}
$$

The left system has solution $x = 2$, $y = 0$. The right system, obtained by a $0.001$ change in one constant, has solution $x = 1$, $y = 1$. The coefficient matrix is ill-conditioned, with condition number $4004$.

### Cost

| Method              | Time     | Space    |
| ------------------- | -------- | -------- |
| Forward elimination | $O(n^3)$ | $O(n^2)$ |
| Back substitution   | $O(n^2)$ | $O(n^2)$ |

Space is for the augmented matrix $[A \mid \vec{b}]$, since elimination is done in place. The $O(n^3)$ elimination is repeated for every new $\vec{b}$, which LU decomposition avoids.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Solve using Gaussian elimination without pivoting.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x_1 + x_2 - x_3 &= 8 \\
-3x_1 - x_2 + 2x_3 &= -11 \\
-2x_1 + x_2 + 2x_3 &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

<Solution>

Augmented matrix

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 1 & -1 & 8 \\ -3 & -1 & 2 & -11 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 & -3 \end{array}\right]
$$

Step 1, pivot $a_{11} = 2$

$$
m_{21} = \frac{-3}{2} = -1.5, \quad m_{31} = \frac{-2}{2} = -1
$$

$$
\xrightarrow[R_3 + R_1]{R_2 + 1.5R_1}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 1 & -1 & 8 \\ 0 & 0.5 & 0.5 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 & 5 \end{array}\right]
$$

Step 2, pivot $a_{22}^{(1)} = 0.5$

$$
m_{32} = \frac{2}{0.5} = 4
$$

$$
\xrightarrow{R_3 - 4R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 1 & -1 & 8 \\ 0 & 0.5 & 0.5 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 & 1 \end{array}\right]
$$

Back substitution

$$
x_3 = \frac{1}{-1} = -1
$$

$$
x_2 = \frac{1 - 0.5(-1)}{0.5} = 3
$$

$$
x_1 = \frac{8 - 1(3) - (-1)(-1)}{2} = 2
$$

$$
\vec{x} = (2, 3, -1)^T
$$

</Solution>

### Example 2

Solve using Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x_2 + x_3 &= 9 \\
2x_1 + 2x_2 - x_3 &= 3 \\
4x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3 &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$

<Solution>

Augmented matrix

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 0 & 3 & 1 & 9 \\ 2 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 4 & 2 & 1 & 11 \end{array}\right]
$$

Step 1, column 1 has magnitudes $|0|, |2|, |4|$. The largest is $|a_{31}| = 4$ in $R_3$, so swap $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_3$.

$$
\xrightarrow{R_1 \leftrightarrow R_3}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 2 & 1 & 11 \\ 2 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 9 \end{array}\right]
$$

$$
m_{21} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.5, \quad m_{31} = 0
$$

$$
\xrightarrow{R_2 - 0.5R_1}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 2 & 1 & 11 \\ 0 & 1 & -1.5 & -2.5 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 9 \end{array}\right]
$$

Step 2, column 2 below row 1 has magnitudes $|1|$ and $|3|$. The largest is $|a_{32}^{(1)}| = 3$ in $R_3$, so swap $R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3$.

$$
\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 2 & 1 & 11 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 9 \\ 0 & 1 & -1.5 & -2.5 \end{array}\right]
$$

$$
m_{32} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

$$
\xrightarrow{R_3 - \frac{1}{3}R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 2 & 1 & 11 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & -\frac{11}{6} & -\frac{11}{2} \end{array}\right]
$$

Back substitution

$$
x_3 = \frac{-11/2}{-11/6} = 3
$$

$$
x_2 = \frac{9 - 1(3)}{3} = 2
$$

$$
x_1 = \frac{11 - 2(2) - 1(3)}{4} = 1
$$

$$
\vec{x} = (1, 2, 3)^T
$$

</Solution>

### Example 3

Solve using Gaussian elimination with scaled partial pivoting.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x_1 + 100x_2 - 50x_3 &= 54 \\
x_1 + x_2 + 2x_3 &= 4 \\
2x_1 - x_3 &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<Solution>

Augmented matrix and row scale factors

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 100 & -50 & 54 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 0 & -1 & 1 \end{array}\right]
$$

$$
s_1 = \max\{4, 100, 50\} = 100, \quad s_2 = \max\{1, 1, 2\} = 2, \quad s_3 = \max\{2, 0, 1\} = 2
$$

Step 1, compare the scaled magnitudes in column 1.

$$
\frac{|a_{11}|}{s_1} = \frac{4}{100} = 0.04, \quad \frac{|a_{21}|}{s_2} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5, \quad \frac{|a_{31}|}{s_3} = \frac{2}{2} = 1
$$

Partial pivoting would keep $R_1$ since $|4|$ is largest in column 1, but scaled partial pivoting picks $R_3$ with ratio $1$ and swaps $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_3$. The scale factors move with their rows to become $(s_3, s_2, s_1) = (2, 2, 100)$.

$$
\xrightarrow{R_1 \leftrightarrow R_3}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 4 & 100 & -50 & 54 \end{array}\right]
$$

$$
m_{21} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5, \quad m_{31} = \frac{4}{2} = 2
$$

$$
\xrightarrow[R_3 - 2R_1]{R_2 - 0.5R_1}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2.5 & 3.5 \\ 0 & 100 & -48 & 52 \end{array}\right]
$$

Step 2, compare the remaining rows in column 2 using their original scale factors $s_2 = 2$ and $s_1 = 100$.

$$
\frac{|a_{22}^{(1)}|}{s_2} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5, \quad \frac{|a_{32}^{(1)}|}{s_1} = \frac{100}{100} = 1
$$

Swap $R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3$.

$$
\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 100 & -48 & 52 \\ 0 & 1 & 2.5 & 3.5 \end{array}\right]
$$

$$
m_{32} = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01
$$

$$
\xrightarrow{R_3 - 0.01R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 100 & -48 & 52 \\ 0 & 0 & 2.98 & 2.98 \end{array}\right]
$$

Back substitution

$$
x_3 = \frac{2.98}{2.98} = 1
$$

$$
x_2 = \frac{52 - (-48)(1)}{100} = 1
$$

$$
x_1 = \frac{1 - (-1)(1)}{2} = 1
$$

$$
\vec{x} = (1, 1, 1)^T
$$

</Solution>

### Example 4

Solve using Gaussian elimination with complete pivoting.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x_1 - x_2 + x_3 &= 3 \\
x_1 + x_2 + 4x_3 &= 15 \\
x_1 + 2x_2 - 2x_3 &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

<Solution>

Augmented matrix with initial variable order $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & -1 & 1 & 3 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 & 15 \\ 1 & 2 & -2 & -1 \end{array}\right]
$$

Step 1, the largest entry in magnitude across the entire coefficient matrix is $|a_{23}| = 4$ at row 2, column 3. Swap $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$ and $C_1 \leftrightarrow C_3$. The variable order becomes $(x_3, x_2, x_1)$.

$$
\xrightarrow{R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2,\; C_1 \leftrightarrow C_3}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 1 & 1 & 15 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ -2 & 2 & 1 & -1 \end{array}\right]
$$

$$
m_{21} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25, \quad m_{31} = \frac{-2}{4} = -0.5
$$

$$
\xrightarrow[R_3 + 0.5R_1]{R_2 - 0.25R_1}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 1 & 1 & 15 \\ 0 & -1.25 & 1.75 & -0.75 \\ 0 & 2.5 & 1.5 & 6.5 \end{array}\right]
$$

Step 2, in the remaining $2 \times 2$ submatrix, the largest entry in magnitude is $|2.5|$ at row 3, column 2. Swap $R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3$. The variable order stays $(x_3, x_2, x_1)$.

$$
\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 1 & 1 & 15 \\ 0 & 2.5 & 1.5 & 6.5 \\ 0 & -1.25 & 1.75 & -0.75 \end{array}\right]
$$

$$
m_{32} = \frac{-1.25}{2.5} = -0.5
$$

$$
\xrightarrow{R_3 + 0.5R_2}
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 4 & 1 & 1 & 15 \\ 0 & 2.5 & 1.5 & 6.5 \\ 0 & 0 & 2.5 & 2.5 \end{array}\right]
$$

Back substitution in the permuted order $(x_3, x_2, x_1)$

$$
x_1 = \frac{2.5}{2.5} = 1
$$

$$
x_2 = \frac{6.5 - 1.5(1)}{2.5} = 2
$$

$$
x_3 = \frac{15 - 1(2) - 1(1)}{4} = 3
$$

Restoring the original variable order gives $\vec{x} = (1, 2, 3)^T$.

</Solution>

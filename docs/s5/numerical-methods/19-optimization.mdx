Optimization is the process of finding the input to a function that minimizes or maximizes it.

## Objective Function

The quantity being optimized, $f(x)$, is the objective function or merit function. The quantities free to adjust, $x$, are the design variables.

## Turning Points

At a turning point, $f'(x) = 0$. The second derivative $f''(x)$ determines the type

- Maximum\
  $f''(x) < 0$.
- Minimum\
  $f''(x) > 0$.

## One-Dimensional Optimization

One-dimensional optimization finds $x$ such that $f'(x) = 0$.

## Constrained and Unconstrained Optimization

- Unconstrained optimization\
  No restrictions are placed on the design variables. See steepest ascent method.
- Constrained optimization\
  Restrictions are placed on the design variables. See constrained optimization.

## Local and Global Optima

A function is unimodal **iff** it has a single optimum. A function with more than 1 local optimum is multimodal.

<Note>

The global optimum must not be mistaken for a local optimum found during a search.

</Note>

## Worked Example

Find and classify the turning points of $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1$.

<Solution>

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3)
$$

$f'(x) = 0$ at $x = 1$ and $x = 3$.

$$
f''(x) = 6x - 12
$$

At $x = 1$

$$
f''(1) = -6 < 0, \quad f(1) = 5
$$

A local maximum.

At $x = 3$

$$
f''(3) = 6 > 0, \quad f(3) = 1
$$

A local minimum.

$f$ has 2 local optima, so it's multimodal. It has no global optimum on $\mathbb{R}$, since $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$.

</Solution>

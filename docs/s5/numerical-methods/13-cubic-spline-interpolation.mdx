A cubic spline uses 1 cubic on each interval between consecutive nodes.

For $x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n$, write

<Formula>

$$
S_j(x)=a_j+b_j(x-x_j)+c_j(x-x_j)^2+d_j(x-x_j)^3,
\qquad x\in[x_j,x_{j+1}].
$$

</Formula>

At every interior node, a spline preserves 3 quantities.

- Value  
  $S_j(x_{j+1})=S_{j+1}(x_{j+1})$.
- Slope  
  $S_j'(x_{j+1})=S_{j+1}'(x_{j+1})$.
- Curvature  
  $S_j''(x_{j+1})=S_{j+1}''(x_{j+1})$.

It also passes through every data point.

<Formula>

$$
S_j(x_j)=f(x_j),
\qquad
S_j(x_{j+1})=f(x_{j+1}).
$$

</Formula>

Remember a spline as value, slope, curvature. The 2 endpoint conditions complete the construction.

## Boundary Conditions

- Natural spline  
  $S''(x_0)=S''(x_n)=0$. The curve has zero curvature at both ends.
- Clamped spline  
  $S'(x_0)=f'(x_0)$ and $S'(x_n)=f'(x_n)$. The curve has prescribed endpoint slopes.

## Construction

Define

<Formula>

$$
h_j=x_{j+1}-x_j,
\qquad
a_j=f(x_j),
\qquad
\delta_j=\frac{a_{j+1}-a_j}{h_j}.
$$

</Formula>

The unknowns are $c_0,\ldots,c_n$. For each interior node,

<Formula>

$$
h_{j-1}c_{j-1}+2(h_{j-1}+h_j)c_j+h_jc_{j+1}
=3(\delta_j-\delta_{j-1}),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1.
$$

</Formula>

Then compute

<Formula>

$$
b_j=\delta_j-\frac{h_j}{3}(2c_j+c_{j+1}),
\qquad
d_j=\frac{c_{j+1}-c_j}{3h_j}.
$$

</Formula>

The recipe is:

1. Compute $h_j$, $a_j$ and $\delta_j$.
2. Solve for the $c_j$ values with the interior equations and 2 boundary conditions.
3. Compute $b_j$ and $d_j$.
4. Substitute $a_j$, $b_j$, $c_j$ and $d_j$ into $S_j(x)$.

For a natural spline, use

<Formula>

$$
c_0=c_n=0.
$$

</Formula>

For a clamped spline, replace those 2 equations with

<Formula>

$$
2h_0c_0+h_0c_1=3\bigl(\delta_0-f'(x_0)\bigr),
$$

</Formula>

$$
h_{n-1}c_{n-1}+2h_{n-1}c_n=3\bigl(f'(x_n)-\delta_{n-1}\bigr).
$$

## Worked Example

Construct the natural cubic spline through $(1,2)$, $(2,3)$ and $(3,5)$.

<Solution>

$$
h_0=h_1=1,
\qquad
a_0=2,
a_1=3,
a_2=5,
\qquad
c_0=c_2=0.
$$

The only interior equation is

$$
4c_1=3\bigl((5-3)-(3-2)\bigr)=3.
$$

Therefore

$$
c_1=\frac{3}{4}.
$$

For $[1,2]$,

$$
b_0=1-\frac{1}{3}\left(0+\frac{3}{4}\right)=\frac{3}{4},
\qquad
d_0=\frac{3/4-0}{3}=\frac{1}{4}.
$$

For $[2,3]$,

$$
b_1=2-\frac{1}{3}\left(2\cdot\frac{3}{4}+0\right)=\frac{3}{2},
\qquad
d_1=\frac{0-3/4}{3}=-\frac{1}{4}.
$$

$$
S(x)=
\begin{cases}
2+\frac{3}{4}(x-1)+\frac{1}{4}(x-1)^3, & 1\le x\le2,\\[4pt]
3+\frac{3}{2}(x-2)+\frac{3}{4}(x-2)^2-\frac{1}{4}(x-2)^3, & 2\le x\le3.
\end{cases}
$$

</Solution>

## Error

For $f\in C^4[a,b]$, let

<Formula>

$$
M=\max_{a\le x\le b}|f^{(4)}(x)|.
$$

</Formula>

The clamped cubic spline $S$ satisfies

<Formula>

$$
|f(x)-S(x)|\le\frac{5M}{384}
\max_{0\le j\le n-1}(x_{j+1}-x_j)^4.
$$

</Formula>

Smaller intervals reduce the error at the 4th-power rate.

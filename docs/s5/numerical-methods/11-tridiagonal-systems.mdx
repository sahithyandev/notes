A tridiagonal matrix is a square matrix with non-zero elements only on the main diagonal and the diagonals directly above and below it:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
f_1 & g_1 & & & \\
e_2 & f_2 & g_2 & & \\
& e_3 & f_3 & \ddots & \\
& & \ddots & \ddots & g_{n-1} \\
& & & e_n & f_n
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix} r_1 \\ r_2 \\ r_3 \\ \vdots \\ r_n \end{pmatrix}
$$

Crout factorization computes $L$ and $U$, both tridiagonal, whenever $l_{ii} \neq 0$ for each $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n$:

$$
L = \begin{pmatrix}
l_{11} & & & & \\
l_{21} & l_{22} & & & \\
& l_{32} & l_{33} & & \\
& & \ddots & \ddots & \\
& & & l_{n,n-1} & l_{nn}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & u_{12} & & & \\
& 1 & u_{23} & & \\
& & \ddots & \ddots & \\
& & & 1 & u_{n-1,n} \\
& & & & 1
\end{pmatrix}
$$

- $l_{11} = a_{11}$, $u_{12} = \dfrac{a_{12}}{l_{11}}$
- For $i = 2, 3, \ldots, n-1$:
  - $l_{i,i-1} = a_{i,i-1}$
  - $l_{ii} = a_{ii} - l_{i,i-1}u_{i-1,i}$
  - $u_{i,i+1} = \dfrac{a_{i,i+1}}{l_{ii}}$
- $l_{n,n-1} = a_{n,n-1}$, $l_{nn} = a_{nn} - l_{n,n-1}u_{n-1,n}$

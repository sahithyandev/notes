Gauss elimination solves $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ by reducing the augmented matrix $[A \mid \vec{b}]$ to upper triangular form using [elementary row operations](/s5/numerical-methods/elementary-row-operations), then solving by back substitution.

## Forward elimination

Provided $a_{11} \neq 0$, eliminate $x_1$ from equations $2$ through $n$ using

$$
R_j - \frac{a_{j1}}{a_{11}} R_1 \to R_j, \quad j = 2, 3, \ldots, n
$$

More generally, eliminate $x_i$ below row $i$, for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$, using

$$
R_j - \frac{a_{ji}^{(i)}}{a_{ii}^{(i)}} R_i \to R_j, \quad j = i+1, i+2, \ldots, n
$$

provided $a_{ii}^{(i)} \neq 0$. This reduces $[A \mid \vec{b}]$ to upper triangular form

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
a_{11} & a_{12} & \cdots & a_{1n} & b_1^{(1)} \\
0 & a_{22}^{(1)} & \cdots & a_{2n} & b_2^{(2)} \\
\vdots & & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \cdots & a_{nn}^{(n-1)} & b_n^{(n-1)}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

## Back substitution

$$
x_n = \frac{b_n^{(n-1)}}{a_{nn}^{(n-1)}}
$$

$$
x_i = \frac{b_i^{(i-1)} - \sum\limits_{j=i+1}^{n} a_{ij}^{(i-1)}x_j}{a_{ii}^{(i-1)}}, \quad i = n-1, n-2, \ldots, 1
$$

Examples:

Solve by Gauss elimination:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y + z &= 2 \\
2x + 3z &= 5 \\
x + y + z &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 & 5 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2}
\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{-\frac{1}{2}R_1 + R_3 \to R_3}
\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{-R_2 + R_3 \to R_3}
\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & -\frac{3}{2} & -\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix}
$$

Back substitution gives $z = 1$, $y = 1$, $x = 1$.

A cubic spline is a piecewise cubic function that interpolates ordered data
points and has continuous value, slope and curvature at every interior node.

Let

$$
(x_0,y_0),\ldots,(x_n,y_n),
\qquad x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n
$$

The interpolant is defined on $[x_0,x_n]$. Use $y_j=f(x_j)$ when the data come
from a known function $f$.

On the interval $[x_j,x_{j+1}]$, write

<Formula>

$$
S_j(x)=a_j+b_j(x-x_j)+c_j(x-x_j)^2+d_j(x-x_j)^3,
\qquad x\in[x_j,x_{j+1}],
\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1
$$

</Formula>

At every interior node $x_j$, where $j=1,\ldots,n-1$, adjacent pieces preserve
3 quantities.

- Value  
  $S_{j-1}(x_j)=S_j(x_j)$.
- Slope  
  $S_{j-1}'(x_j)=S_j'(x_j)$.
- Curvature  
  $S_{j-1}''(x_j)=S_j''(x_j)$.

Each piece passes through its 2 endpoint data points.

<Formula>

$$
S_j(x_j)=y_j,
\qquad
S_j(x_{j+1})=y_{j+1}
$$

</Formula>

There are $4n$ coefficient unknowns. The $n+1$ interpolation conditions, the
$2(n-1)$ independent smoothness conditions and 2 boundary conditions determine
them. Value continuity is already enforced by interpolation at each node.

## Boundary Conditions

### Natural Spline

$S''(x_0)=S''(x_n)=0$. Used when endpoint slopes are unavailable.

### Clamped Spline

$S'(x_0)=f'(x_0)$ and $S'(x_n)=f'(x_n)$. Used when endpoint slopes are known.

## Construction

Set

<Formula>

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_j &= x_{j+1}-x_j \\
a_j &= y_j \\
\delta_j &= \frac{a_{j+1}-a_j}{h_j} \qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1
\end{aligned}
$$

</Formula>

The system can be solved in this order:

1. Compute $h_j$ which are mostly constant.
2. Solve for $a_j$ using the interpolation points.
3. Calculate $\delta_j$.
4. Use the boundary condition and the interior smoothness equations to solve for all $c_j$.
5. Use the slope and curvature equations to compute all $b_j$ and $d_j$.
6. Substitute $a_j$, $b_j$, $c_j$ and $d_j$ into each $S_j(x)$.

### Equations Required

The continuity conditions reduce to a tridiagonal system for the $c_j$ values.

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

Start with the value, slope and curvature conditions on $[x_j,x_{j+1}]$:

$$
a_j=y_j,
\qquad
a_j+b_jh_j+c_jh_j^2+d_jh_j^3=y_{j+1}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_j+2c_jh_j+3d_jh_j^2 &= b_{j+1} \\
c_j+3d_jh_j &= c_{j+1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Use the curvature equation to eliminate $d_j$. Use the value and slope equations on adjacent intervals to eliminate $b_j$ and $d_j$.

</Proof>

For each interior node $x_j$, solve

$$
h_{j-1}c_{j-1}+2(h_{j-1}+h_j)c_j+h_jc_{j+1} =3(\delta_j-\delta_{j-1}),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1
$$

The $c_j$ values satisfy $n-1$ interior equations and 2 boundary equations.
The boundary equations depend on the spline type.

For a natural spline, $c_0=c_n=0$. And for a clamped spline:

<Formula>

$$
\begin{aligned}
2h_0c_0+h_0c_1 &= 3\bigl(\delta_0-f'(x_0)\bigr) \\
h_{n-1}c_{n-1}+2h_{n-1}c_n &=3\bigl(f'(x_n)-\delta_{n-1}\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

</Formula>

### Matrix Forms

The above equations for $c_i$ can be expressed with a $n+1$-variable tridiagonal matrix system.

<Formula>

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\alpha_0 & \beta_0 & & & \\
h_0 & 2(h_0+h_1) & h_1 & & \\
& h_1 & 2(h_1+h_2) & \ddots & \\
& & \ddots & \ddots & h_{n-1} \\
& & & \alpha_n & \beta_n
\end{bmatrix}
\mathbf{c}
 =
\begin{bmatrix}
 r_0 \\
 3(\delta_1-\delta_0) \\
\vdots \\
\ 3(\delta_{n-1}-\delta_{n-2}) \\
\ r_n
\end{bmatrix}
$$

</Formula>

The $\mathbf{c}$ is the vector of all $c_i$ ($i$ from $0$ to $n$).

Only $(\alpha_0,\beta_0,r_0)$ and $(\alpha_n,\beta_n,r_n)$ change between the
2 spline types.

| Spline  | First boundary row                                                  | Last boundary row                                                               |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Natural | $\alpha_0=1$, $\beta_0=0$, $r_0=0$                                  | $\alpha_n=0$, $\beta_n=1$, $r_n=0$                                              |
| Clamped | $\alpha_0=2h_0$, $\beta_0=h_0$, $r_0=3\bigl(\delta_0-f'(x_0)\bigr)$ | $\alpha_n=h_{n-1}$, $\beta_n=2h_{n-1}$, $r_n=3\bigl(f'(x_n)-\delta_{n-1}\bigr)$ |

### Coefficient Recovery

After solving for $c_0,\ldots,c_n$, recover the remaining coefficients on each
interval:

<Formula>

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_j &= \delta_j-\frac{h_j}{3}(2c_j+c_{j+1}) \\
d_j &= \frac{c_{j+1}-c_j}{3h_j} \qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1
\end{aligned}
$$

</Formula>

## Error Bound

For $f\in C^4[x_0,x_n]$, let

<Formula>

$$
M=\max_{x_0\le x\le x_n}|f^{(4)}(x)|
$$

</Formula>

If $S$ is the clamped cubic spline interpolant and $f\in C^4[x_0,x_n]$, then

<Formula>

$$
|f(x)-S(x)|\le\frac{5M}{384}
\max_{0\le j\le n-1}h_j^4,
\qquad x\in[x_0,x_n]
$$

</Formula>

The natural spline also has fourth-order convergence, but its bound has a less
simple constant and depends on the endpoint behaviour.

Smaller intervals reduce the clamped error at the 4th-power rate.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Construct the natural cubic spline through $(1,2)$, $(2,3)$ and $(3,5)$.

<Solution>

$$
h_0=h_1=1,
\qquad
a_0=2,
a_1=3,
a_2=5,
\qquad
c_0=c_2=0
$$

<Method label="Direct Method">

The only interior equation is

$$
4c_1=3\bigl((5-3)-(3-2)\bigr)=3
$$

Therefore

$$
c_1=\frac{3}{4},
$$

For $[1,2]$,

$$
b_0=1-\frac{1}{3}\left(0+\frac{3}{4}\right)=\frac{3}{4},
\qquad
d_0=\frac{3/4-0}{3}=\frac{1}{4}
$$

For $[2,3]$,

$$
b_1=2-\frac{1}{3}\left(2\cdot\frac{3}{4}+0\right)=\frac{3}{2},
\qquad
d_1=\frac{0-3/4}{3}=-\frac{1}{4}
$$

The spline is

$$
S(x)=
\begin{cases}
2+\frac{3}{4}(x-1)+\frac{1}{4}(x-1)^3, & 1\le x\le2,\\[4pt]
3+\frac{3}{2}(x-2)+\frac{3}{4}(x-2)^2-\frac{1}{4}(x-2)^3, & 2\le x\le3.
\end{cases}
$$

</Method>

<Method label="Matrix Method">

The natural matrix system is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
1 & 4 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
c_0\\c_1\\c_2
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\3\\0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore

$$
c_0=0,
\qquad
c_1=\frac{3}{4},
\qquad
c_2=0
$$

The coefficient recovery gives

$$
b_0=\frac{3}{4},
\qquad
d_0=\frac{1}{4},
\qquad
b_1=\frac{3}{2},
\qquad
d_1=-\frac{1}{4}
$$

Therefore

$$
S(x)=
\begin{cases}
2+\frac{3}{4}(x-1)+\frac{1}{4}(x-1)^3, & 1\le x\le2,\\[4pt]
3+\frac{3}{2}(x-2)+\frac{3}{4}(x-2)^2-\frac{1}{4}(x-2)^3, & 2\le x\le3.
\end{cases}
$$

</Method>

</Solution>

### Example 2

Construct the clamped cubic spline through $(0,1)$, $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$, given
the endpoint slopes $S'(0)=1$ and $S'(2)=-1$.

<Solution>

$$
h_0=h_1=1,
\qquad
a_0=1,
a_1=2,
a_2=1,
\qquad
\delta_0=1,
\qquad
\delta_1=-1
$$

<Method label="Direct Method">

The clamped boundary equations are

$$
2c_0+c_1=3(1-1)=0,
\qquad
c_1+2c_2=3(-1+1)=0
$$

The interior equation is

$$
c_0+4c_1+c_2=3(-1-1)=-6
$$

Therefore

$$
c_0=1,
\qquad
c_1=-2,
\qquad
c_2=1
$$

The remaining coefficients are

$$
b_0=1,
\qquad
d_0=-1,
\qquad
b_1=0,
\qquad
d_1=1
$$

The spline is

$$
S(x)=
\begin{cases}
1+x+x^2-x^3, & 0\le x\le1,\\[4pt]
2-2(x-1)^2+(x-1)^3, & 1\le x\le2.
\end{cases}
$$

</Method>

<Method label="Matrix Method">

The clamped matrix system is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 4 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
c_0\\c_1\\c_2
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\-6\\0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore

$$
c_0=1,
\qquad
c_1=-2,
\qquad
c_2=1
$$

The remaining coefficients are

$$
b_0=1,
\qquad
d_0=-1,
\qquad
b_1=0,
\qquad
d_1=1
$$

The spline is

$$
S(x)=
\begin{cases}
1+x+x^2-x^3, & 0\le x\le1,\\[4pt]
2-2(x-1)^2+(x-1)^3, & 1\le x\le2.
\end{cases}
$$

</Method>

</Solution>

### Example 3

For $f(x)=x^4$ on $[0,1]$, use a clamped cubic spline with nodes
$x_0=0$, $x_1=\tfrac12$ and $x_2=1$. Find the error bound.

<Solution>

Since

$$
f^{(4)}(x)=24,
$$

we have

$$
M=24,
\qquad
h=\max(h_0,h_1)=\frac12
$$

The clamped error bound is

$$
\lvert f(x)-S(x)\rvert
\le\frac{5M}{384}h^4
=\frac{5(24)}{384}\left(\frac12\right)^4
=\frac{5}{256}
$$

</Solution>

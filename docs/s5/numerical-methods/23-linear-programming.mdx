Linear programming is [constrained optimization](/s5/numerical-methods/constrained-optimization) where the objective function and every constraint are linear.

## Standard Form

Objective function

$$
z = f(x) = a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + \cdots + a_n x_n
$$

Constraints

$$
a_{i1} x_1 + a_{i2} x_2 + \cdots + a_{in} x_n \leq b_i
$$

or with $\geq$ in place of $\leq$.

## Graphical Solution

For 2 design variables, the problem can be solved graphically

- Plot each constraint as a boundary line
- Shade the feasible region satisfying all constraints
- Move the objective function line, $z$ constant, across the feasible region
- The optimum occurs at a vertex of the feasible region

## Worked Example

A factory produces heaters of types $S$ and $L$, sold wholesale at \$40 and \$88 respectively. Machine $M_1$ needs 2 minutes per $S$ heater and 8 minutes per $L$ heater. Machine $M_2$ needs 5 minutes per $S$ heater and 2 minutes per $L$ heater. Both machines run 60 minutes per hour. Find the hourly production rates $x_1$ ($S$) and $x_2$ ($L$) maximizing hourly revenue.

Objective function

$$
z = f(x) = 40x_1 + 88x_2
$$

Constraints

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x_1 + 8x_2 &\leq 60 \\
5x_1 + 2x_2 &\leq 60 \\
x_1 &\geq 0 \\
x_2 &\geq 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The optimum occurs at the vertex $(x_1, x_2) = (10, 5)$, the intersection of the 2 machine-time constraints.

$$
z_{\max} = 40(10) + 88(5) = 840
$$

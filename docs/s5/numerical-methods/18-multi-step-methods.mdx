A multi-step method computes $w_{i+1}$ from several previous approximations, not just $w_i$. More accurate earlier approximations are then folded into the current step.

An $m$-step method for the [IVP](/s5/numerical-methods/initial-value-problems) $y'(t) = f(t, y)$ has a difference equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_{i+1} = {}& a_{m-1} w_i + a_{m-2} w_{i-1} + \cdots + a_0 w_{i+1-m} \\
& + h\big[ b_m f(t_{i+1}, w_{i+1}) + b_{m-1} f(t_i, w_i) + \cdots + b_0 f(t_{i+1-m}, w_{i+1-m}) \big]
\end{aligned}
$$

for $i = m-1, m, \ldots, N-1$, with constants $a_0, \ldots, a_{m-1}$ and $b_0, \ldots, b_m$, and $h = \dfrac{b-a}{N}$.

Starting values $w_0 = \alpha$ and $w_1, \ldots, w_{m-1}$ come from a one-step method such as [Runge-Kutta](/s5/numerical-methods/runge-kutta-methods).

## Explicit and Implicit Methods

- Explicit  
  $b_m = 0$. $w_{i+1}$ is given directly by previously determined values.
- Implicit  
  $b_m \neq 0$. $w_{i+1}$ appears on both sides and must be solved for.

## Adams Methods

Integrating $y' = f(t, y)$ over $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$

$$
y(t_{i+1}) = y(t_i) + \int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}} f(t, y(t))\, \text{d}t
$$

$f(t, y(t))$ is unknown, so it is replaced by a polynomial interpolating the $m$ most recent points, which is then integrated.

Below, $f_k = f(t_k, w_k)$ and $\mu_i$ lies in the interval spanned by the points used.

### Adams-Bashforth Explicit Methods

2-step, with $\tau_{i+1}(h) = \dfrac{5}{12} y'''(\mu_i) h^2$

$$
w_{i+1} = w_i + \frac{h}{2}\left[ 3 f_i - f_{i-1} \right]
$$

3-step, with $\tau_{i+1}(h) = \dfrac{3}{8} y^{(4)}(\mu_i) h^3$

$$
w_{i+1} = w_i + \frac{h}{12}\left[ 23 f_i - 16 f_{i-1} + 5 f_{i-2} \right]
$$

4-step, with $\tau_{i+1}(h) = \dfrac{251}{720} y^{(5)}(\mu_i) h^4$

$$
w_{i+1} = w_i + \frac{h}{24}\left[ 55 f_i - 59 f_{i-1} + 37 f_{i-2} - 9 f_{i-3} \right]
$$

5-step, with $\tau_{i+1}(h) = \dfrac{95}{288} y^{(6)}(\mu_i) h^5$

$$
w_{i+1} = w_i + \frac{h}{720}\left[ 1901 f_i - 2774 f_{i-1} + 2616 f_{i-2} - 1274 f_{i-3} + 251 f_{i-4} \right]
$$

### Adams-Moulton Implicit Methods

2-step, with $\tau_{i+1}(h) = -\dfrac{1}{24} y^{(4)}(\mu_i) h^3$

$$
w_{i+1} = w_i + \frac{h}{12}\left[ 5 f(t_{i+1}, w_{i+1}) + 8 f_i - f_{i-1} \right]
$$

3-step, with $\tau_{i+1}(h) = -\dfrac{19}{720} y^{(5)}(\mu_i) h^4$

$$
w_{i+1} = w_i + \frac{h}{24}\left[ 9 f(t_{i+1}, w_{i+1}) + 19 f_i - 5 f_{i-1} + f_{i-2} \right]
$$

4-step, with $\tau_{i+1}(h) = -\dfrac{3}{160} y^{(6)}(\mu_i) h^5$

$$
w_{i+1} = w_i + \frac{h}{720}\left[ 251 f(t_{i+1}, w_{i+1}) + 646 f_i - 264 f_{i-1} + 106 f_{i-2} - 19 f_{i-3} \right]
$$

<Note>

The $m$-step Adams-Bashforth method and the $(m-1)$-step Adams-Moulton method both use $m$ evaluations of $f$ per step and have error of order $O(h^m)$. The implicit method is the more accurate of the pair.

</Note>

## Predictor-Corrector Method

Implicit methods usually cannot be solved for $w_{i+1}$ in closed form. An explicit method and an implicit method are combined.

- Predict  
  Compute $w_{i+1}^{(p)}$ with an explicit method.
- Correct  
  Substitute $w_{i+1}^{(p)}$ into the right side of an implicit method to get $w_{i+1}$.

A standard choice uses Runge-Kutta for the starting values, Adams-Bashforth 4-step as predictor, and Adams-Moulton 3-step as corrector. This is more accurate than either Adams method used alone.

## Worked Example

IVP $y'(t) = y - t^2 + 1$, $0 \leq t \leq 2$, $y(0) = 0.5$, with $h = 0.2$, by Adams-Bashforth 2-step. Exact solution $y(t) = (t+1)^2 - 0.5 e^t$, used only to check the result.

The 2-step method needs $w_0$ and $w_1$ before it can start. $w_0 = \alpha = 0.5$ is given; $w_1$ is computed with one step of [order 4 Runge-Kutta](/s5/numerical-methods/runge-kutta-methods).

Starting value $w_1$ by Runge-Kutta, $t_0 = 0$, $w_0 = 0.5$

$$
\begin{aligned}
k_1 &= 0.2\, f(0,\ 0.5) = 0.3 \\
k_2 &= 0.2\, f(0.1,\ 0.5 + 0.15) = 0.328 \\
k_3 &= 0.2\, f(0.1,\ 0.5 + 0.164) = 0.3308 \\
k_4 &= 0.2\, f(0.2,\ 0.5 + 0.3308) = 0.35816 \\
w_1 &= 0.5 + \tfrac{1}{6}\left( k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4 \right) = 0.829293
\end{aligned}
$$

Step to $t_2 = 0.4$ by Adams-Bashforth

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_0 &= w_0 - 0.0^2 + 1 = 1.5 \\
f_1 &= w_1 - 0.2^2 + 1 = 1.789293 \\
w_2 &= w_1 + \frac{0.2}{2}\left[ 3 f_1 - f_0 \right] = 0.829293 + 0.1\,(3.867879) = 1.216081
\end{aligned}
$$

Exact $y(0.4) \approx 1.214088$.

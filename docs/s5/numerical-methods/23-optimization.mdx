Optimization is the process of finding the input to a function that minimizes or maximizes it.

## Objective Function

The quantity being optimized, $f(x)$, is the objective function or merit function. The quantities free to adjust, $x$, are the design variables.

## Turning Points

For a differentiable function on a closed interval, an absolute optimum can occur at an endpoint or at an interior turning point. At an interior turning point, $f'(x) = 0$. The second derivative $f''(x)$ determines the type

- Maximum\
  $f''(x) < 0$.
- Minimum\
  $f''(x) > 0$.

## Saddle Points

A saddle point is a stationary point that is neither a local maximum nor a local minimum.

For a function of 2 variables, $(x_0, y_0)$ is a saddle point if

- Stationary\
  $f_x(x_0, y_0) = 0$ and $f_y(x_0, y_0) = 0$.
- Indefinite curvature\
  The function increases in some directions and decreases in other directions near $(x_0, y_0)$.

The Hessian test uses

$$
D = f_{xx}(x_0, y_0)f_{yy}(x_0, y_0) - [f_{xy}(x_0, y_0)]^2
$$

- $D > 0$ and $f_{xx}(x_0, y_0) > 0$\
  Local minimum.
- $D > 0$ and $f_{xx}(x_0, y_0) < 0$\
  Local maximum.
- $D < 0$\
  Saddle point.
- $D = 0$\
  The test is inconclusive.

## One-Dimensional Optimization

One-dimensional optimization finds $x$ such that $f'(x) = 0$.

## Constrained and Unconstrained Optimization

- Unconstrained optimization\
  No restrictions are placed on the design variables. See steepest ascent method.
- Constrained optimization\
  Restrictions are placed on the design variables. See constrained optimization.

## Local and Global Optima

A function is unimodal **iff** it has a single optimum. A function with more than 1 local optimum is multimodal.

<Note>

A search can stop at a local optimum, so check it against the global optimum.

</Note>

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Find and classify the turning points of $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1$.

<Solution>

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3)
$$

$f'(x) = 0$ at $x = 1$ and $x = 3$.

$$
f''(x) = 6x - 12
$$

At $x = 1$

$$
f''(1) = -6 < 0, \quad f(1) = 5
$$

A local maximum.

At $x = 3$

$$
f''(3) = 6 > 0, \quad f(3) = 1
$$

A local minimum.

$f$ has 2 local optima, so it's multimodal. It has no global optimum on $\mathbb{R}$, since $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$.

</Solution>

### Example 2

Find and classify the turning point of $f(x) = -x^2 + 4x - 1$.

<Solution>

$$
f'(x) = -2x + 4 = 0 \implies x = 2
$$

$$
f''(2) = -2 < 0, \quad f(2) = 3
$$

A maximum. $f$ is unimodal, so it is also the global maximum.

</Solution>

### Example 3

Find and classify the stationary point of $f(x, y) = x^2 - y^2$.

<Solution>

$$
f_x = 2x, \qquad f_y = -2y
$$

The stationary point is $(0, 0)$.

$$
f_{xx} = 2, \qquad f_{yy} = -2, \qquad f_{xy} = 0
$$

$$
D = (2)(-2) - 0^2 = -4 < 0
$$

The stationary point $(0, 0)$ is a saddle point.

</Solution>

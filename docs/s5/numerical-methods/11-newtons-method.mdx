Newton's method for nonlinear systems is a [fixed-point iteration](/s5/numerical-methods/fixed-point-method) that uses the [Jacobian matrix](/s2/methods-of-mathematics/multivariable-calculus/jacobian#jacobian-matrix) $J(\vec{x})$ of $F$ to get quadratic convergence.

## Iteration

$$
G(\vec{x}) = \vec{x} - J(\vec{x})^{-1}F(\vec{x})
$$

$$
\vec{x}^{(k)} = \vec{x}^{(k-1)} - J(\vec{x}^{(k-1)})^{-1}F(\vec{x}^{(k-1)}), \quad k \geq 1
$$

<Note>

In practice, solve the linear system

$$
J(\vec{x}^{(k-1)})\vec{y}^{(k-1)} = -F(\vec{x}^{(k-1)})
$$

by [Gaussian elimination](/s5/numerical-methods/gaussian-elimination) instead of inverting $J$, then set $\vec{x}^{(k)} = \vec{x}^{(k-1)} + \vec{y}^{(k-1)}$.

</Note>

Convergence is quadratic, provided a sufficiently accurate starting value $\vec{x}^{(0)}$ is known and $J(\vec{p})^{-1}$ exists.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Apply Newton's method to the [nonlinear system](/s5/numerical-methods/non-linear-system-of-equations#example-1) with its [Jacobian](/s2/methods-of-mathematics/multivariable-calculus/jacobian#example-1), starting from $\vec{x}^{(0)} = (0.1, 0.1, -0.1)^T$.

<Solution>

$$
F(\vec{x}^{(0)}) \approx (-1.199950, -2.269833, 8.462025)^T
$$

$$
J(\vec{x}^{(0)}) \approx
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & 0.001000 & -0.001000 \\
0.200000 & -32.400000 & 0.995004 \\
-0.099005 & -0.099005 & 20.000000
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Solving $J(\vec{x}^{(0)})\vec{y}^{(0)} = -F(\vec{x}^{(0)})$ gives

$$
\vec{y}^{(0)} \approx (0.399870, -0.080533, -0.421520)^T
$$

$$
\vec{x}^{(1)} = \vec{x}^{(0)} + \vec{y}^{(0)} \approx (0.499870, 0.019467, -0.521520)^T
$$

Repeating converges to $\vec{x}^{(5)} \approx (0.500000, 0.000000, -0.523599)^T$, matching the exact solution $\vec{p} = (0.5, 0, -\pi/6)$.

</Solution>

### Example 2

Apply Newton's method to

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1^2 + x_2^2 - 4 &= 0 \\
x_1 x_2 - 1 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

starting from $\vec{x}^{(0)} = (2, 0.5)^T$.

<Solution>

$$
J(\vec{x}) = \begin{pmatrix} 2x_1 & 2x_2 \\ x_2 & x_1 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Iteration 1

$$
F(\vec{x}^{(0)}) = \begin{pmatrix} 0.25 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
J(\vec{x}^{(0)}) = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 0.5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Solving $J\vec{y} = -F$ gives $\vec{y}^{(0)} = (-0.066667, 0.016667)^T$.

$$
\vec{x}^{(1)} = (1.933333, 0.516667)^T
$$

Iteration 2

$$
F(\vec{x}^{(1)}) = \begin{pmatrix} 0.004722 \\ -0.001111 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
J(\vec{x}^{(1)}) = \begin{pmatrix} 3.866667 & 1.033333 \\ 0.516667 & 1.933333 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\vec{y}^{(1)} = (-0.001481, 0.000970)^T, \quad
\vec{x}^{(2)} = (1.931853, 0.517637)^T
$$

The exact root is $x_1 = \sqrt{2 + \sqrt{3}} = 1.931852$, $x_2 = 1/x_1 = 0.517638$.

</Solution>

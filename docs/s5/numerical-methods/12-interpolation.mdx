Interpolation estimates values between known data points.

Given $n+1$ points $(x_i,y_i)$ with distinct nodes

<Formula>

$$
x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n,
$$

</Formula>

find a function $\phi$ satisfying

<Formula>

$$
\phi(x_i)=y_i, \qquad i=0,1,\ldots,n.
$$

</Formula>

Use $\phi(x)$ for $x\in[x_0,x_n]$. This is interpolation. Values outside this interval are extrapolation. They are less reliable.

## Choosing an Interpolant

Choose the form from the data.

| Data                     | Interpolant          | Key Property                        |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Few points               | Polynomial           | One formula through every point     |
| Many non-periodic points | Piecewise polynomial | Low-degree formula on each interval |
| Periodic data            | Trigonometric        | Repeating sine and cosine terms     |
| Poles or sharp changes   | Rational             | Ratio of polynomials                |
| Values and slopes known  | Hermite              | Matches values and derivatives      |

## Polynomial Interpolation

<Theorem>

For distinct nodes $x_0,\ldots,x_n$ and values $y_0,\ldots,y_n$, exactly 1 polynomial $p_n$ of degree at most $n$ satisfies

<Formula>

$$
p_n(x_i)=y_i.
$$

</Formula>

</Theorem>

The Lagrange form is

<Formula>

$$
p_n(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_iL_i(x),
\qquad
L_i(x)=\prod_{\substack{j=0\\j\ne i}}^{n}\frac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j}.
$$

</Formula>

Each basis polynomial has 1 job.

<Formula>

$$
L_i(x_j)=
\begin{cases}
1, & i=j,\\
0, & i\ne j.
\end{cases}
$$

</Formula>

Therefore $p_n(x_j)=y_j$. At node $x_j$, every term disappears except $y_jL_j(x_j)$.

<Note>

High-degree polynomial interpolation at equally spaced nodes can oscillate near the interval ends. This is the Runge phenomenon.

</Note>

## Piecewise Polynomial Interpolation

Split $[x_0,x_n]$ into intervals. Use a low-degree polynomial on each interval.

- Piecewise linear  
  Straight segments. Continuous with corners at the nodes.
- Piecewise quadratic  
  Parabolic segments. Slopes can jump at the nodes.
- Cubic spline  
  Cubic segments with continuous value, slope and curvature.

## Other Forms

For periodic data,

<Formula>

$$
\phi(x)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(a_k\cos(kx)+b_k\sin(kx)\right).
$$

</Formula>

For rational interpolation,

<Formula>

$$
\phi(x)=\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.
$$

</Formula>

For Hermite interpolation, $\phi$ matches both $y_i$ and $y_i'$ at each node.

A norm is a real-valued function measuring the size of a vector or a matrix.

## Vector Norm

For $\vec{x} = (x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n)^T$, 3 standard vector norms:

### 1-Norm

<Aka>taxicab norm</Aka>. Sum of the absolute values of $\vec{x}$'s components.
Measures distance travelled along grid lines rather than in a straight line.

<Formula>

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_1 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i|
$$

</Formula>

### 2-Norm

<Aka>Euclidean norm</Aka>. Straight-line distance from $\vec{x}$ to the origin.

<Formula>

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_2 = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2 \right)^{1/2}
$$

</Formula>

### Infinity Norm

Largest absolute value among $\vec{x}$'s components.

<Formula>

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_\infty = \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} |x_i|
$$

</Formula>

## Equivalent Norms

2 norms $\lVert \cdot \rVert_{v1}, \lVert \cdot \rVert_{v2}$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ are equivalent **iff** there exist positive constants $c_1, c_2$ such that for all $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$,

<Formula>

$$
c_1 \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_{v1} \leq \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_{v2} \leq c_2 \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_{v1}
$$

</Formula>

$\lVert \cdot \rVert_1, \lVert \cdot \rVert_2, \lVert \cdot \rVert_\infty$ are equivalent on $\mathbb{R}^n$.

<Formula>

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_\infty \leq \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_2 \leq \sqrt{n} \, \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_\infty
$$

</Formula>

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_2 \leq \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_1 \leq \sqrt{n} \, \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_2
$$

<Formula>

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_\infty \leq \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_1 \leq n \, \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_\infty
$$

</Formula>

## Matrix Norm

A matrix norm is a norm on the set of $n \times n$ matrices, additionally satisfying submultiplicativity: $\lVert AB \rVert \leq \lVert A \rVert \, \lVert B \rVert$ for all $n \times n$ matrices $A, B$.

### Induced Matrix Norm

<Aka>operator norm, subordinate norm</Aka>. The matrix norm induced by a vector
norm $\lVert \cdot \rVert_v$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$

<Formula>

$$
\lVert A \rVert_M = \sup_{\vec{x} \neq \vec{0}} \frac{\lVert A\vec{x} \rVert_v}{\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_v} = \sup_{\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_v = 1} \lVert A\vec{x} \rVert_v
$$

</Formula>

For $A = (a_{ij})$, $1 \leq i, j \leq n$:

<Formula>

$$
\lVert A \rVert_\infty = \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} |a_{ij}| \quad \text{(maximum row sum)}
$$

</Formula>

$$
\lVert A \rVert_1 = \max_{1 \leq j \leq n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |a_{ij}| \quad \text{(maximum column sum)}
$$

### Spectrum

The spectrum of $A$, $\sigma(A)$, is the set of all eigenvalues of $A$.

<Formula>

$$
\sigma(A) = \{ \lambda \; ; \; A\vec{v} = \lambda \vec{v}, \vec{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n, \lambda \text{ is an eigenvalue of } A \}
$$

</Formula>

### Spectral Radius

The spectral radius of $A$, $\rho(A)$, is the maximum absolute value among the eigenvalues of $A$.

<Formula>

$$
\rho(A) = \max \{ |\lambda| \; ; \; \lambda \in \sigma(A) \}
$$

</Formula>

The matrix norm induced by the 2-norm

<Formula>

$$
\lVert A \rVert_2 = \sqrt{\rho(A^TA)}
$$

</Formula>

<Note>

For any matrix norm $\lVert \cdot \rVert_m$ induced by a vector norm, $\rho(A) \leq \lVert A \rVert_m$ for all $n \times n$ matrices $A$.

</Note>

## Worked Example

### Vector Norms

For $\vec{x} = (3, -4, 12)^T$, find the $1$-norm, $2$-norm, and infinity norm.

<Solution>

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_1 = 3 + 4 + 12 = 19
$$

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_2 = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = 13
$$

$$
\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_\infty = \max\{3, 4, 12\} = 12
$$

These satisfy $\lVert \vec{x} \rVert_\infty \leq \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_2 \leq \lVert \vec{x} \rVert_1$.

</Solution>

### Induced Matrix Norms

For

<Formula>

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}
$$

</Formula>

find $\lVert A \rVert_1$ and $\lVert A \rVert_\infty$.

<Solution>

$$
\lVert A \rVert_\infty = \max\{1 + 2,\ 3 + 4\} = 7
$$

$$
\lVert A \rVert_1 = \max\{1 + 3,\ 2 + 4\} = 6
$$

</Solution>

### Spectral-Radius Inequality

For

<Formula>

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}
$$

</Formula>

verify $\rho(A) \leq \lVert A \rVert_2$.

<Solution>

First calculate $\lVert A \rVert_2$.

$$
A^TA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ -2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & 10 \\ 10 & 20 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\det(A^TA - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 - 30\lambda + 100 = 0 \implies \lambda = 15 \pm 5\sqrt{5}
$$

$$
\rho(A^TA) = 15 + 5\sqrt{5} \approx 26.180, \quad \lVert A \rVert_2 = \sqrt{26.180} \approx 5.117
$$

Spectral radius of $A$

$$
\det(A - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 10 = 0 \implies \lambda = \frac{5 \pm i\sqrt{15}}{2}
$$

$$
\rho(A) = \sqrt{\frac{25 + 15}{4}} = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.162
$$

$3.162 \leq 5.117$, so $\rho(A) \leq \lVert A \rVert_2$ holds. It also holds for $\lVert A \rVert_1 = 6$ and $\lVert A \rVert_\infty = 7$.

</Solution>

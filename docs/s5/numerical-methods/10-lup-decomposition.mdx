The LU decomposition with partial pivoting of an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is a triple $L, U, P$ such that

$$
PA = LU
$$

where:

- $L$: $n \times n$ lower triangular, with all diagonal entries $1$
- $U$: $n \times n$ upper triangular
- $P$: $n \times n$ permutation matrix

Properties:

- $P$ permutes the rows of $A$ to put large entries in the top-left position of $A$ and each sub-matrix encountered during the recursion, avoiding division by a small or zero element.
- LUP decomposition always exists for $A$.
- LUP decomposition of $A$ is not unique.
- LUP decomposition solves linear systems more robustly than plain LU decomposition, at approximately the same cost.

To solve $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ using an LUP decomposition:

$$
A\vec{x} = \vec{b} \implies PA\vec{x} = P\vec{b} \implies LU\vec{x} = P\vec{b}
$$

then solve $LU\vec{x} = P\vec{b}$ as in [LU decomposition](/s5/numerical-methods/lu-decomposition).

## Recursive leading-row-column algorithm

Finds $L, U, P$ so that $PA = LU$:

- Choose $i$ so row $i$ of $A$ has the largest absolute first entry, $|A_{i1}| \ge |A_{j1}|$ for all $j$. Let $P_1$ pivot row $i$ to the first row, leaving all other rows in order.
- Let $\bar{A} = P_1 A$.
- Let $P_2$ leave the first row fixed and permute the remaining rows, so $P_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & P_{22} \end{pmatrix}$.
- Factor the full permutation as $P = P_2 P_1$.
- Solve $P_{22}(\bar{A}_{22} - \bar{a}_{21}\bar{a}_{11}^{-1}\bar{a}_{12}) = L_{22}U_{22}$ recursively for the remaining rows and columns of $L$ and $U$.

Examples:

Find the LU factorization with partial pivoting of

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 2 & -1 \\ 6 & -6 & 7 \\ 3 & -4 & 4 \end{pmatrix}
$$

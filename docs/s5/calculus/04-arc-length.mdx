Let $\vec{r}(t)$ be defined on $[a, b]$, and let $P = \{t_0, t_1, \dots, t_n\}$, $a = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = b$, be a partition of $[a, b]$. The polygonal length corresponding to $P$ is

$$
s(\vec{r}, P) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \lVert \vec{r}(t_k) - \vec{r}(t_{k-1}) \rVert
$$

The curve is rectifiable **iff** these polygonal lengths have a finite least upper bound. That bound is the arc length of the curve.

If $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$, $z = z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$, have continuous derivatives on $[a, b]$, the arc length from $t = a$ to $t = b$ is

$$
L = \int_a^b \sqrt{\left( \frac{\text{d}x}{\text{d}t} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}t} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\text{d}z}{\text{d}t} \right)^2} \, \text{d}t
$$

Equivalently, for $\vec{r}(t)$ continuously differentiable on $[a, b]$ and $C$ the curve traced by $\vec{r}(t)$,

$$
L = \int_a^b \left\lVert \frac{\text{d}\vec{r}}{\text{d}t} \right\rVert \text{d}t
$$

<Note>

Vector-valued functions do not satisfy a direct Mean Value Theorem of the form $\vec{r}(b) - \vec{r}(a) = \vec{r}'(c)(b-a)$. Arc length is defined via polygonal approximations and computed by the integral formula only once the curve is sufficiently smooth.

</Note>

Let $C$ be a graph of a $C^1$ vector-valued function $\vec{r}$, parametrized by arc length $s$, with $T'(s)$ existing. The curvature of $C$ at $s$ is

$$
\kappa(s) = \left\lVert \frac{\text{d}T}{\text{d}s} \right\rVert
$$

Since $T(s) = \vec{r}'(s)$ for an arc-length parametrization, if $\vec{r}''(s)$ exists,

$$
\kappa(s) = \lVert \vec{r}''(s) \rVert
$$

<Note>

A vector-valued function is $C^1$ on an interval **iff** its derivative exists and is continuous on that interval.

</Note>

If $\vec{r}(t)$ is twice differentiable and $\vec{r}'(t) \ne \vec{0}$, the curvature of $C$ is

$$
\kappa(t) = \frac{\lVert T'(t) \rVert}{\lVert \vec{r}'(t) \rVert}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\kappa(t) = \frac{\lVert \vec{r}'(t) \times \vec{r}''(t) \rVert}{\lVert \vec{r}'(t) \rVert^3}
$$

Examples:

- The curvature of a straight line is $0$.
- The curvature of a circle with radius $a > 0$ is $\frac{1}{a}$.

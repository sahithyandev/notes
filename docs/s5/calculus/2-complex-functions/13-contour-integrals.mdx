A contour is a curve $C$ in the complex plane constructed by joining finitely many smooth curves end to end.

## Contour Integral

Let $C$ be a contour with initial point $A$ and terminal point $B$.

A partition of $C$ is a finite set of points $P = \{z_0, z_1, \ldots, z_n\}$, occurring in order along $C$, with $z_0 = A$ and $z_n = B$.

$$
\Delta z_k = z_k - z_{k-1}, \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots, n
$$

$c_k$: a point on the part of $C$ between $z_{k-1}$ and $z_k$.

The Riemann sum for this partition is

$$
\sum_{k=1}^n f(c_k)\Delta z_k
$$

The contour integral of $f$ over $C$ is

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = \lim_{\|P\| \to 0} \sum_{k=1}^n f(c_k)\Delta z_k
$$

provided the limit exists and is independent of the choice of partition and points $c_k$.

$\|P\|$: the maximum length of the subarcs determined by $P$.

## Evaluation via Parametrization

Let $f(z)$ be continuous on a set containing the contour $C$, and let $z = z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$, be a piecewise smooth parametrization of $C$.

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = \int_a^b f(z(t))z'(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

When $z(t)$ is only piecewise smooth, the right side is evaluated piecewise.

<Note>

The value of $\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z$ depends on the path $C$, not just the endpoints, unless $f$ is analytic on a simply connected domain containing $C$.

</Note>

## Integral Triangle Inequality

Let $f(t) = u(t) + iv(t)$ be continuous on $[a,b]$.

$$
\left|\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t\right| \le \int_a^b |f(t)|\,\text{d}t
$$

## ML Inequality

Let $f$ be continuous on a contour $C$, with $|f(z)| \le M$ for all $z$ on $C$, and let $L$ be the length of $C$.

$$
\left|\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z\right| \le ML
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Parametrize $C$ by $z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$
- Apply the integral triangle inequality to $\int_a^b f(z(t))z'(t)\,\text{d}t$
- Bound $|f(z(t))|$ by $M$
- Recognize $\int_a^b |z'(t)|\,\text{d}t$ as $L$

</Note>

Let $f$ be analytic in a simply connected domain $D$, and let $z_0$ be a fixed point in $D$. For each $z \in D$, let $C_z$ be any contour in $D$ from $z_0$ to $z$.

$$
F(z) = \int_{C_z} f(\zeta)\,\text{d}\zeta
$$

$F$ is analytic in $D$ and

$$
F'(z) = f(z)
$$

<Note>

$F$ is well defined because $\int_{C_z} f(\zeta)\,\text{d}\zeta$ is independent of the choice of $C_z$, by the Cauchy-Goursat theorem.

</Note>

## Definite Integrals

Let $f$ be analytic in a simply connected domain $D$, and let $F$ be an antiderivative of $f$ in $D$. If $C$ is any contour in $D$ from $z_1$ to $z_2$,

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = F(z_2) - F(z_1)
$$

Let $f$ be analytic in a [simply connected](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/connectedness#simply-connected-region) domain $D$, and let $z_0$ be a fixed point in $D$. For each $z \in D$, let $C_z$ be any contour in $D$ from $z_0$ to $z$.

$$
F(z) = \int_{C_z} f(\zeta)\,\text{d}\zeta
$$

$F$ is analytic in $D$ and

$$
F'(z) = f(z)
$$

<Note>

$F$ is well defined because $\int_{C_z} f(\zeta)\,\text{d}\zeta$ is independent of the choice of $C_z$, by the [Cauchy-Goursat theorem](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour-integral#cauchy-goursat-theorem).

</Note>

## Definite Integrals

Let $f$ be analytic in a [simply connected](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/connectedness#simply-connected-region) domain $D$, and let $F$ be an antiderivative of $f$ in $D$. If $C$ is any contour in $D$ from $z_1$ to $z_2$,

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = F(z_2) - F(z_1)
$$

## Cauchy's Integral Formula

Let $f$ be analytic in a [simply connected](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/connectedness#simply-connected-region) domain $D$, and let $C$ be a [positively oriented](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour#positive-orientation) [simple closed](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour#simple-closed-contour) contour lying in $D$. If $z_0$ lies inside $C$,

$$
f(z_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_C \frac{f(z)}{z - z_0}\,\text{d}z
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\int_C \frac{f(z)}{z - z_0}\,\text{d}z = 2\pi i f(z_0)
$$

### Derivatives

Under the same conditions, for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$,

$$
f^{(n)}(z_0) = \frac{n!}{2\pi i}\int_C \frac{f(z)}{(z-z_0)^{n+1}}\,\text{d}z
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\int_C \frac{f(z)}{(z-z_0)^{n+1}}\,\text{d}z = \frac{2\pi i}{n!}f^{(n)}(z_0)
$$

<Note>

Analyticity of $f$ on $D$ forces $f$ to be infinitely differentiable there, since $f^{(n)}(z_0)$ exists for every $n$.

</Note>

## Trigonometric Functions

For any complex number $z$,

$$
\sin z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{z^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}, \qquad \cos z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{z^{2n}}{(2n)!}
$$

### Exponential Form

$e^{iz} = \cos z + i\sin z$ for each $z \in \mathbb{C}$, so

$$
\sin z = \frac{e^{iz} - e^{-iz}}{2i}, \qquad \cos z = \frac{e^{iz} + e^{-iz}}{2}
$$

The hyperbolic functions used below are defined by

$$
\sinh z = \frac{e^z - e^{-z}}{2}, \qquad \cosh z = \frac{e^z + e^{-z}}{2}
$$

### Trigonometric Properties

- $\sin z$ and $\cos z$ are [entire functions](/s1/mathematics/complex-analysis/entire-functions)
- Standard real trigonometric identities hold for complex arguments
- $\sin z = \sin x \cosh y + i\cos x \sinh y$, where $z = x + iy$
- $\cos z = \cos x \cosh y - i\sin x \sinh y$, where $z = x + iy$

### Other Trigonometric Functions

The trigonometric functions $\tan z$, $\cot z$, $\sec z$, and $\csc z$ are defined from $\sin z$ and $\cos z$ as in the real case.

### Trigonometric Analyticity

$\sin z$ and $\cos z$ are analytic on $\mathbb{C}$.

$$
\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\sin z = \cos z, \qquad \frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\cos z = -\sin z
$$

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

- Write $\sin z$ and $\cos z$ in exponential form
- Note that $e^{iz}$ and $e^{-iz}$ are analytic on $\mathbb{C}$ by the chain rule
- Combine them using the algebra of analytic functions
- Differentiate term by term

</Proof>

The other trigonometric functions are quotients, so each is analytic wherever its denominator is non-zero.

| Function           | Analytic on                                     |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| $\tan z$, $\sec z$ | $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{\frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi\}$ |
| $\cot z$, $\csc z$ | $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{n\pi\}$                 |

Here $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

$\sin z = 0$ **iff** $z = n\pi$, and $\cos z = 0$ **iff** $z = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$. All zeros are real.

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

- Write $\sin z = \sin x\cosh y + i\cos x\sinh y$
- Set both the real and imaginary parts to $0$
- $\cosh y \ge 1$, so $\sin x = 0$
- Then $\cos x = \pm 1$, so $\sinh y = 0$ and $y = 0$
- Repeat with $\cos z = \cos x\cosh y - i\sin x\sinh y$

</Proof>

## Hyperbolic Functions

The definitions above give the hyperbolic functions for any complex number $z$.

### Hyperbolic Properties

- $\sinh z$ and $\cosh z$ are [entire functions](/s1/mathematics/complex-analysis/entire-functions)
- $\cosh z = \cosh x \cos y + i\sinh x \sin y$, where $z = x + iy$
- $\sinh z = \sinh x \cos y + i\cosh x \sin y$, where $z = x + iy$

### Other Hyperbolic Functions

$$
\tanh z = \frac{\sinh z}{\cosh z}, \qquad \coth z = \frac{\cosh z}{\sinh z}
$$

$$
\operatorname{sech} z = \frac{1}{\cosh z}, \qquad \operatorname{csch} z = \frac{1}{\sinh z}
$$

### Hyperbolic Analyticity

$\sinh z$ and $\cosh z$ are analytic on $\mathbb{C}$, since $e^z$ and $e^{-z}$ are.

The other hyperbolic functions are quotients, so each is analytic wherever its denominator is non-zero.

| Function                           | Analytic on                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| $\tanh z$, $\operatorname{sech} z$ | $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{i(\frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi)\}$ |
| $\coth z$, $\operatorname{csch} z$ | $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{n\pi i\}$                  |

Here $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

$\sinh z = 0$ **iff** $z = n\pi i$, and $\cosh z = 0$ **iff** $z = i(\frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi)$. All zeros are purely imaginary.

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

- Use $\sinh z = -i\sin(iz)$ and $\cosh z = \cos(iz)$
- $\sin(iz) = 0$ **iff** $iz = n\pi$
- $\cos(iz) = 0$ **iff** $iz = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$
- Multiply by $-i$ to solve for $z$

</Proof>

### Derivatives

- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,\cosh z = \sinh z$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,\sinh z = \cosh z$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,\tanh z = \operatorname{sech}^2 z$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,\coth z = -\operatorname{csch}^2 z$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,\operatorname{sech} z = -\operatorname{sech} z \tanh z$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,\operatorname{csch} z = -\operatorname{csch} z \coth z$

### Identities

Valid whenever the expressions involved are defined.

- $\cosh^2 z - \sinh^2 z = 1$
- $\sinh(z_1 + z_2) = \sinh z_1 \cosh z_2 + \cosh z_1 \sinh z_2$
- $\cosh(z_1 + z_2) = \cosh z_1 \cosh z_2 + \sinh z_1 \sinh z_2$
- $\cosh(-z) = \cosh z$
- $\sinh(-z) = -\sinh z$
- $\cosh(z + 2\pi i) = \cosh z$
- $\sinh(z + 2\pi i) = \sinh z$

### Infinite Series

Both series are Maclaurin series. They converge for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$, since $\sinh z$ and $\cosh z$ are entire.

$$
\sinh z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!} = z + \frac{z^3}{3!} + \frac{z^5}{5!} + \cdots
$$

$$
\cosh z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{2n}}{(2n)!} = 1 + \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} + \cdots
$$

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

- Start with $e^z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \dfrac{z^n}{n!}$
- Write $e^{-z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \dfrac{(-z)^n}{n!}$
- Substitute both into the definitions
- Odd powers cancel in $\cosh z$, even powers cancel in $\sinh z$

</Proof>

## Relations

- $\cosh(iz) = \cos z$
- $\sinh(iz) = i\sin z$
- $\cos(iz) = \cosh z$
- $\sin(iz) = i\sinh z$

## Worked Examples

Evaluate $\sin(1+i)$ and $\cos(1+i)$ in terms of real trigonometric and hyperbolic functions.

<Solution>

Use the component formulas with $x=1$ and $y=1$:

$$
\sin(1+i)=\sin 1\cosh 1+i\cos 1\sinh 1
$$

$$
\cos(1+i)=\cos 1\cosh 1-i\sin 1\sinh 1
$$

</Solution>

Find all zeros of $\cosh z$ in the strip $-2\pi < \operatorname{Im}z \le 2\pi$.

<Solution>

The zeros satisfy

$$
z=i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+n\pi\right), \qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}
$$

Write $z=iy$. The strip condition becomes $-2\pi<y\le2\pi$. Hence

$$
-2\pi<\frac{\pi}{2}+n\pi\le2\pi
$$

which gives $n\in\{-2,-1,0,1\}$. Therefore the zeros are

$$
z\in\left\{-\frac{3\pi i}{2},-\frac{\pi i}{2},\frac{\pi i}{2},\frac{3\pi i}{2}\right\}
$$

</Solution>

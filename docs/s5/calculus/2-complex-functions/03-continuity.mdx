Let $D \subseteq \mathbb{C}$, let $f: D \to \mathbb{C}$ be a function. $f$ is continuous at $z_0 \in D$ **iff**, for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that, for every $z \in D$,

$$
|z-z_0| < \delta \implies |f(z)-f(z_0)| < \epsilon
$$

$f$ is continuous on $A \subseteq D$ **iff** $f$ is continuous at each point of $A$.

## Continuity at a Limit Point

Let $f: D \to \mathbb{C}$, $z_0 \in D$.

- If $z_0$ is not an isolated point of $D$, $f$ is continuous at $z_0$ **iff** $\displaystyle\lim_{z \to z_0} f(z) = f(z_0)$
- If $z_0$ is an isolated point of $D$, $f$ is continuous at $z_0$

## Composition of Continuous Functions

A composition of [continuous](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/continuity) functions is continuous.

## Nonvanishing Near a Point

If $f$ is continuous and non-zero at $z_0$, then $f(z) \ne 0$ throughout some neighborhood of $z_0$.

## Boundedness on a Closed and Bounded Set

If $f$ is continuous on a closed and bounded set $R \subseteq \mathbb{C}$, there exists a non-negative real number $M$ such that

$$
|f(z)| \le M
$$

for all $z \in R$, with equality for at least 1 point in $R$.

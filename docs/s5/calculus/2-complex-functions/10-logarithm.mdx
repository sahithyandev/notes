For a non-zero complex number $z$, $\log z$ is the set of all $w \in \mathbb{C}$ with $e^w = z$.

Writing $z = re^{i\theta}$ with $r = |z|$ and $w = u + iv$, the condition $e^w = z$ gives $e^u = r$ and $v \in \arg z$. So

$$
\log z = \ln|z| + i\arg z = \{\,\ln|z| + i(\operatorname{Arg} z + 2k\pi) : k \in \mathbb{Z}\,\}, \qquad z \ne 0
$$

Here:

- $\operatorname{Arg} z$: principal argument of $z$, the unique argument with $-\pi < \operatorname{Arg} z \le \pi$
- $\arg z = \{\operatorname{Arg} z + 2k\pi : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Write $z = re^{i\theta}$ with $r = |z| > 0$
- Set $w = u + iv$ and require $e^w = z$
- Take the modulus of both sides: $e^u = r$, so $u = \ln r$
- Match the arguments, giving $v = \theta + 2k\pi$ for some $k \in \mathbb{Z}$
- Combine $u$ and $v$ into $w = \ln|z| + i\arg z$

</Note>

$\log z$ is multi-valued.

## Principal Value

The logarithm value deduced by setting $k=0$.

$$
\operatorname{Log} z = \ln|z| + i\operatorname{Arg} z, \qquad z \ne 0
$$

Below you can change $z$ and see how $\operatorname{Log}(z)$ changes.

## Analyticity

$\operatorname{Log} z$ is analytic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus (-\infty, 0]$ and

$$
\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\operatorname{Log} z = \frac{1}{z}
$$

$\operatorname{Log} z$ is not analytic at any point of $(-\infty, 0]$.

- At $z = 0$ it is undefined.
- On the negative real axis $\operatorname{Arg} z$ jumps by $2\pi$, so $\operatorname{Log} z$ is not continuous there.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Write $\operatorname{Log} z = u + iv$ with $u = \ln r$ and $v = \theta$, where $-\pi < \theta < \pi$
- Compute $u_r = \dfrac{1}{r}$, $v_\theta = 1$, $u_\theta = 0$, $v_r = 0$
- Verify the polar Cauchy-Riemann equations $u_r = \dfrac{1}{r}v_\theta$ and $v_r = -\dfrac{1}{r}u_\theta$
- Conclude analyticity from the continuity of the partials
- Use $f'(z) = e^{-i\theta}(u_r + iv_r) = \dfrac{1}{z}$

</Note>

The negative real axis is the branch cut of $\operatorname{Log} z$. Any single-valued branch of $\log z$ is analytic on the plane minus a ray from $0$.

$0$ is a branch point. No branch is analytic on a neighbourhood of $0$ with only $0$ removed, since going once around $0$ changes $\arg z$ by $2\pi$.

## Properties

Let $z_1$ and $z_2$ be non-zero.

- $\log(z_1 z_2) = \log z_1 + \log z_2$
- $\log\dfrac{z_1}{z_2} = \log z_1 - \log z_2$
- $\log\dfrac{1}{z} = -\log z$

<Note>

The identities hold for the multi-valued $\log$ as equalities of sets. They can fail for $\operatorname{Log}$.

$\operatorname{Log}((-1)(-1)) = \operatorname{Log}(1) = 0$, but $\operatorname{Log}(-1) + \operatorname{Log}(-1) = i\pi + i\pi = 2\pi i$.

</Note>

## Examples

### $\log(1+i)$

- $|1+i| = \sqrt{2}$
- $\operatorname{Arg}(1+i) = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$

$$
\log(1+i) = \dfrac{1}{2}\ln 2 + i\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi\right), \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Log}(1+i) = \dfrac{1}{2}\ln 2 + i\dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

### $\log i$

- $|i| = 1$
- $\operatorname{Arg}(i) = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

$$
\log i = i\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi\right), \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Log} i = i\dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

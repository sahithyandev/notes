The contour integral of $f$ over a [contour](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour) $C$ is

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = \lim_{\|P\| \to 0} \sum_{k=1}^n f(c_k)\Delta z_k
$$

provided the limit exists and is independent of the choice of partition and points $c_k$.

## Riemann Sum

Let $C$ be a contour with initial point $A$ and terminal point $B$.

A partition of $C$ is a finite set of points $P = \{z_0, z_1, \ldots, z_n\}$, occurring in order along $C$, with $z_0 = A$ and $z_n = B$.

$$
\Delta z_k = z_k - z_{k-1}, \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots, n
$$

$c_k$ is any point on the part of $C$ between $z_{k-1}$ and $z_k$.

The Riemann sum for this partition, for a function $f$ is

$$
\sum_{k=1}^n f(c_k)\Delta z_k
$$

$\|P\|$ is the maximum length of the subarcs determined by $P$.

## Evaluation via Parametrization

Let $f(z)$ be continuous on a set containing the contour $C$, and let $z = z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$, be a piecewise smooth [parametrization](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour#parametrization) of $C$.

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = \int_a^b f(z(t))z'(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

When $z(t)$ is only piecewise smooth, the right side is evaluated piecewise.

<Note>

The value of $\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z$ depends on the path $C$, not just the endpoints, unless $f$ is analytic on a simply connected domain containing $C$.

</Note>

## Properties

### Piecewise Contours

If $C$ is made of smooth curves $C_1, C_2, \ldots, C_n$, the integral is the sum over the pieces.

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = \sum_{k=1}^n \int_{C_k} f(z)\,\text{d}z
$$

### Reversed Orientation

$$
\int_{-C} f(z)\,\text{d}z = -\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z
$$

## Integral Triangle Inequality

Let $f(t) = u(t) + iv(t)$ be continuous on $[a,b]$.

$$
\left|\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t\right| \le \int_a^b |f(t)|\,\text{d}t
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Write $\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t = re^{i\theta}$ for some $r \ge 0$
- Then $r = e^{-i\theta}\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t = \int_a^b e^{-i\theta}f(t)\,\text{d}t$
- $r$ is real, so it equals the real part of the last integral
- $\text{Re}(e^{-i\theta}f(t)) \le |e^{-i\theta}f(t)| = |f(t)|$ for every $t$
- Integrate this bound over $[a,b]$ to get $r \le \int_a^b |f(t)|\,\text{d}t$

</Note>

## ML Inequality

Let $f$ be continuous on a contour $C$, with $|f(z)| \le M$ for all $z$ on $C$, and let $L$ be the length of $C$.

$$
\left|\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z\right| \le ML
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Parametrize $C$ by $z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$
- Apply the integral triangle inequality to $\int_a^b f(z(t))z'(t)\,\text{d}t$
- Bound $|f(z(t))|$ by $M$
- Recognize $\int_a^b |z'(t)|\,\text{d}t$ as $L$

</Note>

To bound $|f(z)|$ from above, bound the numerator from above and the denominator from below using the reverse triangle inequality.

$$
|a+b| \ge \big||a|-|b|\big|
$$

## Cauchy-Goursat Theorem

Let $f$ be analytic in a [simply connected](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/connectedness#simply-connected-region) domain $D$. If $C$ is a [simple closed](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour#simple-closed-contour) contour lying in $D$,

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = 0
$$

## Deformation Invariance Theorem

Let $C_1$ and $C_2$ be 2 [positively oriented](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour#positive-orientation) [simple closed](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/contour#simple-closed-contour) contours, with $C_2$ lying inside the interior of $C_1$. Let $f$ be analytic in a domain $D$ that contains both $C_1$ and $C_2$, together with the region between them.

$$
\int_{C_1} f(z)\,\text{d}z = \int_{C_2} f(z)\,\text{d}z
$$

<DeformationInvarianceDiagram />

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Split the region between $C_1$ and $C_2$ so its boundary is $C_1$ together with $C_2$ reversed
- Apply the Cauchy-Goursat theorem to the whole boundary to get $0$
- Rearrange

</Note>

The integral over a closed contour depends only on which singularities the contour encloses, not on its shape.

<Note>

The theorem lets $C_1$ be replaced by any simpler contour $C_2$ enclosing the same singularities of $f$, without changing the value of the integral.

</Note>

To evaluate $\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z$, replace $C$ by a small circle $|z-a| = \varepsilon$ around each singular point $a$ inside $C$.

### Corollary of DIT

Let $z_0$ be a fixed complex number, and let $C$ be a simple closed contour with positive orientation such that $z_0$ lies inside $C$.

$$
\int_C \frac{1}{z - z_0}\,\text{d}z = 2\pi i
$$

$$
\int_C \frac{1}{(z-z_0)^n}\,\text{d}z = 0, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}, n > 1
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Deform $C$ to a circle of radius $r$ centered at $z_0$, by the deformation invariance theorem
- Parametrize the circle as $z = z_0 + re^{i\theta}$, $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$
- Substitute and simplify the integrand
- Integrate directly over $\theta$

</Note>

For $n \ge 2$, $\dfrac{-1}{(n-1)(z-z_0)^{n-1}}$ is a single-valued antiderivative on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_0\}$. Every closed integral is $0$.

For $n=1$ the antiderivative is $\ln(z-z_0)$. Its argument increases by $2\pi$ per loop around $z_0$. This jump gives $2\pi i$.

### When to Use DIT

Consider deformation invariance when all of these hold:

- $C$ is a closed contour
- The integrand is analytic on and inside $C$ except at finitely many points
- Those exceptional points lie inside $C$

### Singularities Inside and Outside

Split the integrand by partial fractions into terms with 1 singular point each.

| Term                           | Singular point $a$ | Value of $\int_C$ |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------ | ----------------- |
| $\dfrac{c}{z-a}$               | outside $C$        | $0$               |
| $\dfrac{c}{z-a}$               | inside $C$         | $2\pi i\,c$       |
| $\dfrac{c}{(z-a)^n},\ n \ge 2$ | inside $C$         | $0$               |

A singular point on $C$ is not covered and needs separate treatment.

Singularities outside $C$ never obstruct the deformation, since shrinking $C$ only sweeps its interior.

$$
\int_C f(z)\,\text{d}z = 2\pi i \sum c_a
$$

Here:

- $c_a$: coefficient of $\dfrac{1}{z-a}$, summed over the singular points $a$ inside $C$

### Logarithm Shortcut

$\displaystyle\int_C \frac{g'(z)}{g(z)}\,\text{d}z$ cannot be evaluated directly as $\ln g$ at the endpoints.

The shortcut $\int_C F'\,\text{d}z = F(\text{end}) - F(\text{start})$ needs $F$ single-valued and analytic on a domain containing $C$. Here $\ln g = \ln|g| + i\arg g$, and $\arg g$ is multivalued when $g$ has zeros inside $C$.

A valid but laborious version uses the change in argument. The $\ln|g|$ part cancels on a closed curve.

$$
\int_C \frac{g'(z)}{g(z)}\,\text{d}z = i\,\Delta_C \arg g
$$

Partial fractions with deformation is the preferred route.

## Examples

### Line Integral of $z$ from $0$ to $1+i$

Let $C$ be the line segment from $0$ to $1+i$, parametrized by $z(t) = t(1+i)$, $0 \le t \le 1$. $z'(t) = 1+i$.

$$
\int_C z\,\text{d}z = \int_0^1 t(1+i)(1+i)\,\text{d}t = (1+i)^2\int_0^1 t\,\text{d}t
$$

$(1+i)^2 = 2i$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^1 t\,\text{d}t = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$$
\int_C z\,\text{d}z = 2i \cdot \frac{1}{2} = i
$$

### ML Bound on a Large Circle

Bound $\displaystyle\int_C \frac{z^2}{(z^2+9)^2}\,\text{d}z$ for $C: |z| = R > 3$.

$L = 2\pi R$. On $C$, $|z^2| = R^2$ and $|z^2+9| \ge R^2 - 9 > 0$.

$$
|f(z)| \le \frac{R^2}{(R^2-9)^2} = M
$$

$$
\left|\int_C \frac{z^2}{(z^2+9)^2}\,\text{d}z\right| \le \frac{2\pi R^3}{(R^2-9)^2}
$$

The bound behaves like $\dfrac{2\pi}{R}$, so it tends to $0$ as $R \to \infty$.

### Integral Using Partial Fractions

Show $\displaystyle\int_C \frac{2z}{z^2+2}\,\text{d}z = 4\pi i$ for $C: |z|=2$, positively oriented.

$z^2+2 = (z-i\sqrt{2})(z+i\sqrt{2})$ and $|\pm i\sqrt{2}| = \sqrt{2} < 2$, so both poles lie inside $C$.

$$
\frac{2z}{z^2+2} = \frac{1}{z-i\sqrt{2}} + \frac{1}{z+i\sqrt{2}}
$$

Each term integrates to $2\pi i$ by the key integral.

$$
\int_C \frac{2z}{z^2+2}\,\text{d}z = 2\pi i + 2\pi i = 4\pi i
$$

Using the argument change instead, $g(2e^{i\theta}) = 4e^{2i\theta}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-2i\theta}\right)$. The first factor changes its argument by $4\pi$ and the second by $0$, so the integral is $i \cdot 4\pi = 4\pi i$.

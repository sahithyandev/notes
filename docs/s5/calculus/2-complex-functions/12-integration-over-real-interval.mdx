Let $f(t) = u(t) + iv(t)$ for $a \le t \le b$, where $u$ and $v$ are real-valued continuous functions of the real variable $t$.

$$
\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t = \int_a^b u(t)\,\text{d}t + i\int_a^b v(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

If $U'(t) = u(t)$ and $V'(t) = v(t)$ for each $a \le t \le b$,

$$
\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t = (U(b) - U(a)) + i(V(b) - V(a))
$$

## Properties

Let $f(t) = u(t) + iv(t)$ and $g(t) = p(t) + iq(t)$ be continuous on $[a,b]$.

### Sum

$$
\int_a^b (f(t) + g(t))\,\text{d}t = \int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t + \int_a^b g(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Write $f(t) + g(t) = (u+p) + i(v+q)$
- Apply linearity of the real integral to $u+p$ and to $v+q$ separately
- Recombine the 2 real integrals into $\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t + \int_a^b g(t)\,\text{d}t$ using the definition of $\int f\,\text{d}t$

</Note>

### Additivity over Subintervals

$$
\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t = \int_a^c f(t)\,\text{d}t + \int_c^b f(t)\,\text{d}t, \qquad a \le c \le b
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Apply the additivity of the real integral to $u$ over $[a,c]$ and $[c,b]$, and likewise for $v$
- Recombine the resulting real and imaginary parts using the definition of $\int f\,\text{d}t$

</Note>

### Homogeneity

Let $\alpha = r + is$ be a complex constant.

$$
\int_a^b \alpha f(t)\,\text{d}t = \alpha \int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Expand $\alpha f(t) = (ru - sv) + i(rv + su)$
- Apply linearity of the real integral to $ru - sv$ and to $rv + su$
- Expand $\alpha\left(\int_a^b u\,\text{d}t + i\int_a^b v\,\text{d}t\right)$ and match the real and imaginary parts to the previous step

</Note>

### Reversal of Limits

$$
\int_a^b f(t)\,\text{d}t = -\int_b^a f(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Apply the corresponding reversal property of the real integral to $u$ and to $v$
- Recombine using the definition of $\int f\,\text{d}t$

</Note>

### Fundamental Theorem

Let $f$ be continuously differentiable on $[a,b]$.

$$
\int_a^b f'(t)\,\text{d}t = f(b) - f(a)
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Write $f'(t) = u'(t) + iv'(t)$
- Apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to $u'$ and to $v'$ separately: $\int_a^b u' = u(b) - u(a)$, $\int_a^b v' = v(b) - v(a)$
- Combine into $(u(b) + iv(b)) - (u(a) + iv(a)) = f(b) - f(a)$

</Note>

### Product

$$
\int_a^b f(t)g(t)\,\text{d}t = \int_a^b (u p - v q)\,\text{d}t + i\int_a^b (u q + v p)\,\text{d}t
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Expand $f(t)g(t) = (u+iv)(p+iq) = (up - vq) + i(uq + vp)$
- Apply the definition of $\int f\,\text{d}t$ to this real and imaginary decomposition

</Note>

## Worked Example

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (1 + it)^2\,\text{d}t$.

Expand the integrand.

$$
(1 + it)^2 = 1 + 2it + i^2 t^2 = (1 - t^2) + i(2t)
$$

So $u(t) = 1 - t^2$ and $v(t) = 2t$.

Integrate the real part.

$$
\int_0^1 (1 - t^2)\,\text{d}t = \left[t - \frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

Integrate the imaginary part.

$$
\int_0^1 2t\,\text{d}t = \left[t^2\right]_0^1 = 1
$$

Combine using the definition.

$$
\int_0^1 (1 + it)^2\,\text{d}t = \frac{2}{3} + i
$$

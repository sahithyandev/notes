A point $z_0 \in \mathbb{C}$ is a singular point, or a singularity, of $f$ **if** $f$ fails to be analytic at $z_0$ **and** $f$ is analytic at some point in every neighborhood of $z_0$.

A singular point $z_0$ of $f$ is an isolated singularity **iff** $f$ is analytic in some punctured disk $D_r(z_0) \setminus \{z_0\}$. Otherwise it is a non-isolated singularity.

Examples:

- $f(z) = \frac{1}{1-z}$ has an isolated singularity at $z = 1$

## Classification

Let $\alpha$ be an isolated singularity of $f$ with the [Laurent series](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/laurent-series)

$$
f(z) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k (z-\alpha)^k, \quad 0 < |z-\alpha| < R
$$

Then $\alpha$ is

- Removable singularity  
  **iff** $a_k = 0$ for all $k < 0$.
- Pole of order $n$  
  **iff** $a_{-n} \neq 0$ and $a_k = 0$ for all $k < -n$.
- Essential singularity  
  **iff** $a_k \neq 0$ for infinitely many $k < 0$.

A pole of order 1 is called a simple pole.

Examples:

- $\frac{\sin z}{z}$ and $\frac{\cos z - 1}{z^2}$ have removable singularities at $z = 0$
- $\frac{\sin z}{z^3}$ has a pole of order 2 at $z = 0$
- $\frac{e^z}{z}$ has a simple pole at $z = 0$
- $z^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)$ has an essential singularity at $z = 0$

## Residue

The coefficient $a_{-1}$ is called the residue of $f$ at $\alpha$.

$$
\text{Res}(f, \alpha) = a_{-1}
$$

## Worked Examples

### Isolated Singularity

Show that $z = 1$ is an isolated singularity of $f(z) = \frac{1}{1-z}$.

$f$ is analytic everywhere except at $z = 1$, so it is analytic in the punctured disk $D_1(1) \setminus \{1\}$. Every neighborhood of $1$ contains points where $f$ is analytic, so $z = 1$ is a singular point.

The Laurent series centered at $1$ is $f(z) = -\frac{1}{z-1}$. Only $a_{-1} = -1$ is non-zero, so $z = 1$ is a simple pole with $\text{Res}(f, 1) = -1$.

### Non-Isolated Singularity

Show that $z = 0$ is a non-isolated singularity of $f(z) = \frac{1}{\sin(1/z)}$.

$f$ is not analytic at $z_k = \frac{1}{k\pi}$ for each non-zero integer $k$, since $\sin(1/z_k) = 0$.

$z_k \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$, so every punctured disk centered at $0$ contains a singular point. $f$ is not analytic in any punctured disk centered at $0$, so $z = 0$ is a non-isolated singularity.

### Removable Singularity

Show that $\frac{\sin z}{z}$ and $\frac{\cos z - 1}{z^2}$ have removable singularities at $z = 0$.

$$
\frac{\sin z}{z} = 1 - \frac{z^2}{6} + \frac{z^4}{120} - \cdots
$$

$$
\frac{\cos z - 1}{z^2} = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{z^2}{24} - \cdots
$$

Both series have $a_k = 0$ for all $k < 0$.

### Pole

Show that $\frac{\sin z}{z^3}$ has a pole of order 2 and $\frac{e^z}{z}$ has a pole of order 1 at $z = 0$.

$$
\frac{\sin z}{z^3} = \frac{1}{z^2} - \frac{1}{6} + \frac{z^2}{120} - \cdots
$$

$a_{-2} = 1 \neq 0$ and $a_k = 0$ for all $k < -2$. The residue is $a_{-1} = 0$.

$$
\frac{e^z}{z} = \frac{1}{z} + 1 + \frac{z}{2} + \cdots
$$

$a_{-1} = 1 \neq 0$ and $a_k = 0$ for all $k < -1$. The residue is $1$.

### Essential Singularity

Show that $z^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)$ has an essential singularity at $z = 0$.

$$
z^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{z}\right) = z^2 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)!\, z^{2n+1}} = z - \frac{1}{6z} + \frac{1}{120z^3} - \cdots
$$

$a_k \neq 0$ for $k = -1, -3, -5, \ldots$, which is infinitely many $k < 0$. The residue is $-\frac{1}{6}$.

### Classify from the Series

Classify the singularity of $\frac{e^z - 1 - z}{z^3}$ at $z = 0$ and find the residue.

$$
\frac{e^z - 1 - z}{z^3} = \frac{\frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6} + \frac{z^4}{24} + \cdots}{z^3} = \frac{1}{2z} + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{z}{24} + \cdots
$$

$z = 0$ is a simple pole with $\text{Res} = \frac{1}{2}$.

Classify the singularity of $z^3 \cos\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)$ at $z = 0$ and find the residue.

$$
z^3 \cos\left(\frac{1}{z}\right) = z^3 - \frac{z}{2} + \frac{1}{24z} - \frac{1}{720z^3} + \cdots
$$

$z = 0$ is an essential singularity with $\text{Res} = \frac{1}{24}$.

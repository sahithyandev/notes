The definition and basic properties of complex derivatives are covered in the [S1 differentiability note](/s1/mathematics/complex-analysis/differentiability).

## Differentiation Formulas

Let $c \in \mathbb{C}$, and let $f$, $g$ be functions differentiable at $z \in \mathbb{C}$. Then:

- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,c = 0$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,(cf(z)) = cf'(z)$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,z^n = nz^{n-1}$, for positive integer $n$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,(f(z)+g(z)) = f'(z)+g'(z)$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,(f(z)g(z)) = f(z)g'(z)+f'(z)g(z)$
- $\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,\dfrac{f(z)}{g(z)} = \dfrac{g(z)f'(z)-f(z)g'(z)}{(g(z))^2}$, provided $g(z) \ne 0$

## Polar Cauchy-Riemann Equations

### Polar Form

Suppose $f(z) = u(r,\theta)+iv(r,\theta)$ be defined throughout a neighborhood of a non-zero point $z_0 = r_0e^{i\theta_0}$.

The polar form of the Cauchy-Riemann equations is

$$
ru_r = v_\theta, \qquad u_\theta = -rv_r
$$

at $(r_0,\theta_0)$.

### Jacobian

The [Jacobian matrix](/s2/methods-of-mathematics/multivariable-calculus/jacobian#jacobian-matrix) of $(x,y) \mapsto (u,v)$ is then

$$
\begin{pmatrix} u_x & -v_x \\ v_x & u_x \end{pmatrix}
$$

which acts as multiplication by $u_x + iv_x$. A complex-differentiable map is locally a rotation plus a uniform scaling.

Examples:

- $f(z) = z^2$  
  $u_x = v_y = 2x$, $u_y = -v_x = -2y$, and $f'(z) = 2x + i2y = 2z$.
- $f(z) = \bar{z}$  
  $u_x = 1$, $v_y = -1$, so the equations fail everywhere and $\bar{z}$ is nowhere differentiable.

Under the Cartesian sufficient conditions stated in the [S1 Cauchy–Riemann equations note](/s1/mathematics/complex-analysis/cauchy-riemann-equations), the derivative also has the polar form

$$
f'(z_0) = e^{-i\theta_0}(u_r(r_0,\theta_0)+iv_r(r_0,\theta_0))
$$

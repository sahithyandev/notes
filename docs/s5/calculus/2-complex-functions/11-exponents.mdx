Let $c \in \mathbb{C}$ and $z \ne 0$.

$$
z^c = e^{c\log z}
$$

$z^c$ is multi-valued, since $\log z$ is multi-valued.

## Principal Value

Obtained by using $\operatorname{Log} z$ in place of $\log z$.

$$
z^c = e^{c\operatorname{Log} z}
$$

## Analyticity

The principal value $z^c = e^{c\operatorname{Log} z}$ is analytic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus (-\infty, 0]$ and

$$
\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}\,z^c = cz^{c-1}
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Note that $\operatorname{Log} z$ is [analytic](/s5/calculus/complex-functions/logarithm#analyticity) on $\mathbb{C} \setminus (-\infty, 0]$
- Compose it with $w \mapsto e^{cw}$, which is entire
- Apply the chain rule to get $e^{c\operatorname{Log} z} \cdot \dfrac{c}{z}$
- Use $\dfrac{e^{c\operatorname{Log} z}}{z} = e^{(c-1)\operatorname{Log} z}$

</Note>

The domain is larger for some values of $c$.

- $c$ is a non-negative integer  
  $z^c$ is single-valued and analytic on $\mathbb{C}$.
- $c$ is a negative integer  
  $z^c$ is single-valued and analytic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$.
- $c$ is any other value  
  $z^c$ is multi-valued. The negative real axis is the branch cut and $0$ is a branch point.

## Worked Examples

### $\sqrt{1+i}$

$$
\operatorname{Log}(1+i) = \frac{1}{2}\ln 2 + i\frac{\pi}{4}
$$

$$
(1+i)^{1/2} = e^{\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Log}(1+i)} = e^{\frac{1}{4}\ln 2 + i\frac{\pi}{8}} = 2^{1/4}\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{8} + i\sin\frac{\pi}{8}\right)
$$

Numerically:

$$
(1+i)^{1/2} \approx 1.0987 + 0.4551i
$$

### $i^i$

$$
\operatorname{Log} i = i\frac{\pi}{2}
$$

$$
i^i = e^{i\operatorname{Log} i} = e^{i \cdot i\pi/2} = e^{-\pi/2} \approx 0.2079
$$

$i^i$ is real for every branch of $\log i$, since $\log i = i(\pi/2 + 2k\pi)$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ gives $i^i = e^{-(\pi/2+2k\pi)}$.

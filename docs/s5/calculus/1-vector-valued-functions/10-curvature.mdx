Let $C$ be a graph of a $C^1$ vector-valued function $\boldsymbol{r}$, parametrized by arc length $s$, with $T'(s)$ existing. The curvature of $C$ at $s$ is

$$
\kappa(s) = \left\lVert \frac{\text{d}T}{\text{d}s} \right\rVert
$$

Since $T(s) = \boldsymbol{r}'(s)$ for an arc-length parametrization, if $\boldsymbol{r}''(s)$ exists,

$$
\kappa(s) = \lVert \boldsymbol{r}''(s) \rVert
$$

<Note>

A vector-valued function is $C^1$ on an interval **iff** its derivative exists and is continuous on that interval.

</Note>

If $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ is twice differentiable and $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \ne \vec{0}$, the curvature of $C$ is

$$
\kappa(t) = \frac{\lVert T'(t) \rVert}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\kappa(t) = \frac{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) \rVert}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert^3}
$$

Examples:

- The curvature of a straight line is $0$.
- The curvature of a circle with radius $a > 0$ is $\frac{1}{a}$.

## Osculating Circle

If a curve $C$ in two-dimensional space has non-zero curvature $\kappa$ at a point $P$, the circle of radius

$$
\rho = \frac{1}{\kappa}
$$

that shares a common tangent with $C$ at $P$ and is centered on the concave side of the curve at $P$ is the osculating circle, or circle of curvature, at $P$.

### Center of Curvature

The center of curvature of $C$ at a point $P = \boldsymbol{r}(s)$ with $\kappa(s) \ne 0$ is the center of the osculating circle at $P$:

$$
\boldsymbol{r}(s) + \rho\, N(s)
$$

Since $N(s)$ points toward the concave side of $C$, the center of curvature lies at distance $\rho$ from $P$ on that side.

## Worked Examples

### Helix

Find the curvature and radius of curvature of $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \cos t\,\boldsymbol{i} + \sin t\,\boldsymbol{j} + t\,\boldsymbol{k}$.

Differentiate twice.

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t) = -\sin t\,\boldsymbol{i} + \cos t\,\boldsymbol{j} + \boldsymbol{k}, \quad \boldsymbol{r}''(t) = -\cos t\,\boldsymbol{i} - \sin t\,\boldsymbol{j}
$$

Compute the cross product.

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) = \sin t\,\boldsymbol{i} - \cos t\,\boldsymbol{j} + \boldsymbol{k}
$$

Compute the norms.

$$
\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) \rVert = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + 1} = \sqrt{2}, \quad \lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert^3 = (\sqrt{2})^3 = 2\sqrt{2}
$$

Apply the formula.

$$
\kappa(t) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

The radius of curvature is $\rho = 2$ at every point.

### Parabola

Find the curvature of $y = x^2$, and the osculating circle at the origin.

Parametrize as $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = t\,\boldsymbol{i} + t^2\,\boldsymbol{j}$ and differentiate twice.

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t) = \boldsymbol{i} + 2t\,\boldsymbol{j}, \quad \boldsymbol{r}''(t) = 2\,\boldsymbol{j}
$$

Compute the cross product.

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) = 2\,\boldsymbol{k}
$$

Apply the formula.

$$
\kappa(t) = \frac{2}{(1 + 4t^2)^{3/2}}
$$

At $t = 0$, $\kappa = 2$ and $\rho = \frac{1}{2}$.

The tangent at the origin is $\boldsymbol{i}$, so $N(0) = \boldsymbol{j}$ points toward the concave side. The center of curvature is

$$
\boldsymbol{r}(0) + \rho\, N(0) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\boldsymbol{j}
$$

The osculating circle is $x^2 + \left(y - \frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}$.

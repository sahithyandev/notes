A coordinate system assigns an ordered triple of numbers to each point in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Cylindrical and spherical coordinates use distances and angles instead of 3 signed distances along the axes. They simplify regions and integrands that have an axis or a center of symmetry.

## Rectangular Coordinates

Rectangular coordinates, also called Cartesian coordinates, locate a point $P$ by 3 signed distances from 3 mutually perpendicular coordinate planes.

$$
P = (x, y, z)
$$

Here:

- $x$: signed distance from the $yz$-plane
- $y$: signed distance from the $zx$-plane
- $z$: signed distance from the $xy$-plane

## Cylindrical Coordinates

Cylindrical coordinates extend polar coordinates in the $xy$-plane with the height $z$.

$$
x = r\cos\theta, \quad y = r\sin\theta, \quad z = z, \quad r \ge 0
$$

Here:

- $r$: distance from the $z$-axis
- $\theta$: angle in the $xy$-plane from the positive $x$-axis
- $z$: signed height above the $xy$-plane

## Spherical Coordinates

Spherical coordinates are defined by

$$
x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta, \quad y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta, \quad z = \rho\cos\phi
$$

Here:

- $\rho$: distance from the origin to the point
- $\theta$: angle in the $xy$-plane from the positive $x$-axis
- $\phi$: angle from the positive $z$-axis

Standard ranges are $\rho \ge 0$, $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$, $0 \le \phi \le \pi$.

### Common Spherical Regions

- Spherical shell between $\rho = \rho_1$ and $\rho = \rho_2$, $\rho_1 < \rho_2$  
  $\rho_1 \le \rho \le \rho_2$, $0 \le \phi \le \pi$, $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$
- Sphere $\rho = \rho_0$ restricted to the first octant  
  $0 \le \rho \le \rho_0$, $0 \le \phi \le \frac{\pi}{2}$, $0 \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2}$
- Sphere $\rho = \rho_0$ cut by the cone $\phi = \phi_0$  
  $0 \le \rho \le \rho_0$, $0 \le \phi \le \phi_0$, $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$
- Sphere $\rho = \rho_0$ cut by 2 cones $\phi = \phi_1$ and $\phi = \phi_2$, $\phi_1 < \phi_2$  
  $0 \le \rho \le \rho_0$, $\phi_1 \le \phi \le \phi_2$, $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$
- Solid enclosed laterally by the cone $\phi = \phi_0$, $0 < \phi_0 < \frac{\pi}{2}$, and above by the plane $z = a$, $a > 0$  
  $0 \le \rho \le a\sec\phi$, $0 \le \phi \le \phi_0$, $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$

Let $C$ be a smooth curve with parametric equations $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$, $z = z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$, and $f$ defined on $C$. Let $P = \{t_0, t_1, \dots, t_n\}$, $a = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = b$, be a partition of $[a,b]$, dividing $C$ into $n$ subarcs. Let $\Delta s_k$ be the length of the $k$th subarc, and $(x_k^*, y_k^*, z_k^*)$ a sample point on the $k$th subarc. The line integral of $f$ over $C$ with respect to arc length is

$$
\int_C f(x,y,z)\,\text{d}s = \lim_{\max\{\Delta s_k\} \to 0} \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(x_k^*, y_k^*, z_k^*)\,\Delta s_k
$$

provided the limit exists and does not depend on the choice of partition or sample points.

<Note>

The line integral exists if $f$ is continuous at each point of $C$.

</Note>

For $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j} + z(t)\hat{k}$, $a \le t \le b$,

$$
\text{d}s
= \lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert\,\text{d}t
= \sqrt{\left(\frac{\text{d}x}{\text{d}t}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}t}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\text{d}z}{\text{d}t}\right)^2}\,\text{d}t
$$

so

$$
\int_C f(x,y,z)\,\text{d}s = \int_a^b f(x(t),y(t),z(t))\,\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert\,\text{d}t
$$

<Note>

If $C$ lies in the $xy$-plane and $z = f(x,y)$ is a non-negative continuous function on $C$, $\int_C f(x,y)\,\text{d}s$ is the area of the vertical sheet swept out by a line segment of height $f(x,y)$ as $(x,y)$ moves along $C$.

</Note>

## Line Integral of a Vector Field

Let $\boldsymbol{F}$ be a [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/field) and $C$ a piecewise smooth oriented curve parametrized by $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j} + z(t)\hat{k}$, $a \le t \le b$. The line integral of $\boldsymbol{F}$ along $C$ is

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \int_a^b \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{r}(t)) \cdot \boldsymbol{r}'(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

For $\boldsymbol{F} = P\hat{i} + Q\hat{j} + R\hat{k}$,

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \int_C P\,\text{d}x + Q\,\text{d}y + R\,\text{d}z
$$

To evaluate the above integral when $C$ is given by $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$, $z = z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$:

1. Differentiate the parametric equations to get $\text{d}x = x'(t)\,\text{d}t$, $\text{d}y = y'(t)\,\text{d}t$, $\text{d}z = z'(t)\,\text{d}t$.
2. Replace $x$, $y$, $z$, $\text{d}x$, $\text{d}y$, $\text{d}z$ in the integrand, which leaves an integral in $t$ only.
3. Integrate from $t = a$ to $t = b$.

$$
\int_C P\,\text{d}x + Q\,\text{d}y + R\,\text{d}z = \int_a^b \left(P\,x'(t) + Q\,y'(t) + R\,z'(t)\right)\text{d}t
$$

### Work

For a particle moving along a smooth curve $C$, oriented in the direction of motion, under a continuous [force field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/field#vector-field) $\boldsymbol{F}$, the work done by $\boldsymbol{F}$ on the particle is

$$
W = \int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r}
$$

## Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals

Suppose $C$ be a piecewise smooth curve parametrized by $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$, $a \le t \le b$.

If $\boldsymbol{F}$ is a [conservative field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/conservative-field) with potential function $\phi$ on $C$, and

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \int_C \nabla \phi \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \phi(\boldsymbol{r}(b)) - \phi(\boldsymbol{r}(a))
$$

For a [conservative field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/conservative-field), the work depends only on the end points of $C$.

### Integral over a Closed Curve

Let $\boldsymbol{F}$ be conservative on $D$ and $C$ a piecewise smooth closed curve in $D$. Then

$$
\oint_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = 0
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Apply the fundamental theorem of line integrals
- Use $\boldsymbol{r}(b) = \boldsymbol{r}(a)$ for a closed curve
- Subtract equal values of $\phi$

</Note>

### Equivalence of Conservative Fields

Let $\boldsymbol{F}(x,y,z) = f\hat{i} + g\hat{j} + h\hat{k}$ be continuous on an open connected region $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$. The following are equivalent:

- $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative on $D$
- $\displaystyle\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = 0$ for every piecewise smooth closed curve $C$ in $D$
- For any 2 points $P$, $Q$ in $D$, $\displaystyle\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r}$ is independent of the piecewise smooth curve $C$ in $D$ from $P$ to $Q$

## Worked Examples

### Line Integral with Respect to Arc Length

Evaluate the following integral, where $C$ is the quarter circle $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \cos t\,\hat{i} + \sin t\,\hat{j}$, $0 \le t \le \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

$$
\int_C xy\,\text{d}s
$$

<Solution>

Speed:

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t) = -\sin t\,\hat{i} + \cos t\,\hat{j}, \quad \lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t} = 1
$$

Integral:

$$
\int_C xy\,\text{d}s = \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos t \sin t \cdot 1\,\text{d}t = \left[\frac{\sin^2 t}{2}\right]_0^{\pi/2} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

</Solution>

### Integral Along a Parabola

Evaluate the following integral for $\boldsymbol{F} = x^2\,\hat{i} + xy\,\hat{j}$, where $C$ is $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = t\,\hat{i} + t^2\,\hat{j}$, $0 \le t \le 1$.

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r}
$$

<Solution>

Field along the curve:

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{r}(t)) = t^2\,\hat{i} + t^3\,\hat{j}, \quad \boldsymbol{r}'(t) = \hat{i} + 2t\,\hat{j}
$$

Dot product:

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{r}(t)) \cdot \boldsymbol{r}'(t) = t^2 + 2t^4
$$

Integral:

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \int_0^1 (t^2 + 2t^4)\,\text{d}t = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{11}{15}
$$

</Solution>

### Integral in Differential Form

Evaluate $\int_C (y\,\text{d}x - z\,\text{d}y + x\,\text{d}z)$, where $C$ is $x = t^2$, $y = e^{-t}$, $z = e^{t}$, $0 \le t \le 1$.

<Solution>

Differentials:

$$
\text{d}x = 2t\,\text{d}t, \quad \text{d}y = -e^{-t}\,\text{d}t, \quad \text{d}z = e^{t}\,\text{d}t
$$

Each term in terms of $t$:

$$
y\,\text{d}x = 2t e^{-t}\,\text{d}t, \quad -z\,\text{d}y = e^{t} e^{-t}\,\text{d}t = \text{d}t, \quad x\,\text{d}z = t^2 e^{t}\,\text{d}t
$$

Integral:

$$
\int_C (y\,\text{d}x - z\,\text{d}y + x\,\text{d}z) = \int_0^1 \left(2t e^{-t} + 1 + t^2 e^{t}\right)\text{d}t
$$

Integrating by parts for the 1st and 3rd terms:

$$
\int_0^1 2t e^{-t}\,\text{d}t = \left[-2(t + 1)e^{-t}\right]_0^1 = 2 - \frac{4}{e}, \quad \int_0^1 t^2 e^{t}\,\text{d}t = \left[(t^2 - 2t + 2)e^{t}\right]_0^1 = e - 2
$$

Sum:

$$
\int_C (y\,\text{d}x - z\,\text{d}y + x\,\text{d}z) = \left(2 - \frac{4}{e}\right) + 1 + (e - 2) = 1 + e - \frac{4}{e}
$$

</Solution>

### Work Along a Circle

Find the work done by $\boldsymbol{F} = -y\,\hat{i} + x\,\hat{j}$ on a particle moving once counterclockwise around the unit circle $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \cos t\,\hat{i} + \sin t\,\hat{j}$, $0 \le t \le 2\pi$.

<Solution>

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{r}(t)) = -\sin t\,\hat{i} + \cos t\,\hat{j}, \quad \boldsymbol{r}'(t) = -\sin t\,\hat{i} + \cos t\,\hat{j}
$$

$$
W = \int_0^{2\pi} (\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t)\,\text{d}t = \int_0^{2\pi} 1\,\text{d}t = 2\pi
$$

</Solution>

### Path-Independent Integral

Evaluate $\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r}$ for $\boldsymbol{F} = yz\,\hat{i} + xz\,\hat{j} + xy\,\hat{k}$, where $C$ is any piecewise smooth curve from $(1,1,1)$ to $(2,3,4)$.

<Solution>

Potential function:

$$
\phi = xyz, \quad \nabla \phi = yz\,\hat{i} + xz\,\hat{j} + xy\,\hat{k} = \boldsymbol{F}
$$

Integral:

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \phi(2,3,4) - \phi(1,1,1) = 24 - 1 = 23
$$

</Solution>

<Aka>vector function</Aka>. A function whose domain is a set of real numbers and
whose range is a set of vectors.

$$
\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t), z(t) \rangle = x(t)\boldsymbol{i} + y(t)\boldsymbol{j} + z(t)\boldsymbol{k}
$$

As $t$ varies, $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ is positioned with its initial point at the origin, and its terminal point traces out $C$. In this case $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ is the radius vector, or position vector, for $C$.

### Component Function

$x(t)$, $y(t)$, and $z(t)$ are the component functions of $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$.

### Domain

The domain of $\boldsymbol{r}$ is the set of all $t$ for which $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ is defined.

## Space Curve

A vector-valued function $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ whose values are three-dimensional vectors traces out a space curve in three-dimensional space.

Examples:

- $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t, 0 \rangle$ traces out the unit circle in the $xy$-plane centered at the origin.
- $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \langle t, t^2, t^3 \rangle$ traces out the twisted cubic.

## Parametric Curve

A particle moving along a curve $C$ in three-dimensional space has $x$, $y$, and $z$ coordinates as functions of time $t$:

$$
x = f(t), \qquad y = g(t), \qquad z = h(t)
$$

These are the parametric equations of motion of the particle.

### Parameter

$t$ is the parameter.

### Trajectory

The curve traced out by the particle. Denoted by $C$.

Restricting $t$ to an interval $[a, b]$ gives

$$
x = f(t), \qquad y = g(t), \qquad z = h(t), \qquad a \le t \le b
$$

### Orientation

The direction in which $C$ is traced as $t$ increases is the orientation of the curve.

The circular helix

$$
x = a\cos t, \qquad y = a\sin t, \qquad z = bt, \qquad 0 \le t \le 2\pi
$$

has fixed real parameters $a > 0$ and $b$.

### Closed Curve

A parametric curve $C$ represented by $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$, $a \le t \le b$, is closed **iff**

$$
\boldsymbol{r}(a) = \boldsymbol{r}(b)
$$

<Definition name="Jordan Curve" level="3">

A Jordan curve in the plane is a simple closed curve, a closed curve that does not intersect itself except that the initial and terminal points coincide.

</Definition>

## Particle Motion

For a particle moving along a curve $C$ traced by $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$, the derivatives of $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ describe its motion.

### Velocity

$$
\boldsymbol{v}(t) = \boldsymbol{r}'(t)
$$

$\boldsymbol{v}(t)$ is tangent to $C$ and points in the direction of motion.

### Speed

$$
v(t) = \lVert \boldsymbol{v}(t) \rVert = \lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert
$$

<Note>

Arc length is the accumulation of speed over time: $L = \int_a^b v(t) \, \text{d}t$.

</Note>

### Acceleration

$$
\boldsymbol{a}(t) = \boldsymbol{v}'(t) = \boldsymbol{r}''(t)
$$

$\boldsymbol{a}(t)$ measures how the velocity vector changes, both in magnitude (speed) and in direction.

## Worked Example

For $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \langle t, t^2, 2t \rangle$, $0 \le t \le 2$, identify the endpoint positions, direction of motion, velocity, speed, and acceleration at $t = 1$.

<Solution>

$$
\boldsymbol{r}(0) = \langle 0,0,0\rangle, \qquad \boldsymbol{r}(2) = \langle 2,4,4\rangle
$$

The particle moves from $(0,0,0)$ to $(2,4,4)$ as $t$ increases.

$$
\boldsymbol{v}(t) = \boldsymbol{r}'(t) = \langle 1,2t,2\rangle, \qquad \boldsymbol{v}(1) = \langle 1,2,2\rangle
$$

$$
\lVert\boldsymbol{v}(1)\rVert = 3, \qquad \boldsymbol{a}(t) = \boldsymbol{r}''(t) = \langle 0,2,0\rangle
$$

</Solution>

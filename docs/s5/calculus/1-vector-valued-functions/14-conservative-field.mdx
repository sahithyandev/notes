A [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/field) $\boldsymbol{F}$ in 2-space or 3-space is conservative on a region $D$ **iff** it is the [gradient](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/field#gradient) field of some scalar function $\phi$ defined on $D$,

$$
\boldsymbol{F} = \nabla \phi
$$

$\phi$ is a potential function, or scalar potential, of $\boldsymbol{F}$.

<Theorem>
    
**If** $D$ is [simply connected](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/connectedness#simply-connected-region) and $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{0}$ on $D$, **then** $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative on $D$.

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

- In the plane, write $\boldsymbol{F} = P\boldsymbol{i} + Q\boldsymbol{j}$ and integrate $P$ with respect to $x$: $\phi = \int P\,\mathrm{d}x + a(y)$.
- Differentiate this expression with respect to $y$. Use $P_y = Q_x$ to show that the remaining difference from $Q$ depends only on $y$, then choose $a(y)$ so that $\phi_y = Q$.
- In space, integrate the first component with respect to $x$, then use $P_y = Q_x$ and $P_z = R_x$ to determine the remaining functions of $y$ and $z$.
- Use $Q_z = R_y$ and simple connectedness to ensure these choices are compatible, giving $\nabla\phi = \boldsymbol{F}$.

</Proof>

</Theorem>

### Curl Property

If $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative on $D$ and $\phi$ has continuous second-order partial derivatives, then

$$
\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = \boldsymbol{0}
$$

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

- Write $\boldsymbol{F} = \phi_x\boldsymbol{i} + \phi_y\boldsymbol{j} + \phi_z\boldsymbol{k}$
- Expand each component of the curl determinant
- Cancel the mixed partials, using $\phi_{xy} = \phi_{yx}$ and so on  
  It's possible because it has continuous second-order partial derivatives

</Proof>

$\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{0}$ alone does not make $\boldsymbol{F}$ conservative. On a region with a hole, the curl can vanish everywhere while $\boldsymbol{F}$ has no potential function.

## Conservative Field Test

Let $P(x,y)$ and $Q(x,y)$ have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open, simply connected region $D$ in the plane. The vector field

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(x,y) = P(x,y)\boldsymbol{i} + Q(x,y)\boldsymbol{j}
$$

is conservative on $D$ **iff**, for each point in $D$,

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}
$$

On a region that is not simply connected, the above equality can hold everywhere without $\boldsymbol{F}$ being conservative.

## Finding the Potential Function

Let $\boldsymbol{F} = f\boldsymbol{i} + g\boldsymbol{j} + h\boldsymbol{k}$ be conservative on $D$. A potential function $\phi$ satisfies

$$
\phi_x = f, \quad \phi_y = g, \quad \phi_z = h
$$

Steps:

1. Integrate $f$ with respect to $x$ to get $\phi = \int f\,\text{d}x + a(y,z)$.
2. Differentiate $\phi$ with respect to $y$ and equate to $g$. Solve for $a_y$, then integrate to get $a(y,z) = \int a_y\,\text{d}y + b(z)$.
3. Differentiate $\phi$ with respect to $z$ and equate to $h$. Solve for $b'(z)$, then integrate to get $b(z)$.
4. Substitute back. $\phi$ is unique up to an additive constant.

In the plane, drop $z$ and stop after step 2.

## Worked Example

Find a potential function of

$$
\boldsymbol{F} = (2xy + z)\boldsymbol{i} + (x^2 + 2y)\boldsymbol{j} + x\boldsymbol{k}
$$

Check the curl.

<Solution>

- $\partial h/\partial y = 0 = \partial g/\partial z$
- $\partial f/\partial z = 1 = \partial h/\partial x$
- $\partial g/\partial x = 2x = \partial f/\partial y$

So $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{0}$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, which is simply connected, and $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative.

Step 1.

$$
\phi = \int (2xy + z)\,\text{d}x = x^2 y + xz + a(y,z)
$$

Step 2.

$$
\phi_y = x^2 + a_y = x^2 + 2y \implies a_y = 2y \implies a = y^2 + b(z)
$$

Step 3.

$$
\phi_z = x + b'(z) = x \implies b'(z) = 0 \implies b = C
$$

Result.

$$
\phi(x,y,z) = x^2 y + xz + y^2 + C
$$

</Solution>

Determine whether

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(x,y) = (2xy + 3)\boldsymbol{i} + (x^2 + 4y)\boldsymbol{j}
$$

is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2$. If it is conservative, find a potential function.

<Solution>

Let $P = 2xy + 3$ and $Q = x^2 + 4y$.

$$
P_y = 2x = Q_x
$$

$\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, so $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative.

Integrate $P$ with respect to $x$.

$$
\phi = \int (2xy + 3)\,\text{d}x = x^2y + 3x + a(y)
$$

Differentiate with respect to $y$ and equate to $Q$.

$$
\phi_y = x^2 + a'(y) = x^2 + 4y
$$

$$
a'(y) = 4y \implies a(y) = 2y^2
$$

Therefore,

$$
\phi(x,y) = x^2y + 3x + 2y^2 + C
$$

</Solution>

Determine whether

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(x,y) = -y\boldsymbol{i} + x\boldsymbol{j}
$$

is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

<Solution>

Let $P = -y$ and $Q = x$.

$$
P_y = -1 \neq 1 = Q_x
$$

The conservative-field test fails. Therefore, $\boldsymbol{F}$ is not conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

</Solution>

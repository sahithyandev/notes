$\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ is smooth on an open interval $I$ **iff**:

- Differentiable  
  $\boldsymbol{r}$ is differentiable on $I$, no corners, cusps, or breaks in the position function.
- Continuous derivative  
  $\boldsymbol{r}'$ is continuous on $I$, the direction and speed of the curve can't jump abruptly.
- Non-vanishing derivative  
  $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \ne \boldsymbol{0}$ for all $t \in I$. If the velocity vector hits zero, the curve can stop and reverse, or form a sharp point, even though $\boldsymbol{r}$ is still differentiable there.

A curve traced by $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ may fail to be smooth where $\boldsymbol{r}'$ is not continuous or where $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) = \boldsymbol{0}$.

## Geometric Consequences

For a smooth curve, $\left\|\boldsymbol{r}'(t)\right\|$ is continuous and strictly positive. The unit tangent vector is therefore defined at every point:

$$
\boldsymbol{T}(t)=\frac{\boldsymbol{r}'(t)}{\left\|\boldsymbol{r}'(t)\right\|}
$$

The arc length from $t_0$ to $t$ is

$$
s(t)=\int_{t_0}^{t}\left\|\boldsymbol{r}'(u)\right\|\,\text{d}u
$$

## Smoothness Check

To test whether $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ is smooth on an open interval:

- Differentiate $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$.
- Check that every component of $\boldsymbol{r}'(t)$ is continuous on the interval.
- Solve $\boldsymbol{r}'(t)=\boldsymbol{0}$.
- Exclude any parameter values where the derivative vanishes.

## Worked Example

Determine whether $\boldsymbol{r}(t)=\langle t^2,t^3\rangle$ is smooth on $\mathbb{R}$ and on $(0,\infty)$.

<Solution>

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t)=\langle2t,3t^2\rangle
$$

The derivative is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, but $\boldsymbol{r}'(0)=\boldsymbol{0}$. Therefore, $\boldsymbol{r}$ is not smooth on $\mathbb{R}$.

For $t>0$, the first component $2t$ is positive, so $\boldsymbol{r}'(t)\ne\boldsymbol{0}$. Therefore, the curve is smooth on $(0,\infty)$.

</Solution>

Determine where

$$
\boldsymbol{r}(t)=\langle 6\cos t-\cos 6t,6\sin t-\sin 6t\rangle,
\qquad 0\le t\le 2\pi
$$

is not smooth.

<Solution>

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t)=\langle -6\sin t+6\sin 6t,6\cos t-6\cos 6t\rangle
$$

Set the derivative equal to the zero vector:

$$
\sin 6t=\sin t,\qquad \cos 6t=\cos t
$$

Both equations hold when

$$
6t=t+2\pi k
$$

for an integer $k$. Therefore,

$$
t=\frac{2\pi k}{5}
$$

Within $0\le t\le 2\pi$, the derivative vanishes at

$$
t=0,\frac{2\pi}{5},\frac{4\pi}{5},\frac{6\pi}{5},\frac{8\pi}{5},2\pi
$$

The curve is not smooth at these parameter values. They are the points between its petals.

</Solution>

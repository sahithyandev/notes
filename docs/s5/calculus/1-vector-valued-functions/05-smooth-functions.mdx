$\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ is smooth on an open interval $I$ **iff**:

- Differentiable  
  $\boldsymbol{r}$ is differentiable on $I$, no corners, cusps, or breaks in the position function.
- Continuous derivative  
  $\boldsymbol{r}'$ is continuous on $I$, the direction and speed of the curve can't jump abruptly.
- Non-vanishing derivative  
  $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \ne \vec{0}$ for all $t \in I$. If the velocity vector hits zero, the curve can stop and reverse, or form a sharp point, even though $\boldsymbol{r}$ is still differentiable there.

A curve traced by $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ may fail to be smooth where $\boldsymbol{r}'$ is not continuous or where $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) = \vec{0}$.

Examples:

- $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \langle 6\cos t - \cos 6t, 6\sin t - \sin 6t \rangle$, $0 \le t \le 2\pi$, is not smooth at the points between its petals.

## Worked Example

Determine whether $\boldsymbol{r}(t)=\langle t^2,t^3\rangle$ is smooth on $\mathbb{R}$ and on $(0,\infty)$.

<Solution>

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t)=\langle2t,3t^2\rangle
$$

The derivative is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, but $\boldsymbol{r}'(0)=\vec{0}$. Therefore, $\boldsymbol{r}$ is not smooth on $\mathbb{R}$.

For $t>0$, the first component $2t$ is positive, so $\boldsymbol{r}'(t)\ne\vec{0}$. Therefore, the curve is smooth on $(0,\infty)$.

</Solution>

<Theorem>

Let $\sigma$ be a [closed](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/surface#closed-surface), piecewise smooth, orientable surface that encloses a solid region $R$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Suppose $\boldsymbol{F}$ is a [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/field#vector-field) whose component functions have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open set containing $R$ and its boundary $\sigma$. If $\hat{n}$ is the outward unit normal field for $\sigma$,

$$
\iint_\sigma \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S = \iiint_R \text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}\,\text{d}V
$$

</Theorem>

<DivergenceTheoremDiagram />

<Note>

If $\sigma$ is not closed, close it by adding the missing faces, then subtract their flux to recover the flux across the original surface.

</Note>

## Divergence as Flux Density

Let $G$ be a small solid region centered at $P_0$ with boundary surface $\sigma(G)$ oriented outward, and let $\boldsymbol{F}$ be a vector field whose component functions have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open set containing $G$. Denote the volume of $G$ by $\text{Vol}(G)$ and the flux of $\boldsymbol{F}$ across $\sigma(G)$ by $\Phi(G)$.

$$
\Phi(G) = \iint_{\sigma(G)} \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S = \iiint_G \text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}\,\text{d}V \approx \text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}(P_0)\,\text{Vol}(G)
$$

As $G$ shrinks to $P_0$ in all directions,

$$
\text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}(P_0) = \lim_{G \to P_0} \frac{1}{\text{Vol}(G)} \iint_{\sigma(G)} \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S
$$

If $\boldsymbol{F}$ is the velocity field of a fluid in steady-state flow, $\text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}$ is the limiting outward flux per unit volume at a point.

## Examples

### Sphere

Find the outward flux of $\boldsymbol{F} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ through the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$.

<Solution>

$$
\text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3
$$

Let $R$ be the solid ball of radius $a$, with volume $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi a^3$.

$$
\Phi = \iiint_R 3\,\text{d}V = 3 \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3 = 4\pi a^3
$$

</Solution>

### Cube

Find the outward flux of $\boldsymbol{F} = x^2\hat{i} + y^2\hat{j} + z^2\hat{k}$ through the surface of the cube $0 \le x, y, z \le 1$.

<Solution>

$$
\text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F} = 2x + 2y + 2z
$$

$$
\Phi = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \int_0^1 2(x + y + z)\,\text{d}x\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}z
$$

Each term integrates to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ over the unit cube, so the 3 terms add to $2 \cdot 3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$$
\Phi = 3
$$

</Solution>

### Hemisphere

Find the upward flux of $\boldsymbol{F} = z\hat{k}$ through the hemisphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$, $z \ge 0$.

<Solution>

The hemisphere is not closed. Close it with the disk $D = \{x^2 + y^2 \le 1,\ z = 0\}$. The closed surface encloses the solid half-ball $R$.

$$
\text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F} = 1
$$

$$
\iiint_R 1\,\text{d}V = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi = \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$

The outward normal on $D$ is $-\hat{k}$. On $D$, $z = 0$, so

$$
\iint_D \boldsymbol{F} \cdot (-\hat{k})\,\text{d}S = \iint_D (-z)\,\text{d}S = 0
$$

The flux across the closed surface is the sum of the flux across the hemisphere and across $D$.

$$
\Phi = \frac{2\pi}{3} - 0 = \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$

</Solution>

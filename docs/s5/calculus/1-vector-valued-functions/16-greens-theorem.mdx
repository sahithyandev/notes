<Theorem>

Let $C$ be a positively oriented, piecewise smooth [Jordan curve](./introduction#jordan-curve) in the plane, and let $D$ be the region bounded by $C$. Suppose

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(x,y) = M(x,y)\boldsymbol{i} + N(x,y)\boldsymbol{j}
$$

is continuously differentiable on an open region containing $D$. Then

$$
\oint_C M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y = \iint_D \left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right)\text{d}A
$$

</Theorem>

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

- First prove the result for a rectangle by applying the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to the 2 horizontal and 2 vertical edges
- Integrate $\partial N/\partial x$ with respect to $x$ and $\partial M/\partial y$ with respect to $y$
- Partition a general region into finitely many small rectangles
- Sum the rectangle identities and cancel contributions along every interior edge
- Use the continuity of the partial derivatives and pass to the limit as the partition is refined

</Proof>

<Note>

For a simple closed curve, positive orientation means counterclockwise. Equivalently, as $C$ is traversed, $D$ remains on the left.

</Note>

<Theorem name="Area Formula">
    
The area of the region $D$ bounded by $C$ is a line integral over $C$.

<Formula>

$$
A(D) = \frac{1}{2}\oint_C x\,\text{d}y - y\,\text{d}x
$$

</Formula>

</Theorem>

<Proof>

Take $M = -y/2$ and $N = x/2$. The integrand of Green's theorem becomes constant.

$$
\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1
$$

Green's theorem then reduces to the area of $D$.

$$
\oint_C M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y = \iint_D 1\,\text{d}A = A(D)
$$

Any $M$ and $N$ with $\partial N/\partial x - \partial M/\partial y = 1$ gives an area formula. 2 other common choices:

- $M = 0$, $N = x$  
  $A(D) = \oint_C x\,\text{d}y$
- $M = -y$, $N = 0$  
  $A(D) = -\oint_C y\,\text{d}x$

All 3 formulas give the same value. Their integrands differ by a multiple of $\text{d}(xy) = x\,\text{d}y + y\,\text{d}x$, which integrates to $0$ over a closed curve.

</Proof>

<Theorem name="Green's Theorem for Doubly Connected Regions">

Let $R$ be a doubly connected region in the plane, with outer boundary $C_1$ oriented counterclockwise and inner boundary $C_2$ oriented clockwise. If $C_1$, $C_2$, and $\boldsymbol{F}(x,y) = M(x,y)\boldsymbol{i} + N(x,y)\boldsymbol{j}$ satisfy the hypotheses of Green's theorem, then

$$
\iint_R \left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right)\text{d}A = \oint_{C_1} M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y + \oint_{C_2} M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y
$$

</Theorem>

<Note>

For a multiply connected region with $n$ holes, the boundary integral is the sum of $n+1$ line integrals, taken counterclockwise around the outer boundary and clockwise around each hole.

</Note>

## Worked Examples

### Region Between 2 Curves

Suppose $C$ is the positively oriented boundary of the region $D$ between $y = x^2$ and $y = x$. Evaluate:

$$
\oint_C xy\,\text{d}x + x^2\,\text{d}y
$$

<Solution>

Here $M = xy$ and $N = x^2$.

$$
\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 2x - x = x
$$

The curves meet at $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$, so $D$ is $0 \le x \le 1$, $x^2 \le y \le x$.

$$
\oint_C xy\,\text{d}x + x^2\,\text{d}y = \iint_D x\,\text{d}A = \int_0^1 \int_{x^2}^{x} x\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}x = \int_0^1 (x^2 - x^3)\,\text{d}x = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12}
$$

Direct check along $y = x^2$ from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$, with $x = t$ and $y = t^2$:

$$
\int_0^1 \left(t \cdot t^2 + t^2 \cdot 2t\right)\text{d}t = \int_0^1 3t^3\,\text{d}t = \frac{3}{4}
$$

Direct check along $y = x$ from $(1,1)$ to $(0,0)$, with $x = y = t$:

$$
\int_1^0 \left(t^2 + t^2\right)\text{d}t = -\frac{2}{3}
$$

The sum is $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{12}$.

</Solution>

### Area of an Ellipse

Find the area enclosed by $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ with $a, b > 0$.

<Solution>

Parametrize the positively oriented boundary.

$$
x = a\cos t, \quad y = b\sin t, \quad 0 \le t \le 2\pi
$$

Differentiate $y$.

$$
\text{d}y = b\cos t\,\text{d}t
$$

Substitute into the integrand.

$$
x\,\text{d}y = ab\cos^2 t\,\text{d}t
$$

Apply the area formula $A(D) = \oint_C x\,\text{d}y$ with $\cos^2 t = \frac{1}{2} (1 + \cos 2t)$.

$$
A = ab \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{1 + \cos 2t}{2}\,\text{d}t = ab \left[ \frac{t}{2} + \frac{\sin 2t}{4} \right]_0^{2\pi} = \pi ab
$$

</Solution>

### Hole at the Origin

Suppose $C$ is any positively oriented, piecewise smooth Jordan curve enclosing the origin. Evaluate:

$$
\oint_C \dfrac{-y\,\text{d}x + x\,\text{d}y}{x^2 + y^2}
$$

<Solution>

Here $M = -y/(x^2+y^2)$ and $N = x/(x^2+y^2)$. They are undefined at the origin, so remove a small circle $C_2$ around it. Away from the origin,

$$
\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 0
$$

Apply Green's theorem to the region between $C$ and $C_2$. Its positive orientation traverses $C$ counterclockwise and $C_2$ clockwise.

$$
0 = \oint_C M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y + \oint_{C_2} M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y
$$

Reversing $C_2$ gives

$$
\oint_C M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y = \oint_{C_2^+} M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y
$$

Parametrize $C_2^+$ by $x=r\cos t$, $y=r\sin t$, where $0\le t\le2\pi$. Then

$$
\frac{-y\,\text{d}x+x\,\text{d}y}{x^2+y^2}
= \frac{r^2\sin^2t+r^2\cos^2t}{r^2}\,\text{d}t
= \text{d}t
$$

$$
\oint_C \frac{-y\,\text{d}x+x\,\text{d}y}{x^2+y^2}
= \int_0^{2\pi}\text{d}t
= 2\pi
$$

The curve encloses the origin once counterclockwise, so the value is $2\pi$, independent of its shape.

</Solution>

### Conservative Vector Field Criterion

Let $f$ and $g$ have continuous first partial derivatives on an open simply connected region $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$. Prove that

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(x,y) = f(x,y)\boldsymbol{i} + g(x,y)\boldsymbol{j}
$$

is conservative on $D$ **iff**

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial g}{\partial x}
$$

at every point in $D$.

<Solution>

Set $M=f$ and $N=g$. Green's theorem gives

$$
\oint_C \boldsymbol{F}\cdot\text{d}\boldsymbol{r}
= \oint_C f\,\text{d}x + g\,\text{d}y
= \iint_R \left(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)\text{d}A
$$

for every positively oriented simple closed curve $C$ in $D$, with interior region $R \subseteq D$.

To prove the forward implication, suppose that $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative. There exists a potential function $\phi$ such that

$$
\nabla\phi = \boldsymbol{F}, \qquad \phi_x=f, \qquad \phi_y=g
$$

Every closed line integral of a conservative field is $0$. Therefore,

$$
\iint_R \left(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)\text{d}A=0
$$

for every such region $R$. The integrand is continuous. If it were positive or negative at some point, it would keep that sign on a sufficiently small rectangle around the point, giving a nonzero double integral. Hence,

$$
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=0
$$

throughout $D$. Thus,

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}
$$

To prove the reverse implication, suppose that

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}
$$

throughout $D$. Since $D$ is simply connected, every piecewise smooth closed curve in $D$ bounds a region in $D$, possibly after decomposing the curve into finitely many simple closed curves. Green's theorem then gives

$$
\oint_C f\,\text{d}x+g\,\text{d}y
=\iint_R \left(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)\text{d}A
=0
$$

Therefore, the line integral is independent of path. Fix $(x_0,y_0)\in D$ and define

$$
\phi(x,y)=\int_{(x_0,y_0)}^{(x,y)} f\,\text{d}x+g\,\text{d}y
$$

where the path lies in $D$. Path independence makes $\phi$ well-defined.

For a small $h$ such that the horizontal segment remains in $D$,

$$
\phi(x+h,y)-\phi(x,y)=\int_x^{x+h}f(t,y)\,\text{d}t
$$

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus,

$$
\phi_x(x,y)=f(x,y)
$$

Similarly, using a vertical segment,

$$
\phi(x,y+h)-\phi(x,y)=\int_y^{y+h}g(x,t)\,\text{d}t
$$

so

$$
\phi_y(x,y)=g(x,y)
$$

Hence,

$$
\nabla\phi=f\boldsymbol{i}+g\boldsymbol{j}=\boldsymbol{F}
$$

Therefore, $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative on $D$.

</Solution>

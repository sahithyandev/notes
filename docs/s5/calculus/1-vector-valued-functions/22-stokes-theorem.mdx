<Theorem>

Let $\sigma$ be a piecewise smooth oriented surface bounded by a [positively oriented](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/orientation-of-surface#positive-orientation-of-a-boundary-curve), piecewise smooth, simple closed curve $C$. Suppose

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(x,y,z) = f(x,y,z)\hat{i} + g(x,y,z)\hat{j} + h(x,y,z)\hat{k}
$$

is a [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/field#vector-field) whose component functions have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open set containing $\sigma$. If $\hat{n}$ is the chosen unit normal field for $\sigma$,

$$
\oint_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \iint_\sigma (\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F}) \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S
$$

</Theorem>

<StokesTheoremDiagram />

<Note>

Green's Theorem is the special case $\sigma = D$, $\hat{n} = \hat{k}$, $\boldsymbol{F}(x,y,z) = M(x,y)\hat{i} + N(x,y)\hat{j}$, where $D$ is a region in the $xy$-plane. Then

$$
\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k}
$$

and Stokes' Theorem gives

$$
\oint_C M\,\text{d}x + N\,\text{d}y = \iint_D \left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right)\text{d}A
$$

</Note>

## Examples

### Curve on a Cylinder

Evaluate $\oint_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r}$ for $\boldsymbol{F} = -y^2\hat{i} + x\hat{j} + z^2\hat{k}$, where $C$ is the intersection of the plane $y + z = 2$ and the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, oriented counterclockwise when viewed from above.

<Solution>

Stokes' Theorem needs a surface bounded by $C$. Choose $\sigma$ as the part of the plane $y + z = 2$ inside the cylinder. Its boundary is $C$.

The flux of $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F}$ through $\sigma$ is computed with the [flux through a graph](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/flux#through-a-graph) formula, which needs the surface written as $G(x,y,z) = 0$. Move all terms of the plane equation to the left side.

$$
G(x,y,z) = y + z - 2 = 0
$$

The formula replaces $\hat{n}\,\text{d}S$ by $\nabla G\,\text{d}A$, where $\text{d}A$ is the area element in the $xy$-plane. So $\nabla G$ is the normal vector that already includes the area scaling between $\sigma$ and its projection.

$$
\nabla G = \hat{j} + \hat{k}
$$

The $\hat{k}$ component is positive, so $\nabla G$ gives the upward orientation. This matches the counterclockwise orientation of $C$ viewed from above.

By Stokes' Theorem, the line integral equals the flux of $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F}$ through $\sigma$. So $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F}$ takes the place of $\boldsymbol{F}$ in $\iint_R \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \nabla G\,\text{d}A$.

$$
\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ -y^2 & x & z^2 \end{vmatrix} = (1 + 2y)\hat{k}
$$

$$
\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} \cdot \nabla G = 1 + 2y
$$

The projection of $\sigma$ onto the $xy$-plane is the disk $R = \{x^2 + y^2 \le 1\}$. The term $2y$ integrates to 0 over $R$ by symmetry.

$$
\oint_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \iint_R (1 + 2y)\,\text{d}A = \pi
$$

</Solution>

### Hemisphere

Find the flux of $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F}$ upward through the hemisphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$, $z \ge 0$, for $\boldsymbol{F} = z\hat{i} + x\hat{j} + y\hat{k}$.

<Solution>

The boundary of the hemisphere is the unit circle $C$ in the $xy$-plane, oriented counterclockwise. Stokes' Theorem converts the surface integral into a line integral over $C$.

Parametrize $C$ as $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \cos t\,\hat{i} + \sin t\,\hat{j}$ for $0 \le t \le 2\pi$.

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{r}(t)) = \cos t\,\hat{j} + \sin t\,\hat{k}
$$

$$
\text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = (-\sin t\,\hat{i} + \cos t\,\hat{j})\,\text{d}t
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \cos^2 t\,\text{d}t
$$

$$
\iint_\sigma (\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F}) \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S = \oint_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \int_0^{2\pi} \cos^2 t\,\text{d}t = \pi
$$

</Solution>

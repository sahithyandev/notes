The [Jacobian determinant](/s2/methods-of-mathematics/multivariable-calculus/jacobian#jacobian-determinant) of a transformation $T$ measures the local scaling of area. A change of variables moves an integral from the target region onto the source region.

<Theorem>

Let $T$ be a transformation from the $uv$-plane to the $xy$-plane, mapping a region $S$ onto a region $R$. If:

- $T$ is one-to-one on $S$
- $T$ has continuous first partial derivatives
- $\dfrac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}$ is non-zero and does not change sign on $S$

then

$$
\iint_R f(x,y)\,\text{d}A = \iint_S f(x(u,v), y(u,v)) \left| \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} \right| \text{d}A
$$

</Theorem>

<Note>

Polar coordinates are a special case of this change of variables, with Jacobian $r$.

</Note>

## Worked Examples

### Parallelogram

Evaluate $\iint_R (x^2 - y^2)\,\text{d}A$ over the parallelogram $R$ bounded by the lines $x + y = 1$, $x + y = 3$, $x - y = 0$ and $x - y = 2$.

<Solution>

The boundary lines are constant values of $x + y$ and $x - y$, and the integrand factors as $(x+y)(x-y)$. Let $u = x + y$ and $v = x - y$. Then $R$ becomes the rectangle $S: 1 \le u \le 3,\ 0 \le v \le 2$ and the integrand becomes $uv$.

Solve for $x$ and $y$.

$$
x = \frac{u+v}{2}, \quad y = \frac{u-v}{2}
$$

</Solution>

Compute the Jacobian.

<Solution>

$$
\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
\frac{1}{2} & -\frac{1}{2}
\end{vmatrix} =
-\frac{1}{2}
$$

The transformation is one-to-one, has continuous first partial derivatives, and its Jacobian is a non-zero constant, so the change of variables applies with $\left| -\frac{1}{2} \right| = \frac{1}{2}$.

$$
\iint_R (x^2 - y^2)\,\text{d}A = \int_1^3 \int_0^2 uv \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\text{d}v\,\text{d}u
$$

The integral separates.

$$
\frac{1}{2} \int_1^3 u\,\text{d}u \cdot \int_0^2 v\,\text{d}v = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 4
$$

</Solution>

A [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/vector-field) $\boldsymbol{F}$ in 2-space or 3-space is conservative on a region $D$ **iff** it is the [gradient](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/field) field of some scalar function $\phi$ defined on $D$,

$$
\boldsymbol{F} = \nabla \phi
$$

$\phi$ is a potential function, or scalar potential, of $\boldsymbol{F}$.

## Curl Condition

If $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative on $D$ and $\phi$ has continuous second-order partial derivatives, then

$$
\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = \boldsymbol{0}
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Write $\boldsymbol{F} = \phi_x\hat{i} + \phi_y\hat{j} + \phi_z\hat{k}$
- Expand each component of the curl determinant
- Cancel the mixed partials, using $\phi_{xy} = \phi_{yx}$ and so on

</Note>

The converse needs a condition on $D$. If $D$ is [simply connected](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/connectedness#simply-connected-region) and $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{0}$ on $D$, then $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative on $D$.

<Note>

$\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{0}$ alone does not make $\boldsymbol{F}$ conservative. On a region with a hole, the curl can vanish everywhere while $\boldsymbol{F}$ has no potential function.

</Note>

## Conservative Field Test

Let $P(x,y)$ and $Q(x,y)$ have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open, simply connected region $D$ in the plane. The vector field

$$
\boldsymbol{F}(x,y) = P(x,y)\hat{i} + Q(x,y)\hat{j}
$$

is conservative on $D$ **iff**, for each point in $D$,

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}
$$

The region must be simply connected. On a region with a hole, $\partial P/\partial y = \partial Q/\partial x$ can hold everywhere without $\boldsymbol{F}$ being conservative.

## Integral over a Closed Curve

Let $\boldsymbol{F}$ be conservative on $D$ and $C$ a piecewise smooth closed curve in $D$. Then

$$
\oint_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = 0
$$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Apply the [fundamental theorem of line integrals](/s5/calculus/vector-valued-functions/line-integral#fundamental-theorem-of-line-integrals)
- Use $\boldsymbol{r}(b) = \boldsymbol{r}(a)$ for a closed curve
- Subtract equal values of $\phi$

</Note>

## Equivalence of Conservative Fields

Let $\boldsymbol{F}(x,y,z) = f\hat{i} + g\hat{j} + h\hat{k}$ be continuous on an open connected region $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$. The following are equivalent:

- $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative on $D$
- $\displaystyle\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = 0$ for every piecewise smooth closed curve $C$ in $D$
- For any 2 points $P$, $Q$ in $D$, $\displaystyle\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r}$ is independent of the piecewise smooth curve $C$ in $D$ from $P$ to $Q$

## Finding the Potential Function

Let $\boldsymbol{F} = f\hat{i} + g\hat{j} + h\hat{k}$ be conservative on $D$. A potential function $\phi$ satisfies

$$
\phi_x = f, \quad \phi_y = g, \quad \phi_z = h
$$

Steps:

1. Integrate $f$ with respect to $x$ to get $\phi = \int f\,\text{d}x + a(y,z)$.
2. Differentiate $\phi$ with respect to $y$ and equate to $g$. Solve for $a_y$, then integrate to get $a(y,z) = \int a_y\,\text{d}y + b(z)$.
3. Differentiate $\phi$ with respect to $z$ and equate to $h$. Solve for $b'(z)$, then integrate to get $b(z)$.
4. Substitute back. $\phi$ is unique up to an additive constant.

In the plane, drop $z$ and stop after step 2.

## Worked Example

Find a potential function of

$$
\boldsymbol{F} = (2xy + z)\hat{i} + (x^2 + 2y)\hat{j} + x\hat{k}
$$

Check the curl.

- $\partial h/\partial y = 0 = \partial g/\partial z$
- $\partial f/\partial z = 1 = \partial h/\partial x$
- $\partial g/\partial x = 2x = \partial f/\partial y$

So $\text{curl}\,\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{0}$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, which is simply connected, and $\boldsymbol{F}$ is conservative.

Step 1.

$$
\phi = \int (2xy + z)\,\text{d}x = x^2 y + xz + a(y,z)
$$

Step 2.

$$
\phi_y = x^2 + a_y = x^2 + 2y \implies a_y = 2y \implies a = y^2 + b(z)
$$

Step 3.

$$
\phi_z = x + b'(z) = x \implies b'(z) = 0 \implies b = C
$$

Result.

$$
\phi(x,y,z) = x^2 y + xz + y^2 + C
$$

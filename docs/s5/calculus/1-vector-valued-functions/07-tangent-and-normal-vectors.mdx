Let $C$ be a curve traced by a differentiable vector-valued function $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ with $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \ne \boldsymbol{0}$, where $t$ is the parameter. $s$ denotes the arc-length parameter of $C$.

## Unit Tangent Vector

The unit tangent vector at $t$ is

$$
\boldsymbol{T}(t) = \frac{\boldsymbol{r}'(t)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert}
$$

$\boldsymbol{T}(t)$ is tangent to $C$ and points in the direction of increasing $t$.

If $\boldsymbol{r}$ is parametrized by arc length $s$, $\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(s) \rVert = 1$, so

$$
\boldsymbol{T}(s) = \boldsymbol{r}'(s)
$$

## Unit Normal Vector

If $\boldsymbol{T}'(t)$ exists and $\boldsymbol{T}'(t) \ne \boldsymbol{0}$, the principal unit normal vector at $t$ is

$$
\boldsymbol{N}(t) = \frac{\boldsymbol{T}'(t)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{T}'(t) \rVert}
$$

$\boldsymbol{N}(t)$ is normal to $C$ and points in the direction of $\boldsymbol{T}'(t)$.

If $\boldsymbol{T}'(t)$ exists and is non-zero, $\boldsymbol{N}(t) \perp \boldsymbol{T}(t)$.

<Proof label="Proof Hint">

Since $\lVert \boldsymbol{T}(t) \rVert = 1$, $\boldsymbol{T}(t) \cdot \boldsymbol{T}(t) = 1$. Differentiating both sides gives $\boldsymbol{T}(t) \cdot \boldsymbol{T}'(t) = 0$.

</Proof>

In two-dimensional space, 2 unit vectors are orthogonal to $\boldsymbol{T}(t)$. In three-dimensional space, infinitely many unit vectors are orthogonal to $\boldsymbol{T}(t)$. $\boldsymbol{N}(t)$ is the one in the direction of $\boldsymbol{T}'(t)$.

$\boldsymbol{T}$ points in the direction of motion. $\boldsymbol{N}$ points in the direction the moving object is turning. For a plane curve, $\boldsymbol{N}$ points toward the concave side of the trajectory.

If $\boldsymbol{r}$ is parametrized by arc length $s$ and $\boldsymbol{r}''(s) \ne \boldsymbol{0}$, then $\boldsymbol{T}(s) = \boldsymbol{r}'(s)$ gives $\boldsymbol{T}'(s) = \boldsymbol{r}''(s)$, so

$$
\boldsymbol{N}(s) = \frac{\boldsymbol{r}''(s)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}''(s) \rVert}
$$

## Binormal Vector

For $C$ in three-dimensional space, with $\boldsymbol{T}(t)$ and $\boldsymbol{N}(t)$ defined, the binormal vector is

$$
\boldsymbol{B}(t) = \boldsymbol{T}(t) \times \boldsymbol{N}(t)
$$

$\boldsymbol{B}(t)$ is orthogonal to both $\boldsymbol{T}(t)$ and $\boldsymbol{N}(t)$, oriented by the right-hand rule, and is a unit vector since $\lVert \boldsymbol{T}(t) \times \boldsymbol{N}(t) \rVert = \lVert \boldsymbol{T}(t) \rVert \lVert \boldsymbol{N}(t) \rVert \sin\frac{\pi}{2} = 1$.

If $\boldsymbol{r}'(t)$ and $\boldsymbol{r}''(t)$ exist and $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) \ne \boldsymbol{0}$,

$$
\boldsymbol{B}(t) = \frac{\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) \rVert}
$$

If $\boldsymbol{r}$ is parametrized by arc length $s$ and $\boldsymbol{r}''(s) \ne \boldsymbol{0}$,

$$
\boldsymbol{B}(s) = \frac{\boldsymbol{r}'(s) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(s)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}''(s) \rVert}
$$

## TNB Frame

<Aka>Frenet frame</Aka>. The moving orthonormal frame determined by $\boldsymbol
{T}(t)$, $\boldsymbol{N}(t)$, $\boldsymbol{B}(t)$. Origin is the current point
in the curve, and changes with $t$.

At each point on $C$, $\boldsymbol{T}(t)$, $\boldsymbol{N}(t)$, $\boldsymbol{B}(t)$ determine 3 mutually perpendicular planes:

- $\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{B}$-plane  
  Rectifying plane. $C$ does not bend within it; to second order, $C$ projected onto this plane is a straight line, i.e. "rectified".
- $\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{N}$-plane  
  Osculating plane. The plane that best approximates $C$ near the point.
- $\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{B}$-plane  
  Normal plane. Perpendicular to $T$. Contains all normals to $C$ at the point.

## Worked Example

For the curve $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \cos t\,\boldsymbol{i} + \sin t\,\boldsymbol{j} + t\,\boldsymbol{k}$, find the point and the TNB frame at $t = \pi/2$.

<Solution>

Differentiate and compute the speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{r}'(t) &= -\sin t\,\boldsymbol{i} + \cos t\,\boldsymbol{j} + \boldsymbol{k} \\
\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert &= \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + 1} = \sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Compute $\boldsymbol{T}(t)$.

$$
\boldsymbol{T}(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\sin t\,\boldsymbol{i} + \cos t\,\boldsymbol{j} + \boldsymbol{k}\right)
$$

Differentiate $\boldsymbol{T}(t)$ and compute its length.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{T}'(t) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\cos t\,\boldsymbol{i} - \sin t\,\boldsymbol{j}\right) \\
\lVert \boldsymbol{T}'(t) \rVert &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Compute $\boldsymbol{N}(t)$.

$$
\boldsymbol{N}(t) = \frac{\boldsymbol{T}'(t)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{T}'(t) \rVert} = -\cos t\,\boldsymbol{i} - \sin t\,\boldsymbol{j}
$$

Differentiate $\boldsymbol{r}'(t)$ and compute the cross product.

$$
\boldsymbol{r}''(t) = -\cos t\,\boldsymbol{i} - \sin t\,\boldsymbol{j}
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) = \sin t\,\boldsymbol{i} - \cos t\,\boldsymbol{j} + \boldsymbol{k}, \quad \lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) \rVert = \sqrt{2}
$$

Compute $\boldsymbol{B}(t)$.

$$
\boldsymbol{B}(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\sin t\,\boldsymbol{i} - \cos t\,\boldsymbol{j} + \boldsymbol{k}\right)
$$

Evaluate at $t = \pi/2$, where $\sin t = 1$ and $\cos t = 0$.

$$
\boldsymbol{r}(\pi/2) = (0, 1, \pi/2)
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{T} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-1, 0, 1), \quad \boldsymbol{N} = (0, -1, 0), \quad \boldsymbol{B} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 0, 1)
$$

Verify orthogonality.

$$
\boldsymbol{T} \cdot \boldsymbol{N} = 0, \quad \boldsymbol{T} \cdot \boldsymbol{B} = \frac{-1 + 1}{2} = 0, \quad \boldsymbol{N} \cdot \boldsymbol{B} = 0
$$

At $t = \pi/2$, the TNB frame at $(0, 1, \pi/2)$ is $\boldsymbol{T} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-1, 0, 1)$, $\boldsymbol{N} = (0, -1, 0)$, $\boldsymbol{B} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 0, 1)$.

</Solution>

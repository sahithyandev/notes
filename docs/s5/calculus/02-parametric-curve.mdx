A particle moving along a curve $C$ in three-dimensional space has $x$, $y$, $z$ coordinates as functions of time $t$:

$$
x = f(t), \qquad y = g(t), \qquad z = h(t)
$$

These are the parametric equations of motion of the particle.

## Parameter

$t$ is the parameter.

## Trajectory

The curve traced out by the particle. Denoted by $C$ usually. Restricting $t$ to an interval $[a, b]$ gives

$$
x = f(t), \qquad y = g(t), \qquad z = h(t), \qquad a \le t \le b
$$

## Orientation

The direction in which $C$ is traced as $t$ increases is the orientation of the curve.

Examples:

- The circular helix $x = a\cos t$, $y = a\sin t$, $z = bt$, $0 \le t \le 2\pi$, for fixed real $a > 0$ and $b$.

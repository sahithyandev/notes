## Unit Tangent Vector

Let $C$ be the curve traced by a differentiable $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ with $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \ne \vec{0}$. The unit tangent vector at $t$ is

$$
T(t) = \frac{\boldsymbol{r}'(t)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert}
$$

$T(t)$ is tangent to $C$ and points in the direction of increasing $t$.

## Unit Normal Vector

If $T'(t)$ exists and $T'(t) \ne \vec{0}$, the principal unit normal vector at $t$ is

$$
N(t) = \frac{T'(t)}{\lVert T'(t) \rVert}
$$

$N(t)$ is normal to $C$ and points in the direction of $T'(t)$.

If $T'(t)$ exists and is non-zero, $N(t) \perp T(t)$.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

Since $\lVert T(t) \rVert = 1$, $T(t) \cdot T(t) = 1$. Differentiating both sides gives $T(t) \cdot T'(t) = 0$.

</Note>

In two-dimensional space, 2 unit vectors are orthogonal to $T(t)$. In three-dimensional space, infinitely many unit vectors are orthogonal to $T(t)$. $N(t)$ is the one in the direction of $T'(t)$.

$T$ points in the direction of motion. $N$ points in the direction the moving object is turning. For a plane curve, $N$ points toward the concave side of the trajectory.

## Binormal Vector

Let $C$ be the curve traced by a differentiable $\boldsymbol{r}(t)$ in three-dimensional space, with $T(t)$ and $N(t)$ defined. The binormal vector is

$$
B(t) = T(t) \times N(t)
$$

$B(t)$ is orthogonal to both $T(t)$ and $N(t)$, oriented by the right-hand rule, and is a unit vector since $\lVert T(t) \times N(t) \rVert = \lVert T(t) \rVert \lVert N(t) \rVert \sin\frac{\pi}{2} = 1$.

If $\boldsymbol{r}'(t)$ and $\boldsymbol{r}''(t)$ exist and $\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) \ne \vec{0}$,

$$
B(t) = \frac{\boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(t) \rVert}
$$

If $\boldsymbol{r}$ is parametrized by arc length $s$ and $\boldsymbol{r}''(s) \ne \vec{0}$,

$$
B(s) = \frac{\boldsymbol{r}'(s) \times \boldsymbol{r}''(s)}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}''(s) \rVert}
$$

## TNB Frame

Aka. Frenet frame. The moving orthonormal frame determined by $T(t)$, $N(t)$, $B(t)$. Origin is the current point in the curve, and changes with $t$.

At each point on $C$, $T(t)$, $N(t)$, $B(t)$ determine 3 mutually perpendicular planes:

- $TB$-plane  
  Rectifying plane. $C$ does not bend within it; to second order, $C$ projected onto this plane is a straight line, i.e. "rectified".
- $TN$-plane  
  Osculating plane. The plane that best approximates $C$ near the point.
- $NB$-plane  
  Normal plane. Perpendicular to $T$. Contains all normals to $C$ at the point.

The flux of a [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-values-functions/vector-field) through an [oriented surface](/s5/calculus/vector-values-functions/orientation) measures the net amount of flow passing through the surface in the chosen normal direction, assuming a fluid in steady state and incompressible. The volume of fluid flowing through a portion of a surface depends on:

- Speed of the fluid.
- Orientation of the surface relative to the flow.
- Area of the portion of the surface.

Let $\boldsymbol{F}$ be a continuous vector field on an oriented surface $\sigma$, and let $\hat{n}$ be the chosen unit normal field on $\sigma$. The flux of $\boldsymbol{F}$ across $\sigma$ is

$$
\Phi = \iint_\sigma \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S
$$

<Note>

A positive flux means more flow passes through $\sigma$ in the positive normal direction than the negative. A negative flux means the reverse. A zero flux means the 2 amounts balance.

</Note>

## Flux through a Parametric Surface

Let $\sigma$ be a smooth parametric surface $\boldsymbol{r} = \boldsymbol{r}(u,v)$, where $(u,v)$ varies over a region $R$ in the $uv$-plane. Suppose the component functions of $\boldsymbol{F}$ are continuous on $\sigma$ and that

$$
\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{r}}{\partial u} \times \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{r}}{\partial v}
$$

determines the chosen orientation of $\sigma$. Then

$$
\Phi = \iint_\sigma \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S = \iint_R \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{r}(u,v)) \cdot \left(\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{r}}{\partial u} \times \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{r}}{\partial v}\right) \text{d}A_{uv}
$$

## Flux through a Graph

Let $\sigma$ be a smooth surface of the form $z = g(x,y)$, $y = g(x,z)$, or $x = g(y,z)$, written as $G(x,y,z) = 0$ by moving all terms to the left side, with [orientation](/s5/calculus/vector-values-functions/orientation) determined by $\nabla G$. Let $R$ be the projection of $\sigma$ onto the coordinate plane determined by the independent variables of $g$. If $\boldsymbol{F}$ is continuous on $\sigma$,

$$
\Phi = \iint_\sigma \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S = \iint_R \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \nabla G\,\text{d}A
$$

<Note>

For the opposite orientation, $\Phi = -\displaystyle\iint_R \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \nabla G\,\text{d}A$.

$\boldsymbol{F}$ and $\nabla G$ are evaluated on $\sigma$, and $\text{d}A$ is the area element in the corresponding projection plane.

</Note>

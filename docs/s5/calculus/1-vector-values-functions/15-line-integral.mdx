Let $C$ be a smooth curve with parametric equations $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$, $z = z(t)$, $a \le t \le b$, and $f$ defined on $C$. Let $P = \{t_0, t_1, \dots, t_n\}$, $a = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = b$, be a partition of $[a,b]$, dividing $C$ into $n$ subarcs. Let $\Delta s_k$ be the length of the $k$th subarc, and $(x_k^*, y_k^*, z_k^*)$ a sample point on the $k$th subarc. The line integral of $f$ over $C$ with respect to arc length is

$$
\int_C f(x,y,z)\,\text{d}s = \lim_{\max\{\Delta s_k\} \to 0} \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(x_k^*, y_k^*, z_k^*)\,\Delta s_k
$$

provided the limit exists and does not depend on the choice of partition or sample points.

<Note>

The line integral exists if $f$ is continuous at each point of $C$.

</Note>

For $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j} + z(t)\hat{k}$, $a \le t \le b$,

$$
\text{d}s
= \lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert\,\text{d}t
= \sqrt{\left(\frac{\text{d}x}{\text{d}t}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}t}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\text{d}z}{\text{d}t}\right)^2}\,\text{d}t
$$

so

$$
\int_C f(x,y,z)\,\text{d}s = \int_a^b f(x(t),y(t),z(t))\,\lVert \boldsymbol{r}'(t) \rVert\,\text{d}t
$$

<Note>

If $C$ lies in the $xy$-plane and $z = f(x,y)$ is a non-negative continuous function on $C$, $\int_C f(x,y)\,\text{d}s$ is the area of the vertical sheet swept out by a line segment of height $f(x,y)$ as $(x,y)$ moves along $C$.

</Note>

## Line Integral of a Vector Field

Let $\boldsymbol{F}$ be a [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-values-functions/vector-field) and $C$ a piecewise smooth oriented curve parametrized by $\boldsymbol{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j} + z(t)\hat{k}$, $a \le t \le b$. The line integral of $\boldsymbol{F}$ along $C$ is

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \int_a^b \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{r}(t)) \cdot \boldsymbol{r}'(t)\,\text{d}t
$$

For $\boldsymbol{F} = P\hat{i} + Q\hat{j} + R\hat{k}$,

$$
\int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r} = \int_C P\,\text{d}x + Q\,\text{d}y + R\,\text{d}z
$$

### Work

For a particle moving along a smooth curve $C$, oriented in the direction of motion, under a continuous force field $\boldsymbol{F}$, the work done by $\boldsymbol{F}$ on the particle is

$$
W = \int_C \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \text{d}\boldsymbol{r}
$$

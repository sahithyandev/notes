### Closed Surface

A surface $\sigma$ is a closed surface **iff** it is the boundary of a solid region in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

For a closed surface, the positive orientation is the outward orientation.

## Statement

Let $\sigma$ be a closed, piecewise smooth, orientable surface that encloses a solid region $R$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Suppose $\boldsymbol{F}$ is a [vector field](/s5/calculus/vector-values-functions/vector-field) whose component functions have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open set containing $R$ and its boundary $\sigma$. If $\hat{n}$ is the outward unit normal field for $\sigma$,

$$
\iint_\sigma \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S = \iiint_R \text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}\,\text{d}V
$$

<Note>

If $\sigma$ is not closed, close it by adding the missing faces, then subtract their flux to recover the flux across the original surface.

</Note>

## Divergence as Flux Density

Let $G$ be a small solid region centered at $P_0$ with boundary surface $\sigma(G)$ oriented outward, and let $\boldsymbol{F}$ be a vector field whose component functions have continuous first-order partial derivatives on an open set containing $G$. Denote the volume of $G$ by $\text{Vol}(G)$ and the flux of $\boldsymbol{F}$ across $\sigma(G)$ by $\Phi(G)$.

$$
\Phi(G) = \iint_{\sigma(G)} \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S = \iiint_G \text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}\,\text{d}V \approx \text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}(P_0)\,\text{Vol}(G)
$$

As $G$ shrinks to $P_0$ in all directions,

$$
\text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}(P_0) = \lim_{G \to P_0} \frac{1}{\text{Vol}(G)} \iint_{\sigma(G)} \boldsymbol{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\text{d}S
$$

If $\boldsymbol{F}$ is the velocity field of a fluid in steady-state flow, $\text{div}\,\boldsymbol{F}$ is the limiting outward flux per unit volume at a point.

Security evaluation frameworks give independent assurance about a product or the process that built it. The 3 frameworks cover different targets and do not replace each other.

| Framework       | Evaluates                           |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Common Criteria | Security of an IT product or system |
| FIPS 140-3      | Cryptographic module                |
| SSE-CMM         | Maturity of engineering processes   |

A project needing both high cryptographic assurance and functional security validation combines FIPS 140-3 for the cryptographic module with a Common Criteria evaluation of the whole system.

## Common Criteria

Common Criteria (ISO/IEC 15408) evaluates the security functions of a product and the assurance gained in building it. An accredited third-party laboratory performs the evaluation, and results are recognized internationally under the CCRA.

### Terminology

- Target of Evaluation
  <Aka>TOE</Aka>. The product or system being evaluated.
- Protection Profile
  <Aka>PP</Aka>. An implementation-independent statement of security
  requirements for a category of products, such as next-generation firewalls or
  enterprise routers.
- Security Target
  <Aka>ST</Aka>. An implementation-dependent statement of what a specific TOE
  claims to provide.
- Evaluation Assurance Level
  <Aka>EAL</Aka>. The depth and rigor of the evaluation.

An ST that claims conformance to a PP must show strict compliance or demonstrable equivalence to it. A product that satisfies its own ST but whose ST omits requirements mandated by the applicable PP fails certification.

### Evaluation Assurance Levels

- EAL1  
  Functionally tested.
- EAL2  
  Structurally tested.
- EAL3  
  Methodically tested and checked.
- EAL4  
  Methodically designed, tested, and reviewed. The limit of economically feasible security retrofitted onto an existing product.
- EAL5  
  Semi-formally designed and tested. Security must be designed in from the start.
- EAL6  
  Semi-formally verified design and tested.
- EAL7  
  Formally verified design and tested.

A higher EAL means more assurance, not more security functions.

### Criticism

The process is slow and costly. A certified version is often obsolete by the time certification is granted.

## FIPS 140-3

FIPS 140-3 validates cryptographic modules. It checks that the algorithms are approved and correctly implemented, and that the module protects its keys. Common Criteria does not provide this.

- Level 1  
  Basic requirements. Approved algorithms and production-grade components. No physical security mechanisms.
- Level 2  
  Adds tamper evidence and role-based authentication.
- Level 3  
  Adds tamper resistance and response. The module detects physical tampering and zeroizes its keys. Identity-based authentication.
- Level 4  
  Adds a formal model of the module's state transitions and protection against environmental attacks and side-channel analysis such as differential power analysis.

## SSE-CMM

The Systems Security Engineering Capability Maturity Model (ISO/IEC 21827) rates the maturity of an organization's security engineering processes. It does not inspect the technical attributes of a specific product release.

- Level 1  
  Performed informally.
- Level 2  
  Planned and tracked.
- Level 3  
  Well defined.
- Level 4  
  Quantitatively controlled. The organization measures its processes against defined metrics.
- Level 5  
  Continuously improving.

High maturity makes engineering predictable and lowers systemic risk. It does not guarantee defect-free output, so a Level 5 organization can still ship a design flaw.

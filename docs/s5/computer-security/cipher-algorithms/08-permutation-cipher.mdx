Requires a block length $n$, typically small, and a permutation $\sigma \in S_n$ as the secret key.

$$
\sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 & 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Encrypts the plaintext HELLO as ciphertext LHLEO.

### Attacking Permutation Ciphers

A character frequency analysis matching the standard distribution exactly reveals that a substitution cipher, not a permutation cipher, was used, since permutation ciphers preserve single-character frequencies.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Find repeating character sequences aligned to the same block position, since these produce similar repeating sequences in the ciphertext.
- Compute the distance between 2 such repeating blocks, or the gcd across several distances, to reveal the block length.
- Once the block length is known, deduce the permutation by exhaustive search over $n!$ possibilities.

</Note>

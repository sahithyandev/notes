A5/1 is an [LFSR](/s5/computer-security/linear-feedback-shift-registers) based [stream cipher](/s5/computer-security/stream-ciphers) used to encrypt on-air mobile communication traffic in the GSM standard.

It combines 3 LFSRs $R_1$, $R_2$, $R_3$ of lengths 19, 22 and 23 bits. The keystream bit at each step is the XOR of the output bits of the 3 registers.

## Kerckhoffs's Principle Violation

The A5/1 design was kept secret from its creation in 1987 until 1999, violating [Kerckhoffs's principle](/s5/computer-security/kerckhoffs-principle).

- 1999  
  Marc Briceno reverse engineered the design.
- 2006  
  With the design public, Elad Barkan, Eli Biham and Nathan Keller showed how to break the cipher in real time.

## Registers

Each register is defined by a feedback polynomial.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_1 &: x^{19} + x^{18} + x^{17} + x^{14} + 1 \\
R_2 &: x^{22} + x^{21} + 1 \\
R_3 &: x^{23} + x^{22} + x^{21} + x^{8} + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Irregular Clocking

Each register has a clocking bit. At every step, the majority of the 3 clocking bits $c_1, c_2, c_3$ is computed.

$$
\text{maj} = (c_1 \cdot c_2) \oplus (c_2 \cdot c_3) \oplus (c_3 \cdot c_1)
$$

A register is clocked **iff** its clocking bit equals $\text{maj}$. At least 2 registers are clocked at every step.

| $c_1$ | $c_2$ | $c_3$ | $\text{maj}$ | $R_1$ | $R_2$ | $R_3$ |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ------------ | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0     | 0     | 0     | 0            | Y     | Y     | Y     |
| 0     | 0     | 1     | 0            | Y     | Y     | N     |
| 0     | 1     | 0     | 0            | Y     | N     | Y     |
| 0     | 1     | 1     | 1            | N     | Y     | Y     |
| 1     | 0     | 0     | 0            | N     | Y     | Y     |
| 1     | 0     | 1     | 1            | Y     | N     | Y     |
| 1     | 1     | 0     | 1            | Y     | Y     | N     |
| 1     | 1     | 1     | 1            | Y     | Y     | Y     |

Here Y means clocked and N means not clocked.

Whether a register advances depends on the other registers, so the keystream is not a linear function of the key.

An optical image forms when multiple light rays, originating from the same point, converge at a different point, creating contrast against the background.

- The object emits light, or more commonly reflects incident light from another source
- Light energy at the image depends on how much light the object reflects and the area of the lens surface

## Lens equation

$$
\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v}
$$

Here:

- $f$: focal distance of the lens
- $u$: distance to the object
- $v$: distance to the image

- A lens is a cut-out section of a spherical surface. Its curvature determines $f$.
- For a given $f$, exactly one point produces a real focused image
- Most practical images form between the optical center and the focal length
- The quantum of light entering the lens depends on the lens surface area it passes through
- Shorter focal lengths require sharper curves, reducing the lens surface area

## Depth of field

Depth of field is the distance between the nearest and furthest points in object space that produce a properly focused image in the image plane.

- Only one plane in the object region is at proper focus, per the lens equation
- Objects nearer or farther than this plane are slightly out of focus
- Since the sensor integrates total light, no difference is visible until the circle of confusion exceeds one sensor element
- This tolerance is what creates the depth of field that appears to have focus

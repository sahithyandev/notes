Compression reduces the volume of data required to represent an image, without necessarily reducing its information content. It works by removing [data redundancy](/s5/image-processing/entropy#data-redundancy).

- Lossless compression  
  Source and reconstructed image match pixel to pixel.
- Lossy compression  
  Source and reconstructed image may not match pixel to pixel.

## Compression Ratio

For the same information encoded with $n_1$ units of data under scheme $C_1$ and $n_2$ units under scheme $C_2$:

$$
CR = \frac{n_1}{n_2}
$$

Relative data redundancy of $C_2$ compared to $C_1$:

$$
RD = 1 - \frac{1}{CR}
$$

- $CR > 1$ means $C_2$ compresses relative to $C_1$
- $CR = 1$ means $RD = 0$ and no redundancy is removed
- $CR < 1$ means $RD < 0$ and $C_2$ expands the data

For an $M \times N$ image coded in $b$ bits:

$$
\text{bpp} = \frac{b}{MN}
\qquad
CR = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{\text{bpp}_1}{\text{bpp}_2}
$$

For example, $CR = 10$ gives $RD = 0.9$, so 90% of the original data is redundant.

## Reconstruction Error

A lossy scheme reconstructs $\hat{f}(x,y)$ from the original $f(x,y)$. The error measures below compare the 2 images.

$$
\begin{align}
e(x,y) &= \hat{f}(x,y) - f(x,y) \\
\text{MSE} &= \frac{1}{MN} \sum_{x=0}^{M-1} \sum_{y=0}^{N-1} e(x,y)^2 \\
e_\text{rms} &= \sqrt{\text{MSE}} \\
\text{PSNR} &= 10 \log_{10} \frac{\text{MAX}^2}{\text{MSE}} \text{ dB}
\end{align}
$$

A lossless scheme has $e(x,y) = 0$ for every pixel. See [image quality assessment](/s5/image-processing/image-quality-assessment) for PSNR and SSIM.

## Compression Pipeline

A compression system has an encoder and a decoder. The encoder is a chain of 4 stages. Each stage targets 1 kind of redundancy.

$$
\text{Mapper} \to \text{Quantizer} \to \text{Symbol encoder} \to \text{Channel encoder}
$$

Decompression runs the inverse of each stage in reverse order. The quantizer has no inverse, so a pipeline containing it is lossy.

### Mapper

Transforms the pixel array into a representation where [interpixel redundancy](/s5/image-processing/image-encoding#interpixel-redundancy) is exposed. The mapping is reversible and loses no information.

Examples:

- Run-length encoding, which replaces a run of equal pixels by the pixel value and the run length
- Predictive coding, which stores the difference between each pixel and its prediction from neighbours
- Transform coding, which stores coefficients of a transform such as the DCT

### Quantizer

Reduces the number of distinct values the mapper output can take, so fewer bits per value are needed. It removes [psychovisual redundancy](/s5/image-processing/image-encoding#psychovisual-redundancy) by discarding detail the eye barely perceives.

- Irreversible, so it is the only stage that makes the pipeline lossy
- Optional, and omitted in lossless compression
- Coarser quantization gives a higher $CR$ and a lower reconstruction quality

A uniform quantizer with step $\Delta$ is:

$$
\hat{v} = \Delta \cdot \text{round}\left(\frac{v}{\Delta}\right)
\qquad
|v - \hat{v}| \le \frac{\Delta}{2}
$$

With the error spread evenly over the step, the mean squared error is:

$$
\text{MSE} = \frac{\Delta^2}{12}
$$

### Symbol Encoder

Assigns a code to each quantized value. Short codes go to frequent values and long codes to rare values. It removes [coding redundancy](/s5/image-processing/entropy#average-code-length).

- Reversible
- The average code length approaches the entropy of the source

$$
B_\text{avg} \le L_\text{avg} < B_\text{avg} + 1
$$

Examples:

- Huffman coding
- Arithmetic coding
- LZW coding

### Channel Encoder

Adds controlled redundancy to the compressed bitstream. The decoder uses it to detect and correct errors from storage or transmission, since a compressed stream has little natural redundancy left to survive a bit error.

A code that turns $k$ data bits into $n$ transmitted bits has:

$$
R_c = \frac{k}{n}
\qquad
\text{overhead} = \frac{n - k}{k}
$$

Examples:

- Parity bit, with $n = k + 1$
- Hamming code, such as $(n, k) = (7, 4)$ with $R_c = \frac{4}{7}$

## Worked Example

### Uniform Quantization

A mapper coefficient is $v = 27$. A uniform quantizer has $\Delta = 8$. Find the reconstructed coefficient and its squared error.

<Solution>

$$
\hat{v} = 8 \cdot \operatorname{round}\left(\frac{27}{8}\right) = 8 \cdot 3 = 24
$$

$$
(v - \hat{v})^2 = (27 - 24)^2 = 9
$$

The quantizer reconstructs $24$ with squared error $9$.

</Solution>

An image is modeled as a function $I(x,y)$ returning the light intensity at position $(x,y)$.

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="x, y">spatial coordinates of a point in the image</Sym>
  <Sym s="I(x,y)">
    light intensity at that point, bounded between a minimum and maximum
  </Sym>
</Symbols>

## Analog Image Model

$x, y$ are continuous and range over a bounded region. $I$ is defined at every point of that region and varies continuously.

Properties:

- Infinite spatial resolution  
  A value exists between any two points.
- Continuous intensity  
  No fixed set of allowed values.
- Not directly computable  
  Needs a discrete representation before a computer can store or process it.

Examples:

- Photographic film
- Image projected on the retina
- Analog video signal

## Digital Image Model

A digital image is a discrete representation of an image, with a finite number of positions and a fixed number of intensity values.

- Finite grid of pixels  
  The spatial domain is an $m \times n$ grid. Each cell $(i,j)$ covers a small finite region.
- Fixed number of levels  
  The intensity of each cell is one of $L$ discrete levels.

$I(i,j)$ is the intensity of cell $(i,j)$, one of the $L$ levels. The digital image is the matrix of these values. Each element is a pixel.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
I(0,0) & I(0,1) & \cdots & I(0,n-1) \\
I(1,0) & I(1,1) & \cdots & I(1,n-1) \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
I(m-1,0) & I(m-1,1) & \cdots & I(m-1,n-1)
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Here:

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="m">number of rows</Sym>
  <Sym s="n">number of columns</Sym>
  <Sym s="i">row index, $0 \le i \le m-1$</Sym>
  <Sym s="j">column index, $0 \le j \le n-1$</Sym>
  <Sym s="L">
    number of intensity levels, usually $L = 2^k$ for a bit depth $k$
  </Sym>
</Symbols>

$m \times n$ is the spatial resolution. An 8-bit grayscale image has $L = 256$ levels, $0$ for black and $255$ for white.

Storage size in bits is $m \times n \times k$ for a single channel. A color image repeats this per channel.

In a computer program, an image is simply a matrix of integer values. Image processing means manipulating this matrix.

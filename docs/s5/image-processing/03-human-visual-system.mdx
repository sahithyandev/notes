The human eye has about 13 million photo-receptor cells sensitive to light. Cones are sensitive to color and come in 3 types (L, M, S). Rods are sensitive only to brightness.

Each cell produces an electro-chemical signal when exposed to light. Signals are sent to the brain's visual cortex for processing. The image is perceived through a complex network of neurons in the visual cortex.

### Mechanical Properties

- Focusing  
  The eye focuses on objects from nearby to infinity by changing the shape of the biological lens.
- Light adaptation  
  Each photo-receptor is sensitive only to a narrow intensity region. The pupil dilates or contracts to admit the amount of light the photo-receptors are most sensitive to.

### Perceived Brightness

Perceived brightness is not a simple function of light intensity.

- Simultaneous contrast  
  A region's perceived brightness depends on the background intensity, not only its own intensity.
- Edge sensitivity  
  The eye is more sensitive to edges, so perceived brightness tends to undershoot or overshoot at sharp brightness differences.

<Note>

Optical illusions arise from these properties of the human visual system, not from the physical image itself.

</Note>

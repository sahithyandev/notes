Accounting is the process of identifying, measuring, recording, and communicating financial information about an entity to interested users. It emphasizes accuracy, transparency, and relevance to support management and control of resources.

**The American Institute of Certified Public Accountants** defines it as:

The art of recording, classification and summarizing in a significant manner and in terms of money, transactions and events which are, in part at least, of a financial character, and interpreting the results thereof.

## Objective and need

- Know whether the business has earned an adequate profit.
- Know whether the financial position of the business is sound.
- Communicate financial information to users.
- Support decision-making and resource management.
- Attract investors.
- Fulfill statutory reporting requirements.
- Minimize errors and omissions in recording transactions.
- Track project expenditures.

## Users of accounting information

Internal users  
Engineers, managers, and project teams, for budget control and performance monitoring.

External users  
Investors, creditors, and regulatory bodies, for assessing financial health and compliance.

| Stakeholder                  | Purpose                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Owners / shareholders        | Profitability, return on investment, dividend potential      |
| Managers                     | Budgeting, cost control, pricing, performance evaluation     |
| Employees / trade unions     | Job security, salary negotiations, retirement benefits       |
| Investors                    | Investment decisions based on profitability and risk         |
| Creditors                    | Ability to repay loans before granting credit                |
| Suppliers                    | Ability to pay for goods and services on time                |
| Customers                    | Long-term stability for warranty and service purposes        |
| Government / tax authorities | Tax liability and regulatory compliance                      |
| Regulatory authorities       | Compliance with reporting standards and corporate governance |
| Competitors                  | Benchmarking financial performance and market position       |
| General public               | Economic contribution, employment, environmental impact      |

## Branches of accounting

Financial accounting  
Recording business transactions to determine the operating result of a period and the financial position on a given date.

Cost accounting  
Collection, classification, and ascertainment of the cost of production, including tracking and controlling project costs, materials, and labor.

Management accounting  
Uses financial and cost accounting data for policy formulation, planning, control, and decision-making by management.

## Generally accepted accounting principles

- Business entity concept  
  The business is a distinct accounting entity, separate from its owners.
- Money measurement concept  
  Only information expressible in monetary terms is recorded.
- Historical cost concept  
  Assets are shown at cost, not current market value.
- Periodic concept  
  Income or loss is measured over a fixed accounting period, e.g. calendar year (1 Jan to 31 Dec) or financial year (1 April to 31 March).
- Matching concept  
  Expenses incurred in a period are matched with the revenue earned in that period.
- Materiality concept  
  Information is material **iff** its misstatement could influence the economic decisions of users.
- Going concern concept  
  The business is assumed to continue operating for the foreseeable future.
- Consistency concept  
  Accounting methods are applied uniformly across periods.
- Accrual concept  
  Accounts are prepared on an accrual basis by default, though some entities use cash basis.
- Realization concept  
  Revenue is realized when goods or services are actually delivered.
- Prudence concept  
  Assets or profits are not overstated, and losses are not understated.

<Note>

Realization concept example: a company contracted to deliver a machine recognizes revenue at the point of delivery, not at the point of contract signing.

Prudence concept examples:

- Recognize provision for trade receivable impairment.
- Recognize stock losses.
- Recognize gratuity provision expenses.

</Note>

## Why engineers need accounting

- Effective budget management on projects.
- Better cost estimation and control.
- Enhanced communication with finance teams.
- Informed decision-making on investments.
- Optimized resource allocation.

## Accounting process

- Identification  
  Recognize and analyze financial transactions, preparing source documents to extract financial information.
- Classification  
  Group similar transactions into categories, posted in the general ledger.
- Recording  
  Document transactions systematically in journals and ledgers.
- Summarization  
  Prepare trial balances and financial statements.
- Interpretation  
  Analyze financial data to support decision-making, transforming raw financial information into actionable insight.

## Elements of financial statements

- Income  
  Increases in assets, or decreases in liabilities, that increase equity, excluding contributions from equity holders.
- Expense  
  Decreases in assets, or increases in liabilities, that decrease equity, excluding distributions to equity holders.
- Assets  
  A present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events, where the resource is a right with potential to produce economic benefits.
- Liabilities  
  A present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events.
- Equity  
  The residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities.

## Basic principles of accounting

The accounting equation:

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}
$$

Double-entry bookkeeping  
Every transaction affects at least 2 accounts, a debit and a credit, ensuring the equation stays balanced.

Accrual vs cash basis  
Accrual records revenue and expenses when earned or incurred. Cash basis records them only when cash changes hands.

For each account type, an increase is recorded as a debit or credit as follows:

| Account   | Increase | Decrease |
| --------- | -------- | -------- |
| Asset     | Debit    | Credit   |
| Expense   | Debit    | Credit   |
| Equity    | Credit   | Debit    |
| Liability | Credit   | Debit    |
| Income    | Credit   | Debit    |

<Note title="Extended accounting equation">

$$
\text{Assets} + \text{Expenses} = \text{Capital} + \text{Income} + \text{Liability}
$$

Derived progressively:

- $\text{Assets} = \text{Capital}$
- $\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Liability}$
- $\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + \text{Profit} + \text{Liability}$
- $\text{Assets} = \text{Capital} + (\text{Income} - \text{Expenses}) + \text{Liability}$
- $\text{Assets} + \text{Expenses} = \text{Capital} + \text{Income} + \text{Liability}$

</Note>

The statement of cash flows reports cash and cash-equivalent movements under operating, investing, and financing activities.

## Activity Types

### Operating Activities

Cash flows from selling goods or services and paying the costs of running the business. They exclude the purchase or sale of long-term assets and changes in equity or borrowings.

Operating inflows include:

- Cash received from customers
- Commission, royalty, and fee income received
- Interest received when classified as operating

Operating outflows include:

- Cash paid to suppliers
- Wages and salaries paid
- Rent, electricity, and other operating expenses paid
- Interest and income taxes paid when classified as operating

Use operating activities when the cash flow would occur as part of the entity's ordinary revenue-producing work.

### Investing Activities

Cash flows from buying or selling resources that the entity will use for more than 1 accounting period, or from lending money to another entity.

Investing inflows include:

- Proceeds from selling land, buildings, or equipment
- Proceeds from selling long-term investments
- Repayment of a loan advanced to another entity

Investing outflows include:

- Purchase of land, buildings, or equipment
- Purchase of long-term investments
- Loan advanced to another entity

The purchase of equipment is investing even when the equipment is used in ordinary operations. The cash flow is classified by what was purchased, not by how the asset will be used.

### Financing Activities

Cash flows that change the amount of equity contributed by owners or the amount borrowed by the entity.

Financing inflows include:

- Cash received from issuing ordinary shares
- Cash received from obtaining a bank loan
- Cash received from issuing bonds

Financing outflows include:

- Repayment of loan principal
- Dividends paid
- Repurchase of the entity's own shares

Loan interest is not loan principal. Under the policy used in this note, interest paid is operating. The principal repayment is financing.

### Non-Cash Transactions

Non-cash investing and financing transactions do not enter the cash-flow totals.

Examples include:

- Equipment acquired through a finance lease
- A loan converted into ordinary shares
- An asset acquired by issuing shares

Disclose material non-cash transactions separately.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Closing Cash}
&= \text{Opening Cash} \\
&+ \text{Operating Cash Flow} \\
&+ \text{Investing Cash Flow} \\
&+ \text{Financing Cash Flow}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Direct Method

The direct method lists major operating cash receipts and payments. It starts with cash collected and cash paid instead of profit.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cash from Customers}
&= \text{Sales} \\
&+ \text{Opening Receivables} \\
&- \text{Closing Receivables}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Purchases}
&= \text{Cost of Sales} \\
&+ \text{Closing Inventory} \\
&- \text{Opening Inventory}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cash to Suppliers}
&= \text{Purchases} \\
&+ \text{Opening Payables} \\
&- \text{Closing Payables}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Indirect Method

The indirect method starts with profit before tax and reconciles it to operating cash flow.

Adjustments follow these rules:

- Add non-cash expenses such as depreciation.
- Deduct gains on disposal of non-current assets.
- Add losses on disposal of non-current assets.
- Deduct increases in operating current assets.
- Add decreases in operating current assets.
- Add increases in operating current liabilities.
- Deduct decreases in operating current liabilities.

The direct and indirect methods produce the same operating cash flow. They differ only in presentation.

## Worked Examples

### Cash-Flow Classification

Classify each transaction as operating, investing, financing, or non-cash.

1. Cash received from customers: 80,000.
2. Equipment purchased for cash: 25,000.
3. Shares issued for cash: 40,000.
4. Loan principal repaid: 12,000.
5. Equipment acquired through a finance lease: 30,000.
6. Wages paid: 18,000.

<Solution>

1. Operating inflow: 80,000.
2. Investing outflow: 25,000.
3. Financing inflow: 40,000.
4. Financing outflow: 12,000.
5. Non-cash investing and financing transaction: 30,000. Disclose separately.
6. Operating outflow: 18,000.

</Solution>

### Direct Method: Cash from Customers

Sales were 240,000. Opening trade receivables were 36,000. Closing trade receivables were 52,000. Calculate cash received from customers.

<Solution>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cash from Customers}
&= 240{,}000 + 36{,}000 - 52{,}000 \\
&= 224{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Cash received from customers is 224,000.

</Solution>

### Direct Method: Purchases and Suppliers

Cost of sales was 150,000. Opening inventory was 30,000. Closing inventory was 42,000. Opening trade payables were 28,000. Closing trade payables were 20,000. Calculate purchases and cash paid to suppliers.

<Solution>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Purchases}
&= 150{,}000 + 42{,}000 - 30{,}000 \\
&= 162{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cash to Suppliers}
&= 162{,}000 + 28{,}000 - 20{,}000 \\
&= 170{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Purchases are 162,000. Cash paid to suppliers is 170,000.

</Solution>

### Direct Method: Cash Paid for Expenses

Wages expense was 48,000. Opening wages payable were 6,000. Closing wages payable were 9,000. Rent expense was 20,000. Opening prepaid rent was 3,000. Closing prepaid rent was 5,000. Calculate cash paid for wages and rent.

<Solution>

Wages:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cash Paid for Wages}
&= 48{,}000 + 6{,}000 - 9{,}000 \\
&= 45{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Rent:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cash Paid for Rent}
&= 20{,}000 + 5{,}000 - 3{,}000 \\
&= 22{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Cash paid for wages is 45,000. Cash paid for rent is 22,000.

</Solution>

### Direct Method: Operating Cash Flow

Calculate net cash from operating activities.

- Cash from customers  
  224,000
- Cash to suppliers  
  170,000
- Cash paid to employees  
  24,000
- Cash paid for other expenses  
  12,000
- Interest paid  
  4,000
- Income taxes paid  
  6,000

<Solution>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Operating Cash Flow}
&= 224{,}000 - 170{,}000 - 24{,}000 \\
&\quad - 12{,}000 - 4{,}000 - 6{,}000 \\
&= 8{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Net cash from operating activities is 8,000.

</Solution>

### Indirect Method: Operating Cash Flow

Profit before tax was 96,000. Depreciation was 14,000. A gain on disposal of equipment was 5,000. Receivables increased by 11,000. Inventory decreased by 7,000. Payables increased by 9,000. Accrued operating expenses decreased by 3,000. Calculate net cash from operating activities.

<Solution>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Operating Cash Flow}
&= 96{,}000 + 14{,}000 - 5{,}000 - 11{,}000 \\
&\quad + 7{,}000 + 9{,}000 - 3{,}000 \\
&= 107{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Net cash from operating activities is 107,000.

</Solution>

### Indirect Method: Full Statement

Prepare a statement of cash flows using the indirect method.

- Profit before tax  
  120,000
- Depreciation  
  20,000
- Gain on sale of equipment  
  8,000
- Increase in receivables  
  15,000
- Increase in inventory  
  6,000
- Increase in payables  
  10,000
- Equipment purchased for cash  
  75,000
- Equipment-sale proceeds  
  22,000
- New loan  
  50,000
- Loan principal repaid  
  18,000
- Dividends paid  
  12,000
- Opening cash  
  40,000

<Solution>

Operating activities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Operating Cash Flow}
&= 120{,}000 + 20{,}000 - 8{,}000 - 15{,}000 \\
&\quad - 6{,}000 + 10{,}000 \\
&= 121{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Investing activities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Investing Cash Flow}
&= 22{,}000 - 75{,}000 \\
&= -53{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Financing activities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Financing Cash Flow}
&= 50{,}000 - 18{,}000 - 12{,}000 \\
&= 20{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Cash reconciliation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Net Change in Cash}
&= 121{,}000 - 53{,}000 + 20{,}000 \\
&= 88{,}000 \\
\text{Closing Cash}
&= 40{,}000 + 88{,}000 \\
&= 128{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

| Section                  |   Cash flow |
| :----------------------- | ----------: |
| Operating activities     |     121,000 |
| Investing activities     |    (53,000) |
| Financing activities     |      20,000 |
| **Net increase in cash** |  **88,000** |
| Opening cash             |      40,000 |
| **Closing cash**         | **128,000** |

</Solution>

### Missing Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was 65,000. Investing cash flow was -42,000. Opening cash was 28,000. Closing cash was 71,000. Calculate financing cash flow.

<Solution>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Financing Cash Flow}
&= 71{,}000 - 28{,}000 - 65{,}000 - (-42{,}000) \\
&= 20{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Financing cash flow is 20,000.

</Solution>

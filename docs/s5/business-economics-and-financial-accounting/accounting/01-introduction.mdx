Accounting records financial transactions in money terms, summarizes them, and explains what the results show.

Example: a shop records a $500 sale, groups it with other sales, and compares the total with its costs to calculate profit.

## Objectives

- Know whether the business has earned an adequate profit.
- Know whether the financial position of the business is sound.
- Communicate financial information to users.
- Support decision-making and resource management.
- Attract investors.
- Fulfill statutory reporting requirements.
- Minimize errors and omissions in recording transactions.
- Track project expenditures.

### For Engineers

Engineers must be familiar with accounting concepts to:

- Manage project budgets effectively.
- Estimate and control costs more accurately.
- Communicate more clearly with finance teams.
- Make informed investment decisions.
- Allocate resources efficiently.

## Users of Accounting Information

### Internal Users

Parties within the entity who use accounting information for day-to-day management and control.

- Owners / shareholders  
  Assess profitability, return on investment, and dividend potential.
- Managers  
  Use it for budgeting, cost control, pricing, and performance evaluation.
- Employees / trade unions  
  Judge job security, negotiate salaries, and evaluate retirement benefits.

### External Users

Parties outside the entity who use accounting information to make decisions about their relationship with it.

- Investors  
  Base investment decisions on profitability and risk.
- Creditors  
  Assess ability to repay loans before granting credit.
- Suppliers  
  Assess ability to pay for goods and services on time.
- Customers  
  Judge long-term stability for warranty and service purposes.
- Government / tax authorities  
  Determine tax liability and regulatory compliance.
- Regulatory authorities  
  Verify compliance with reporting standards and corporate governance.
- Competitors  
  Benchmark financial performance and market position.
- General public  
  Gauge economic contribution, employment, and environmental impact.

## Branches

### Financial Accounting

Recording business transactions to determine the operating result of a period and the financial position on a given date, as stewardship for the benefit of shareholders. Mandatory, audited externally, and looks only at the past.

- Outputs  
  Summary annual statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, statement of financial position, and statement of cash flow, from historical transactions, for a fixed period, usually the financial year (aka. fiscal year).
- Users  
  Stakeholders, creditors, tax authorities, regulators.
- Basis  
  Standards such as IFRS or GAAP, fixing format, terminology, and recognition rules, so an external user can compare 2 companies' statements directly.

### Cost Accounting

Collection, classification, and ascertainment of the cost of production, including tracking and controlling project costs, materials, and labor.

- Outputs  
  Cost sheets and cost reports, per product, job, or process, tracked continuously as production happens rather than tied to a fixed period.
- Users  
  Production and operations managers.
- Basis  
  Historical cost and predetermined standard cost, the latter used to spot variances against actual cost. Feeds cost data into management accounting for pricing and efficiency decisions.

### Management Accounting

Uses financial and cost accounting data for policy formulation, planning, control, and decision-making by management, to improve the economy, efficiency, and effectiveness of operations. Looks at the past, present, and future.

Example: a manager compares the contribution from 2 products before deciding which product should receive scarce machine time.

- Outputs  
  Detailed monthly and annual accounts by product and function, plus adhoc reports, forward-looking, with no fixed reporting cycle.
- Users  
  Management, at all levels of the organization.
- Basis  
  Combines historical data with projections and estimates. Not governed by external reporting standards, so report format is chosen freely to fit the decision at hand.

Management accounting activities include:

- Explain production and non-production costs.
- Compute the cost of a service or product.
- Analyze how costs change with activity levels.
- Analyze cost-volume-profit relationships.
- Support profit planning and budgeting.
- Compare actual results with budgets and standard costs.
- Present relevant data for management decisions.

<Note>

Cost and management accounting are voluntary, adopted for internal decision-making.

</Note>

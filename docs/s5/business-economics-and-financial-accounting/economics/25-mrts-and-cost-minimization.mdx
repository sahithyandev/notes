## Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution

<Aka>MRTS</Aka>. The rate at which one input can be substituted for another
while keeping output constant. Slope of an [isoquant](./isoquant).

Example: moving from 10 to 11 workers while keeping output fixed may allow a reduction from 5 to 4 machines. The MRTS is 1 machine per extra worker.

For labour and capital:

<Formula>

$$
MRTS_{LK} = -\frac{\Delta K}{\Delta L}
$$

</Formula>

Using marginal products:

<Formula>

$$
MRTS_{LK} = -\frac{MP_L}{MP_K}
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="MP_L">Marginal product of labour</Sym>
  <Sym s="MP_K">Marginal product of capital</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Marginal Products Using Calculus

The marginal product of labour is:

<Formula>

$$
MP_L = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial L}
$$

</Formula>

The marginal product of capital is:

<Formula>

$$
MP_K = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial K}
$$

</Formula>

Therefore:

<Formula>

$$
MRTS_{LK} = -\frac{MP_L}{MP_K}
$$

</Formula>

## Diminishing MRTS

For a convex isoquant, MRTS decreases as more labour is substituted for capital.

This means that as the firm uses more labour and less capital, it becomes willing to give up less capital for an additional unit of labour.

## Cost Minimization

A firm wants to produce a given level of output at the lowest possible cost.

The firm chooses the combination of inputs that produces the required output at minimum cost.

### Cost-Minimizing Condition

At the cost-minimizing input combination:

<Formula>

$$
\frac{MP_L}{MP_K} = \frac{P_L}{P_K}
$$

</Formula>

Equivalently:

<Formula>

$$
MRTS_{LK} = \frac{P_L}{P_K}
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="P_L">Price of labour</Sym>
  <Sym s="P_K">Price of capital</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Worked Example

Tabs (Pvt.) Ltd. has $Q = 150L^{0.5}K^{0.5}$, $P_L = 1{,}500$, $P_K = 4{,}500$, and target output $Q = 1{,}200$. Find the cost-minimizing input combination and minimum cost.

<Solution>

The input price ratio is:

$$
\frac{P_L}{P_K} = \frac{1{,}500}{4{,}500} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

For this production function:

$$
MRTS_{LK} = \frac{MP_L}{MP_K} = \frac{K}{L}
$$

At the cost-minimizing point:

$$
\frac{K}{L} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

The production target gives:

$$
1{,}200 = 150\sqrt{LK}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad LK = 64
$$

Substitute $K = L/3$:

$$
L\left(\frac{L}{3}\right) = 64
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L = 8\sqrt{3} \approx 13.86
$$

$$
K = \frac{8}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 4.62
$$

The minimum cost is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\min} &= 1{,}500(8\sqrt{3}) + 4{,}500\left(\frac{8}{\sqrt{3}}\right) \\
&= 24{,}000\sqrt{3} \\
&\approx 41{,}569.22
\end{aligned}
$$

The cost-minimizing combination is approximately 13.86 units of labour and 4.62 units of capital. The minimum cost is approximately Rs. 41,569.22.

</Solution>

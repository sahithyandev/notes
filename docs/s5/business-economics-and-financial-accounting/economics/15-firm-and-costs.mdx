## The Firm

A firm is an institution that hires factors of production and organizes them to produce and sell goods and services.

The main goal of a firm is to maximize profit.

$$
\text{Profit} = \text{Total Revenue} - \text{Total Cost}
$$

## Types of Costs

### Explicit Costs

Costs that involve an actual monetary payment.

Examples:

- Wages
- Raw materials
- Rent
- Interest on borrowed funds

### Implicit Costs

Non-monetary opportunity costs of using resources owned by the firm or owner.

Examples:

- Salary the owner gives up by running the business
- Interest the owner gives up by using personal savings

## Economic Profit and Accounting Profit

### Accounting Profit

$$
\text{Accounting Profit}
=
\text{Total Revenue} - \text{Explicit Costs}
$$

### Economic Profit

$$
\text{Economic Profit}
=
\text{Total Revenue}
-
(\text{Explicit Costs} + \text{Implicit Costs})
$$

Economic profit is less than or equal to accounting profit because it includes implicit costs.

When economic profit is zero, the firm earns a normal profit.

## Sunk Costs

A [sunk cost](/s5/business-economics-and-financial-accounting/accounting/cost-terms#sunk-cost) has already been incurred and cannot be recovered.

A sunk cost does not affect a firm's current decision. Only future costs are compared with expected revenue.

## Shutdown Rule

In the short run, [fixed costs](/s5/business-economics-and-financial-accounting/accounting/cost-classification#fixed-cost) are sunk. They are paid whether the firm operates or not.

A firm should shut down **iff** total revenue is less than [total variable cost](/s5/business-economics-and-financial-accounting/accounting/cost-classification#variable-cost).

Total cost is the wrong comparison, since it includes fixed costs that shutting down does not avoid.

## Worked Examples

### Accounting and Economic Profit

Akash sells 1,000 bottles at Rs. 100 each. Raw materials cost Rs. 20,000. Capital of Rs. 100,000 is half his own savings and half borrowed at 10% interest. He could instead lend his savings at 10% or work elsewhere for Rs. 40,000. Find the accounting profit and the economic profit.

<Solution>

$$
\text{Revenue} = 1000 \times 100 = 100000
$$

Explicit costs are the raw materials and the loan interest.

$$
\text{Explicit Costs} = 20000 + 0.10 \times 50000 = 25000
$$

$$
\text{Accounting Profit} = 100000 - 25000 = 75000
$$

Implicit costs are the forgone wage and the forgone interest on his own savings.

$$
\text{Implicit Costs} = 40000 + 0.10 \times 50000 = 45000
$$

$$
\text{Economic Profit} = 100000 - 25000 - 45000 = 30000
$$

The accounting profit is Rs. 75,000 and the economic profit is Rs. 30,000.

</Solution>

### Closing Time

When should a grocery store close at night?

- Total costs of staying open are less than total revenue
- Total costs of staying open are greater than total revenue
- Variable costs of staying open are less than total revenue
- Variable costs of staying open are greater than total revenue

<Solution>

The store should close when the variable costs of staying open are greater than total revenue.

Fixed costs are sunk and are paid whether the store is open or closed. Comparing total costs with revenue is the trap, since it includes those fixed costs.

</Solution>

### Completing a Project

An ice-cream hut has already cost Rs. 1,000. Another Rs. 300 is needed to finish it. Expected sales are now Rs. 800. Find the remaining cost below which completing the hut is worthwhile.

<Solution>

The Rs. 1,000 is a sunk cost and is ignored.

Completing is worthwhile while the remaining cost is below the expected revenue of Rs. 800.

At a remaining cost of Rs. 300, the net gain is Rs. 500.

</Solution>

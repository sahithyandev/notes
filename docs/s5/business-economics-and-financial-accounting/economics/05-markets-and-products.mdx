## Market

A market is a set of arrangements through which buyers and sellers
interact to exchange goods and services.

### Types of Markets

- **Physical Market** – buyers, sellers, and goods/services are in physical contact.
- **Intermediate Market** – markets such as the share market.
- **Super Market** – prices are generally fixed.
- **Electronic Market** – buying and selling through the internet.
- **Tele Market** – transactions through telephone, video, fax, etc.
- **Auction Market** – prices are determined through bidding.
- **Differentiated Market** – products differ in quality, features, or customer preferences.
- **Homogeneous Market** – products are similar and generally sold at similar prices.
- **Geographical Market** – markets defined by a particular region, city, rural area, etc.

## Types of Products

### Normal Products

Quantity demanded increases when consumer income increases and
decreases when income decreases.

### Inferior Products

Quantity demanded decreases when consumer income increases and
increases when income decreases.

### Giffen Products

A special type of inferior product where, under certain conditions,
an increase in price can cause quantity demanded to increase,
resulting in an upward-sloping demand curve.

### Veblen Products

Luxury products for which a higher price may increase demand because
the high price can signal status or exclusivity.

For example: luxury jewellery.

### Substitute Products

Goods that can be used instead of one another.

For example: tea and coffee.

If the price of one substitute increases, demand for the other
may increase.

### Complementary Products

Goods that are normally consumed together.

For example: cars and petrol.

If the price of one complementary good increases, demand for the
other may decrease.

### Independent Products

Goods whose consumption does not significantly depend on the
consumption of another good.

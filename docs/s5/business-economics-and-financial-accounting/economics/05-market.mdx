A market is a set of arrangements through which buyers and sellers interact to exchange goods and services. The exchange of goods or services, with or without money, is called a transaction.

Markets are interrelated. Changes in one affects another.

## Types of Markets

- Physical Market  
  Buyers, sellers, and goods/services are in physical contact.
- Intermediate Market  
  Markets such as the share market.
- Super Market  
  Prices are generally fixed.
- Electronic Market  
  Buying and selling through the internet.
- Tele Market  
  Transactions through telephone, video, fax, etc.
- Auction Market  
  Prices are determined through bidding.
- Differentiated Market  
  Products differ in quality, features, or customer preferences.
- Homogeneous Market  
  Products are similar and generally sold at similar prices.
- Geographical Market  
  Markets defined by a particular region, city, rural area, etc.

## Narrowly Defined Market

A market defined with a specific product and close substitutes.

Example: "iPhone 16" or "organic Fuji apples".

## Broadly Defined Market

A market defined by a general product category.

Example: "smartphones" or "fruit".

## Market Structures

A market structure is the set of characteristics that determine the level of competition and pricing power within a market.

### Competition

Competition is the effort of firms to attract customers and achieve their objectives.

The 4 main market structures are:

1. Perfect Competition
2. Monopolistic Competition
3. Oligopoly
4. Monopoly

### Perfect and Imperfect Competition

#### Perfect Competition

A market with:

- Many buyers and sellers
- Identical products
- Well-informed buyers and sellers
- Free entry and exit

Firms are price takers.

#### Imperfect Competition

Markets where firms have some control over price because of factors such as product differentiation or a small number of competitors.

Includes:

- Monopolistic Competition
- Oligopoly
- Monopoly

### Barriers to Entry

Barriers to entry are factors that make it difficult for new firms to enter a market.

Examples:

- High start-up costs
- Technological requirements
- Patents
- Control over important resources
- Legal restrictions

## Worked Examples

### Identifying a Market Structure

A market has many small firms selling homogeneous products. Identify its market structure.

<Solution>

The market is perfectly competitive. Many small firms are price takers selling identical products.

Monopolistic competition also has many firms, but its products are differentiated.

</Solution>

A market is a set of arrangements through which buyers and sellers interact to exchange goods and services.

Markets are interrelated. Changes in one affects another.

## Types of Markets

- Physical Market  
  Buyers, sellers, and goods/services are in physical contact.
- Intermediate Market  
  Markets such as the share market.
- Super Market  
  Prices are generally fixed.
- Electronic Market  
  Buying and selling through the internet.
- Tele Market  
  Transactions through telephone, video, fax, etc.
- Auction Market  
  Prices are determined through bidding.
- Differentiated Market  
  Products differ in quality, features, or customer preferences.
- Homogeneous Market  
  Products are similar and generally sold at similar prices.
- Geographical Market  
  Markets defined by a particular region, city, rural area, etc.

## Narrowly Defined Market

A market defined with a specific product and close substitutes.

Example: "iPhone 16" or "organic Fuji apples".

- Fewer substitutes within the definition.  
  Demand tends to be more elastic; consumers easily switch to close alternatives.
- Small price changes cause large quantity shifts.

## Broadly Defined Market

A market defined by a general product category.

Example: "smartphones" or "fruit".

- More substitutes are outside the definition.  
  Demand tends to be less elastic; fewer alternatives within the category.
- Price changes cause smaller quantity responses.

The same good can be in a narrow or broad market; elasticity differs accordingly.

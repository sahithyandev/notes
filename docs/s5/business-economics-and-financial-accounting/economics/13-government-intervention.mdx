Government intervention is action taken by the state to influence market outcomes and resource allocation, overriding the free interaction of supply and demand.

## Taxation

An indirect tax increases production costs and shifts the supply curve
upward by the amount of the tax.

The tax burden splits between consumers and producers based on
relative price elasticities.

<Formula>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Consumer burden} &= \frac{E_s}{E_d + E_s} \times \text{tax} \\
\text{Producer burden} &= \frac{E_d}{E_d + E_s} \times \text{tax}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Formula>

Here:

- $E_d$  
  Price elasticity of demand (absolute value).
- $E_s$  
  Price elasticity of supply.

When demand is more elastic ($E_d > E_s$):

- Quantity demanded falls significantly.
- Government tax revenue is relatively lower.
- Producers bear a larger share of the tax burden.
- Consumers bear a smaller share of the tax burden.

## Subsidies

A subsidy is a government payment to producers (or consumers) that lowers the per-unit cost of production or the effective price paid. It shifts the supply curve downward (or to the right) by the amount of the subsidy. With a per-unit subsidy $s$, producers receive $P_p = P_c + s$ where $P_c$ is the price consumers pay.

The subsidy benefit splits between consumers and producers based on relative price elasticities.

When demand is more elastic ($E_d > E_s$):

- Quantity demanded increases significantly.
- Producers receive a larger share of the subsidy benefit.
- Consumers receive a smaller share through a lower price.

## Price Controls

Price controls are government restrictions on market prices.

### Price Floor

A minimum price set above the equilibrium price. To protect producers.

Examples:

- Minimum wage
- Agricultural price supports

Effects:

- Price is kept above equilibrium.
- Quantity supplied increases.
- Quantity demanded decreases.
- A surplus is created.  
  $Q_s > Q_d$

### Price Ceiling

A maximum price set below the equilibrium price. To protect consumers from high prices.

Examples:

- Rent controls
- Price caps on essential goods

Effects:

- Price is kept below equilibrium.
- Quantity demanded increases.
- Quantity supplied decreases.
- A shortage is created.  
  $ Q_d > Q_s $

At a price ceiling, only $Q_s$ units are traded. The demand price $P_d$ is the highest price consumers are willing to pay for $Q_s$, read off the demand curve at that quantity.

<Definition name="Deadweight Loss">

The loss of total surplus from mutually beneficial trades that no longer take place. Under a price ceiling it is the triangle between the supply and demand curves, from $Q_s$ to $Q^*$:

$$
\text{DWL} = \frac{1}{2} \times (Q^* - Q_s) \times (P_d - P_c)
$$

</Definition>

## Worked Examples

### Taxation Example

Market for cigarettes. Demand: $Q_d = 100 - 2P$. Supply: $Q_s = 20 + 3P$.
Per-unit tax: $t = 10$.

Find the equilibrium price and quantity before and after the tax, and how the tax burden splits between consumers and producers.

<Solution>

<StepByStep>

<StepItem label="Equilibrium before tax">

$$
100 - 2P = 20 + 3P \implies 5P = 80 \implies P^* = 16,\quad Q^* = 68
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Supply with tax">

$$
Q_s = 20 + 3(P - 10) = 3P - 10
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="New equilibrium">

$$
100 - 2P = 3P - 10 \implies 5P = 110 \implies P_c = 22
$$

Producer price: $P_p = P_c - t = 12$.

Quantity: $Q = 100 - 2(22) = 56$.

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Burden per unit">

- Consumer burden  
  $P_c - P^* = 22 - 16 = 6$.
- Producer burden  
  $P^* - P_p = 16 - 12 = 4$.

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Elasticities at equilibrium">

At equilibrium:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_d &= \left| \frac{dQ_d}{dP} \frac{P}{Q} \right| = 2 \cdot \frac{16}{68} \approx 0.47 \\
E_s &= \frac{dQ_s}{dP} \frac{P}{Q} = 3 \cdot \frac{16}{68} \approx 0.71
\end{aligned}
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Verify burden split">

Consumer share $= \frac{0.71}{0.47 + 0.71} \times 10 \approx 6$. Producer share $\approx 4$.

Matches. Demand is inelastic relative to supply, so consumers bear more.

</StepItem>

</StepByStep>

</Solution>

### Subsidy Example: Elastic Demand

Market for electric vehicles. Demand: $Q_d = 200 - 5P$. Supply: $Q_s = 50 + 2P$.
Per-unit subsidy: $s = 20$.

Find the equilibrium price and quantity before and after the subsidy, and how the subsidy benefit splits between consumers and producers.

<Solution>

<StepByStep>

<StepItem label="Equilibrium before subsidy">

$$
200 - 5P = 50 + 2P \implies 7P = 150 \implies P^* = \frac{150}{7} \approx 21.43,\quad Q^* = \frac{700}{7} = 100
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Supply with subsidy">

$$
Q_s = 50 + 2(P + 20) = 2P + 90
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="New equilibrium">

$$
200 - 5P = 2P + 90 \implies 7P = 110 \implies P_c = \frac{110}{7} \approx 15.71
$$

Producer price: $P_p = P_c + s = \frac{110}{7} + 20 = \frac{250}{7} \approx 35.71$.

Quantity: $Q = 200 - 5(\frac{110}{7}) = \frac{850}{7} \approx 121.43$.

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Benefit per unit">

- Consumer benefit  
  $P^* - P_c = \frac{150}{7} - \frac{110}{7} = \frac{40}{7} \approx 5.71$.
- Producer benefit  
  $P_p - P^* = \frac{250}{7} - \frac{150}{7} = \frac{100}{7} \approx 14.29$.

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Verify with formula">

At original equilibrium:

$$
E_d = \left| \frac{dQ_d}{dP} \frac{P}{Q} \right| = 5 \cdot \frac{150/7}{100} = \frac{750}{700} \approx 1.07
$$

$$
E_s = \frac{dQ_s}{dP} \frac{P}{Q} = 2 \cdot \frac{150/7}{100} = \frac{300}{700} \approx 0.43
$$

Consumer share $= \frac{0.43}{1.07 + 0.43} \times 20 \approx 5.71$. Producer share $\approx 14.29$.

Matches. Demand is elastic relative to supply, so producers receive more.

</StepItem>

</StepByStep>

</Solution>

### Subsidy Example: Inelastic Demand

Market for insulin. Demand: $Q_d = 100 - P$. Supply: $Q_s = 20 + 4P$.
Per-unit subsidy: $s = 10$.

Find the equilibrium price and quantity before and after the subsidy, and how the subsidy benefit splits between consumers and producers.

<Solution>

<StepByStep>

<StepItem label="Equilibrium before subsidy">

$$
100 - P = 20 + 4P \implies 5P = 80 \implies P^* = 16,\quad Q^* = 84
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Supply with subsidy">

$$
Q_s = 20 + 4(P + 10) = 4P + 60
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="New equilibrium">

$$
100 - P = 4P + 60 \implies 5P = 40 \implies P_c = 8
$$

Producer price: $P_p = P_c + s = 18$.

Quantity: $Q = 100 - 8 = 92$.

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Benefit per unit">

- Consumer benefit  
  $P^* - P_c = 16 - 8 = 8$.
- Producer benefit  
  $P_p - P^* = 18 - 16 = 2$.

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Verify with formula">

At original equilibrium:

$$
E_d = \left| \frac{dQ_d}{dP} \frac{P}{Q} \right| = 1 \cdot \frac{16}{84} \approx 0.19
$$

$$
E_s = \frac{dQ_s}{dP} \frac{P}{Q} = 4 \cdot \frac{16}{84} \approx 0.76
$$

Consumer share $= \frac{0.76}{0.19 + 0.76} \times 10 \approx 8$. Producer share $\approx 2$.

Matches. Demand is inelastic relative to supply, so consumers receive more.

</StepItem>

</StepByStep>

</Solution>

### Price Floor Example

Market for wheat. Demand: $Q_d = 100 - 2P$. Supply: $Q_s = 20 + 3P$.
Price floor: $P_f = 25$ (above equilibrium).

Find the quantity demanded and quantity supplied at the floor, the resulting surplus, and the cost to the government of buying the surplus.

<Solution>

<StepByStep>

<StepItem label="Equilibrium without floor">

$$
100 - 2P = 20 + 3P \implies 5P = 80 \implies P^* = 16,\quad Q^* = 68
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Quantity demanded at floor price">

$$
Q_d = 100 - 2(25) = 50
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Quantity supplied at floor price">

$$
Q_s = 20 + 3(25) = 95
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Surplus">

$$
\text{Surplus} = Q_s - Q_d = 95 - 50 = 45 \text{ units}
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Government cost (if buying surplus)">

$$
\text{Cost} = \text{Surplus} \times P_f = 45 \times 25 = 1125
$$

</StepItem>

</StepByStep>

</Solution>

### Price Ceiling Example

Market for rental housing. Demand: $Q_d = 200 - 4P$. Supply: $Q_s = 20 + 2P$.
Price ceiling: $P_c = 20$ (below equilibrium).

Find the equilibrium without the ceiling, the quantity demanded and quantity supplied at the ceiling, the resulting shortage, and the deadweight loss.

<Solution>

<StepByStep>

<StepItem label="Equilibrium without ceiling">

$$
200 - 4P = 20 + 2P \implies 6P = 180 \implies P^* = 30,\quad Q^* = 80
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Quantity demanded at ceiling price">

$$
Q_d = 200 - 4(20) = 120
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Quantity supplied at ceiling price">

$$
Q_s = 20 + 2(20) = 60
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Shortage">

$$
\text{Shortage} = Q_d - Q_s = 120 - 60 = 60 \text{ units}
$$

</StepItem>

<StepItem label="Deadweight loss">

DWL $= \frac{1}{2} \times (Q^* - Q_s) \times (P_d - P_c)$ where $P_d$ is demand price at $Q_s$:

$$
P_d = \frac{200 - 60}{4} = 35
$$

DWL $= \frac{1}{2} \times (80 - 60) \times (35 - 20) = 150$

</StepItem>

</StepByStep>

</Solution>

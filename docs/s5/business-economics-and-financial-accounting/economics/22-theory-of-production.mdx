Production is the transformation of inputs or resources into goods and services.

Example: a bakery combines flour, ovens, labour, and time to transform inputs into bread.

## Production Function

Shows the relationship between the quantities of inputs used and the maximum output that can be produced efficiently.

The general production function is:

<Formula>

$$
Q = f(K,L,M)
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="Q">output</Sym>
  <Sym s="K">capital</Sym>
  <Sym s="L">labour</Sym>
  <Sym s="M">materials</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Run

### Short Run

A period during which at least one input is fixed.

For example, capital may be fixed while labour is variable.

### Long Run

A period during which all inputs are variable.

The distinction between short run and long run depends on whether inputs can be adjusted, not simply on the number of days, months, or years.

## Types of Input

### Fixed Input

An input whose quantity cannot be changed practically in the short run.

### Variable Input

An input whose quantity can be changed readily by the firm.

## Total Product

Total amount of output produced using a given amount of the variable input.

## Average Product

Total output divided by the amount of variable input used.

<Formula>

$$
AP_L = \frac{TP}{L}
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="AP_L">average product of labour</Sym>
  <Sym s="TP">total product</Sym>
  <Sym s="L">quantity of labour</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Marginal Product

The change in total output resulting from using one additional unit of the variable input while other factors remain constant.

<Formula>

$$
MP_L = \frac{\Delta TP}{\Delta L}
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="MP_L">marginal product of labour</Sym>
  <Sym s="TP">total product</Sym>
  <Sym s="L">quantity of labour</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Relationship Between TP, AP and MP

- When `MP > 0`, TP increases.
- When `MP = 0`, TP reaches its maximum.
- When `MP < 0`, TP decreases.
- When `MP > AP`, AP increases.
- When `MP < AP`, AP decreases.
- When `MP = AP`, AP is at its maximum.

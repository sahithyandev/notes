Income elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity
demanded to a change in consumer income.

$$
E_i =
\frac{\%\text{ change in quantity demanded}}
{\%\text{ change in income}}
$$

### Types of Goods

| Income Elasticity | Type of Good     | Effect of Income Increase                           |
| ----------------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Ei > 1            | Normal luxury    | Quantity demanded increases by a larger percentage  |
| 0 < Ei < 1        | Normal necessity | Quantity demanded increases by a smaller percentage |
| Ei < 0            | Inferior good    | Quantity demanded decreases                         |

### Normal Goods

Income elasticity is positive.

Normal goods can be divided into:

**Necessities**: 0 < Ei < 1

**Luxuries**: Ei > 1

### Inferior Goods

Income elasticity is negative.

When consumer income increases, quantity demanded decreases.

## Cross Elasticity of Demand

Cross elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity
demanded of one good to a change in the price of another good.

$$
E_{xy} =
\frac{\%\text{ change in quantity demanded of X}}
{\%\text{ change in price of Y}}
$$

### Types of Related Goods

| Cross Elasticity    | Relationship        |
| ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Exy > 0             | Substitute goods    |
| Exy < 0             | Complementary goods |
| Exy approximately 0 | Independent goods   |

### Substitute Goods

Substitute goods can be used instead of each other.

Cross elasticity is **positive**.

Example: Coca-Cola and Pepsi.

If the price of Coca-Cola increases, demand for Pepsi may increase.

### Complementary Goods

Complementary goods are normally consumed together.

Cross elasticity is **negative**.

Example: Mobile phones and mobile phone services.

If the price of mobile phones decreases, demand for mobile phone
services may increase.

### Independent Goods

Independent goods have little or no relationship in consumption.

Their cross elasticity is approximately zero.

## Price Elasticity of Supply

Price elasticity of supply measures the responsiveness of quantity
supplied to a change in price.

$$
E_s =
\frac{\%\text{ change in quantity supplied}}
{\%\text{ change in price}}
$$

### Categories of Price Elasticity of Supply

| Type                    | Elasticity | Meaning                                                      |
| ----------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Elastic Supply**      | Es > 1     | Quantity supplied changes by a larger percentage than price  |
| **Inelastic Supply**    | Es < 1     | Quantity supplied changes by a smaller percentage than price |
| **Unit Elastic Supply** | Es = 1     | Quantity supplied changes by the same percentage as price    |

## Worked Examples

### Income Elasticity Calculation

Income rises from 1,000 to 2,000 and quantity demanded rises from 10 to 20. Find the income elasticity of demand.

<Solution>

$$
\%\Delta Y = +100\%, \quad \%\Delta Q = +100\%
$$

$$
E_i = \frac{100\%}{100\%} = 1
$$

The good is a normal good with unitary income elasticity.

</Solution>

### Identifying Complements

The price of good A falls and the demand for good B rises. Identify the relationship between A and B.

<Solution>

The cross elasticity is negative, so A and B are complements.

For example: printers and ink.

</Solution>

### Zero Cross Elasticity

What is the relationship between 2 goods with a cross elasticity of zero?

<Solution>

A price change in one good does not affect the demand for the other, so the goods are independent and have no relationship.

Substitutes have positive cross elasticity and complements have negative cross elasticity.

</Solution>

### Elasticities and a Price Rise

For a good, $E_d = -2$ and $E_s = 1$. The price rises by 10%. Find the changes in quantity demanded and quantity supplied.

<Solution>

$$
\%\Delta Q_d = -2 \times 10\% = -20\%
$$

$$
\%\Delta Q_s = 1 \times 10\% = +10\%
$$

Quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded, so there is a [surplus](/s5/business-economics-and-financial-accounting/economics/market-equilibrium#surplus) that puts downward pressure on price.

</Solution>

Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures the responsiveness of
quantity demanded to a change in price.

$$
E_d = \frac{\%\text{ change in quantity demanded}}{\%\text{ change in price}}
$$

Because price and quantity demanded are inversely related, price
elasticity of demand has a negative sign. It is commonly expressed
using its absolute value.

## Categories of Price Elasticity

| Type                           |             Elasticity | Meaning                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Elastic Demand**             | $\lvert E_d\rvert > 1$ | Quantity demanded changes by a larger percentage than price               |
| **Inelastic Demand**           | $\lvert E_d\rvert < 1$ | Quantity demanded changes by a smaller percentage than price              |
| **Unit Elastic Demand**        | $\lvert E_d\rvert = 1$ | Quantity demanded changes by the same percentage as price                 |
| **Perfectly Inelastic Demand** |              $E_d = 0$ | Quantity demanded does not respond to price                               |
| **Perfectly Elastic Demand**   |         $E_d = \infty$ | A very small price change causes a very large change in quantity demanded |

## Determinants of Price Elasticity

### Elastic Demand

Demand tends to be more elastic when:

- Many close substitutes are available.
- The good takes up a large share of income.
- The good is a luxury or non-essential.
- Consumers have a longer time to adjust.
- The market is narrowly defined.

### Inelastic Demand

Demand tends to be more inelastic when:

- Few or no close substitutes are available.
- The good takes up a small share of income.
- The good is a necessity or essential.
- Consumers have little time to adjust.
- The market is broadly defined.

## Elasticity Along a Linear Demand Curve

Elasticity varies at different points along the same downward-sloping
linear demand curve.

- Upper-left region → More elastic
- Middle region → Unit elastic
- Lower-right region → More inelastic

For a straight-line demand curve, elasticity is therefore not constant
even though the slope is constant.

## Elasticity and Slope

Slope and elasticity are related but are not the same.

- A straight-line demand curve can have a **constant slope** but
  changing elasticity.
- A demand curve with **constant elasticity** does not have a
  constant slope.

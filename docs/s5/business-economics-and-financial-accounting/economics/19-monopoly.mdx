A market structure where a single seller dominates the market for a product or service. A monopoly has [market power](./market-structure#market-power).

Example: a local water network may be a natural monopoly because duplicating the pipes would waste resources.

## Output and Price

Compared with perfect competition:

<Formula>

$$
Q_m < Q_c
\quad
\text{and}
\quad
P_m > P_c
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="Q_m">monopoly quantity</Sym>
  <Sym s="Q_c">competitive quantity</Sym>
  <Sym s="P_m">monopoly price</Sym>
  <Sym s="P_c">competitive price</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Types

### Natural Monopoly

Occurs when very high infrastructure or start-up costs make it inefficient to have many competing firms.

Examples:

- A single electricity distribution network serving a region

### Government Monopoly

A government agency is the sole provider of a particular good or service, sometimes protected by law.

Examples:

- The United States Postal Service for non-urgent letter delivery
- A state-owned railway operator with exclusive rights on a route

### Technological Monopoly

Occurs when a firm controls a technology, manufacturing method, or patent that gives it exclusive production rights.

Examples:

- A pharmaceutical firm selling a patented medicine
- Pfizer's sale of Viagra while its patent was in force

### Geographic Monopoly

Occurs when one firm is the only provider of a good or service in a particular isolated area.

Examples:

- The only fuel station on a remote island
- The only grocery store in an isolated village

## Efficiency

A monopoly restricts output and charges a higher price.

This results in:

- Lower consumer surplus
- Higher producer surplus
- [Deadweight loss](./government-intervention#deadweight-loss)

## Price Discrimination

The practice of charging different prices to different buyers for the same or similar product, when the price differences are not based on cost differences.

Conditions:

- Market power  
  The firm must be able to set prices without competitors undercutting it.
- Separate groups of buyers  
  The firm must identify buyers who can be charged different prices.
- Different elasticities of demand  
  Buyer groups must respond differently to a change in price.
- Resale is not possible  
  Buyers paying a low price cannot resell to buyers facing a high price.

Can increase the firm's revenue and profit. Increases total consumption and reduces deadweight loss.

## Government Policy Toward Monopoly

Government may respond to monopoly through:

- Government ownership
- Competition policy
- Regulation
- Measures against price fixing and collusion

## Marginal Cost Pricing

An unregulated monopoly sets output where $MR = MC$.

<Formula>

$$
MR = MC
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="MR">marginal revenue, revenue from 1 additional unit sold</Sym>
  <Sym s="MC">marginal cost, cost of producing 1 additional unit</Sym>
  <Sym s="ATC">average total cost, total cost per unit of output</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

A regulator can force the monopoly to set price equal to marginal cost. Output is then where $MC$ intersects the demand curve ($AR$).

Effects:

- Output increases to the competitive level
- Price falls to $MC$
- Profit falls

<Note>

The monopoly makes a loss if $ATC > MC$, as with a natural monopoly.

</Note>

## Worked Examples

### Regulated Monopoly

A monopoly is forced to price at marginal cost. Which statement is incorrect?

- The monopoly sets output where $MC$ intersects $AR$
- The monopolist's price equals its marginal cost
- The monopoly moves from making a profit to making a loss
- The monopoly reduces its output

<Solution>

"The monopoly reduces its output" is incorrect. Under marginal cost pricing, output increases to the competitive level.

The first 2 statements are true by definition of the policy. The 3rd is plausible when $ATC > MC$.

</Solution>

Shows all combinations of two inputs that can produce the same level of output.

Example: a factory may produce 100 units with 4 machines and 10 workers, or 3 machines and 14 workers. Both combinations lie on the same isoquant.

In the long run, both capital and labour are variable.

<Formula>

$$
Q = f(K,L)
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="Q">output</Sym>
  <Sym s="K">capital</Sym>
  <Sym s="L">labour</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Properties

### Higher Isoquants Represent Higher Output

Isoquants farther from the origin represent higher levels of output.

### Isoquants Do Not Intersect

Two isoquants cannot intersect because each isoquant represents a different level of output.

### Isoquants Slope Downward

An isoquant normally slopes downward because increasing one input requires decreasing the other input to maintain the same output.

### Every Input Combination Has an Isoquant

There is an isoquant through every possible combination of inputs.

## Convex Isoquants

Most isoquants are generally **convex to the origin** when the two inputs are imperfect substitutes.

For a convex isoquant, the **MRTS is diminishing**.

## Perfectly Substitutable Inputs

When two inputs can substitute for each other at a constant rate:

- The isoquant is a straight line.
- MRTS is constant.
- Inputs can be substituted at a constant rate.

<PerfectSubstitutesDiagram />

## Perfectly Complementary Inputs

When inputs must be used in a specific ratio:

- The isoquant is right-angled.
- Efficient production occurs only at a specific input ratio.

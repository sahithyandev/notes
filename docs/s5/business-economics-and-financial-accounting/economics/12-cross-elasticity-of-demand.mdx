Cross elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity
demanded of one good to a change in the price of another good.

$$
E_{xy} =
\frac{\%\text{ change in quantity demanded of X}}
{\%\text{ change in price of Y}}
$$

### Types of Related Goods

| Cross Elasticity   | Relationship        |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| $E_{xy} > 0$       | Substitute goods    |
| $E_{xy} < 0$       | Complementary goods |
| $E_{xy} \approx 0$ | Independent goods   |

### Substitute Goods

Substitute goods can be used instead of each other.

Cross elasticity is **positive**.

Example: Coca-Cola and Pepsi.

If the price of Coca-Cola increases, demand for Pepsi may increase.

### Complementary Goods

Complementary goods are normally consumed together.

Cross elasticity is **negative**.

Example: Mobile phones and mobile phone services.

If the price of mobile phones decreases, demand for mobile phone
services may increase.

### Independent Goods

Independent goods have little or no relationship in consumption.

Their cross elasticity is approximately zero.

## Worked Examples

### Identifying Complements

The price of good A falls and the demand for good B rises. Identify the relationship between A and B.

<Solution>

The cross elasticity is negative, so A and B are complements.

For example: printers and ink.

</Solution>

### Zero Cross Elasticity

What is the relationship between 2 goods with a cross elasticity of zero?

<Solution>

A price change in one good does not affect the demand for the other, so the goods are independent and have no relationship.

Substitutes have positive cross elasticity and complements have negative cross elasticity.

</Solution>

Cross elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded of one good to a change in the price of another good.

$$
E_{xy} = \frac{\%\text{ change in quantity demanded of X}}{\%\text{ change in price of Y}}
$$

### Types of Related Goods

| Cross Elasticity   | Relationship        |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| $E_{xy} < 0$       | Complementary goods |
| $E_{xy} \approx 0$ | Independent goods   |
| $E_{xy} > 0$       | Substitute goods    |

### Interpretation

The magnitude of $E_{xy}$ indicates the strength of the relationship:

- **Close complements**  
  $E_{xy} \ll 0$ (large negative value). Example: printers and ink cartridges.
- **Weak complements**  
  $E_{xy} < 0$ but close to zero. Example: coffee and sugar.
- **Close substitutes**  
  $E_{xy} \gg 0$ (large positive value). Example: butter and margarine.
- **Weak substitutes**  
  $E_{xy} > 0$ but close to zero. Example: tea and coffee.

<Note>

Cross elasticity is asymmetric: $E_{xy}$ need not equal $E_{yx}$. The percentage changes use different bases.

</Note>

## Worked Example

Good X and good Y are substitutes. The price of Y increases from \$10 to \$12. Quantity demanded of X increases from 100 to 130 units.

Calculate $E_{xy}$.

<Solution>

Percentage change in price of Y:

$$
\%\Delta P_Y = \frac{12 - 10}{10} \times 100 = 20\%
$$

Percentage change in quantity demanded of X:

$$
\%\Delta Q_X = \frac{130 - 100}{100} \times 100 = 30\%
$$

Cross elasticity:

$$
E_{xy} = \frac{30\%}{20\%} = 1.5
$$

Since $E_{xy} > 0$, X and Y are substitutes. The magnitude $1.5$ indicates they are relatively close substitutes.

</Solution>

A market structure where a few large firms dominate the industry. Products may be [identical](./product#identical-products) or [differentiated](./product#differentiated-products). There are usually significant [barriers to entry](./market-structure#barriers-to-entry). Firms have substantial [market power](./market-structure#market-power).

Example: each mobile-network operator must consider how rivals will respond before changing its price or data plan.

## Collusion

Occurs when firms in an oligopoly cooperate to influence prices or production.

### Price Fixing

An agreement among firms to sell at the same or similar prices.

### Cartel

An association of producers formed to coordinate prices and production.

## Price War

Where firms in an oligopoly compete by reducing prices.

## Predatory Pricing

Occurs when a firm sets its price below cost for a short period to drive competitors out of the market.

After competitors leave, the firm may increase its price.

## Competition Policy

A government action that prevents firms from gaining excessive market power and protects competition.

### Anti-Trust Laws

Designed to encourage competition and limit monopoly or oligopoly power.

Four forms include:

1. Regulating business practices  
   Government can intervene when a firm has excessive market power.
2. Breaking up monopolies  
   Monopolies may be broken up to increase competition.
3. Blocking mergers  
   A merger may be blocked if it significantly reduces competition.
4. Preserving incentives  
   Mergers may be allowed when they provide benefits to consumers.

## Deregulation

Removal of some government controls over a market. Intended to promote competition.

Possible effects include:

- More competition
- Lower prices
- Greater variety
- Possible short-term job losses or business closures

## Worked Examples

### Identifying an Oligopoly

In the smartphone industry, firm ABC holds 35% of the market and firm XYZ holds 25%. R&D costs are heavy. Identify the market structure.

<Solution>

The market is an oligopoly.

- 2 firms hold 60% of the market
- Barriers to entry are high
- Rivals' decisions are interdependent

</Solution>

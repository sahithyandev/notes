Production can be divided into three stages based on the relationship between Total Product (TP), Average Product (AP), and Marginal Product (MP).

<TPAPMPDiagram />

### Stage 1

$$
MP > AP
$$

MP is greater than AP. AP and TP are increasing.

### Stage 2

$$
0 < MP < AP
$$

MP and AP are positive. MP is lower than AP. TP continues to increase.

This is the economically relevant stage of production.

### Stage 3

$$
MP < 0
$$

MP is negative. TP decreases.

Additional units of the variable input reduce total output.

## Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns

States that the marginal product of the variable input will eventually decline as more units of the variable input are added while the fixed input remains constant.

There is a limit to the output that can be produced in a productive facility of a given size and technology.

A short-run concept because at least one input is fixed.

## Marginal Revenue Product

<Aka>MRP</Aka>. Additional revenue generated by using one more unit of an input.

For labour:

<Formula>

$$
\text{MRP}_L = \text{MP}_L \times P
$$

<Symbols>
  <Sym s="\text{MRP}_L">Marginal Revenue Product of Labour</Sym>
  <Sym s="\text{MP}_L">Marginal Product of Labour</Sym>
  <Sym s="P">Price of output</Sym>
</Symbols>

</Formula>

## Marginal Resource Cost

<Aka>MRC</Aka>. Additional cost of using one more unit of a resource.

For labour:

$$
\text{MRC}_L = \text{Wage}
$$

## Optimal Use of Labour

A firm should increase its use of labour when the additional revenue generated is greater than the additional cost.

The optimal level occurs when:

$$
MRP_L = MRC_L
$$

Therefore:

- `MRP > MRC` → increase labour.
- `MRP < MRC` → decrease labour.
- `MRP = MRC` → optimal level of labour.

## Diminishing Returns and Cost

Diminishing marginal product means each added unit of the variable input adds less output. The cost of each extra unit of output, $MC$, therefore rises.

As output expands, the total cost curve becomes steeper.

## Worked Examples

### Marginal Product Under Diminishing Returns

What happens to $MP_L$ under diminishing marginal returns?

<Solution>

$MP_L$ decreases. Each extra worker adds less than the previous one because the fixed inputs are spread thinner.

$MP_L$ stays positive until total output actually falls.

</Solution>

### Shape of the Total Cost Curve

True or false. Because of diminishing marginal product, the total cost curve becomes flatter as output expands.

<Solution>

False.

Diminishing marginal product means rising marginal cost, so the total cost curve becomes steeper.

</Solution>

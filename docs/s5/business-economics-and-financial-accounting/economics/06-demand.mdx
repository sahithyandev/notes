Demand is the quantity of a good or service that consumers are
willing and able to purchase at various prices during a given period.

## Demand Curve

A demand curve shows the relationship between the price of a good
and the quantity demanded.

It normally slopes downward from left to right.

The demand curve generally slopes downward, with:

- **Price** on the vertical axis.
- **Quantity Demanded** on the horizontal axis.

## Law of Demand

Holding other factors constant, when the price of a good or service
increases, quantity demanded decreases. When the price decreases,
quantity demanded increases.

Therefore, price and quantity demanded are **inversely related**.

### Change in Quantity Demanded

A change in quantity demanded occurs because of a change in the
**price of the good itself**.

It causes a **movement along the same demand curve**.

- Price increases → quantity demanded decreases.
- Price decreases → quantity demanded increases.

### Why Does the Law of Demand Occur?

#### Substitution Effect

When the price of a good increases, consumers tend to substitute
relatively cheaper alternatives.

#### Income Effect

When the price of a good increases, the consumer's purchasing power
or real income decreases, so the consumer may buy less of the good.

## Demand Function

The relationship between quantity demanded and its determinants can
be represented as:

$$
Q_d = f(P_x, P_y, M, T, A, U)
$$

Where:

- $Q_d$ = quantity demanded of good X
- $P_x$ = price of good X
- $P_y$ = price of related goods
- $M$ = consumer income
- $T$ = consumer tastes and preferences
- $A$ = advertising effects
- $U$ = other unknown influences

## Determinants of Demand

A change in a non-price determinant causes the **demand curve to shift**.

Main determinants include:

- Number of consumers / population
- Consumer income
- Consumer tastes and preferences
- Prices of related goods
- Expectations about the future
- Advertising and other influences

### Income

For a normal good:

Income increases → Demand increases → Curve shifts right.

Income decreases → Demand decreases → Curve shifts left.

### Population

Population increases → Demand increases → Curve shifts right.

### Consumer Preferences

If a product becomes more fashionable or desirable,
demand increases and the curve shifts right.

### Prices of Related Goods

#### Substitutes

If the price of a substitute increases, demand for the original
good increases.

Example:

Price of coffee ↑ → Demand for tea ↑

#### Complements

If the price of a complement increases, demand for the original
good decreases.

Example:

Price of cars ↑ → Demand for petrol ↓

## Change in Demand

A change in any non-price determinant causes the entire demand curve
to shift.

| Change                 | Curve Shift                   | Equilibrium Price | Equilibrium Quantity |     |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------: | -------------------: | --- |
| **Increase in Demand** | Demand curve shifts **right** |                 ↑ |                    ↑ |     |
| **Decrease in Demand** | Demand curve shifts **left**  |                 ↓ |                    ↓ | ↓   |

## Demand Schedule

A demand schedule is a table showing the quantities consumers are
willing and able to purchase at different prices.

As price falls, quantity demanded generally increases.

## Movement Along vs Shift of Demand Curve

| Change                           | Result                      |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Change in price of the good      | Movement along demand curve |
| Change in income                 | Shift of demand curve       |
| Change in population             | Shift of demand curve       |
| Change in preferences            | Shift of demand curve       |
| Change in price of related goods | Shift of demand curve       |

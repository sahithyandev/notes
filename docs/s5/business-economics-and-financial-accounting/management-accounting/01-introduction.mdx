Management accounting is concerned with the provision and use of accounting information to managers within an organization, to facilitate their decision making and management control functions.

## Financial Accounting vs Management Accounting

### Objective

- Financial accounting  
  Stewardship for the benefit of shareholders.
- Management accounting  
  Improve the economy, efficiency, and effectiveness of operations.

### Time Horizon

- Financial accounting  
  Looks only at the past.
- Management accounting  
  Looks at the past, present, and future.

### Report Recipients

- Financial accounting  
  External parties, namely shareholders and government.
- Management accounting  
  Internal parties, namely directors and managers.

### Outputs

- Financial accounting  
  A summary annual statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, statement of financial position, and statement of cash flow.
- Management accounting  
  Detailed monthly and annual management accounts by product and function, plus adhoc reports.

### Regulating Framework

- Financial accounting  
  Accounting standards, accounting concepts, and statutory requirements under the Companies Act.
- Management accounting  
  None prescribed.

## Activities

- Explaining production and non-production costs and how they are reported in the financial statements.
- Computing the cost of providing a service or manufacturing a product.
- Determining the behavior of costs and expenses as activity levels change.
- Analyzing cost-volume-profit relationships within a company.
- Assisting management in profit planning and budgeting.
- Providing a basis for controlling costs and expenses by comparing actual results with planned objectives and standard costs.
- Accumulating and presenting relevant data for management decision making.

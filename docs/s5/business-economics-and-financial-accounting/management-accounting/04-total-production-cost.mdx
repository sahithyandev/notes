Sum of prime cost and manufacturing overhead cost.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Prime Cost} = \ &\text{Direct Material Cost} \\
+ \ &\text{Direct Labour Cost} \\
+ \ &\text{Direct Other Cost}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{Total Production Cost} = \text{Prime Cost} + \text{Production Overhead Cost}
$$

## Cost Structure

- Manufacturing / production cost  
  Direct material, direct labour, variable manufacturing overhead, fixed manufacturing overhead.
- Non-manufacturing / non-production cost  
  Variable non-manufacturing overhead, fixed non-manufacturing overhead. Any expense incurred after completion of manufacturing, or not incurred for production.

## Worked Example

Cost object: an electric car.

- Direct material cost  
  8,000, for the battery pack, motor, and body panels.
- Direct labour cost  
  1,200, for assembly and battery installation technicians.
- Direct other cost  
  300.

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Prime Cost} &= 8000 + 1200 + 300 \\
&= 9500
\end{aligned}
$

- Variable manufacturing overhead  
  450, electricity for robotic assembly lines and welding gas.
- Fixed manufacturing overhead  
  850, factory rent and the production manager's salary.

$
\text{Production Overhead Cost} = 450 + 850 = 1300
$

$
\text{Total Production Cost} = 9500 + 1300 = 10800
$

Variable non-manufacturing overhead (admin office electricity, sales commission) and fixed non-manufacturing overhead (administrative salaries, office depreciation) are excluded, since they're incurred after production or not for production.

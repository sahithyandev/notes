Perfect competition is a theoretical market structure with a large number of buyers and sellers selling identical products.

Perfect competition does not commonly exist in its pure form.

Agricultural markets are often considered close examples.

## Conditions for Perfect Competition

1. **Many buyers and sellers**  
   No individual buyer or seller can control the market.

2. **Identical products**  
   Products from different sellers are the same.

3. **Informed buyers and sellers**  
   Buyers and sellers have information about prices and product quality.

4. **Free entry and exit**  
   Firms can enter when profits are available and leave when they experience losses.

## Price Taker

A perfectly competitive firm is a **price taker**.

The market determines the equilibrium price, and an individual firm must accept that price.

Therefore, the demand curve facing an individual firm is horizontal.

## Entry and Exit of Firms

### Economic Profit

Economic profit attracts new firms into the market.

$$
\text{Economic Profit}
\rightarrow
\text{New Firms Enter}
\rightarrow
\text{Market Supply Increases}
\rightarrow
\text{Price Falls}
$$

Entry continues until economic profit becomes zero.

### Economic Loss

Economic loss causes some firms to leave the market.

$$
\text{Economic Loss}
\rightarrow
\text{Firms Exit}
\rightarrow
\text{Market Supply Decreases}
\rightarrow
\text{Price Rises}
$$

Exit continues until firms return to normal profit.

## Efficiency

Perfect competition can achieve:

### Productive Efficiency

Firms produce goods at the lowest possible cost.

### Allocative Efficiency

Resources are allocated toward the goods consumers want.

$$
MR = MC
$$

### Dynamic Efficiency

Firms have incentives to innovate and adapt to changes in consumer preferences.

The main forms of government intervention discussed are:

- Taxation
- Subsidies
- Price controls

## Taxation

An indirect tax increases production costs and shifts the supply curve
upward by the amount of the tax.

### Tax with Elastic Demand

When demand is elastic:

- Quantity demanded falls significantly.
- Government tax revenue is relatively lower.
- Producers bear a larger share of the tax burden.
- Consumers bear a smaller share of the tax burden.

### Tax with Inelastic Demand

When demand is inelastic:

- Quantity demanded falls only slightly.
- Government tax revenue is relatively higher.
- Consumers bear a larger share of the tax burden.
- Producers bear a smaller share of the tax burden.

## Subsidies

A subsidy reduces the per-unit cost of production and shifts the
supply curve downward.

### Subsidy with Elastic Demand

When demand is elastic:

- Quantity demanded increases significantly.
- Producers receive a larger share of the subsidy benefit.
- Consumers receive a smaller share through a lower price.

### Subsidy with Inelastic Demand

When demand is inelastic:

- Quantity demanded changes only slightly.
- Consumers receive a larger share of the subsidy benefit.
- Producers receive a smaller share of the benefit.

## Price Controls

Price controls are government restrictions on market prices.

### Price Floor

A price floor is a **minimum price set above the equilibrium price**.

Its purpose is to protect producers.

Examples:

- Minimum wage
- Agricultural price supports

Effects:

- Price is kept above equilibrium.
- Quantity supplied increases.
- Quantity demanded decreases.
- A **surplus** is created.

$$
Q_s > Q_d
$$

### Price Ceiling

A price ceiling is a **maximum price set below the equilibrium price**.

Its purpose is to protect consumers from high prices.

Examples:

- Rent controls
- Price caps on essential goods

Effects:

- Price is kept below equilibrium.
- Quantity demanded increases.
- Quantity supplied decreases.
- A **shortage** is created.

$$
Q_d > Q_s
$$

## Summary

| Intervention      | Elastic Demand                                  | Inelastic Demand                                |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Indirect Tax**  | Producers bear a larger share of the burden     | Consumers bear a larger share of the burden     |
| **Subsidy**       | Producers receive a larger share of the benefit | Consumers receive a larger share of the benefit |
| **Price Floor**   | Creates a surplus                               | Creates a surplus                               |
| **Price Ceiling** | Creates a shortage                              | Creates a shortage                              |

A **firm** is an organization that converts inputs such as labour, materials, and capital into outputs.

**Production** is the transformation of inputs or resources into goods and services.

The main inputs include:

- Capital (K)
- Labour (L)
- Materials (M)

## Production Function

A production function shows the relationship between the quantities of inputs used and the maximum output that can be produced efficiently.

The general production function is:

$$
Q = f(K,L,M)
$$

Where:

- `Q` = Output
- `K` = Capital
- `L` = Labour
- `M` = Materials

## Fixed and Variable Inputs

### Fixed Input

An input whose quantity cannot be changed practically in the short run.

### Variable Input

An input whose quantity can be changed readily by the firm.

## Short Run

The **short run** is a period during which at least one input is fixed.

For example, capital may be fixed while labour is variable.

Therefore:

$$
Q = f(L,K)
$$

When capital is fixed:

$$
Q = f(L)
$$

## Long Run

The **long run** is a period during which all inputs are variable.

The distinction between short run and long run depends on whether inputs can be adjusted, not simply on the number of days, months, or years.

## Total Product

**Total Product (TP)** is the total amount of output produced using a given amount of the variable input.

## Average Product

**Average Product (AP)** is total output divided by the amount of variable input used.

$$
AP_L = \frac{TP}{L}
$$

## Marginal Product

**Marginal Product (MP)** is the change in total output resulting from using one additional unit of the variable input while other factors remain constant.

$$
MP_L = \frac{\Delta TP}{\Delta L}
$$

## Relationship Between TP, AP and MP

- When `MP > 0`, TP increases.
- When `MP = 0`, TP reaches its maximum.
- When `MP < 0`, TP decreases.
- When `MP > AP`, AP increases.
- When `MP < AP`, AP decreases.
- When `MP = AP`, AP is at its maximum.

Production can be divided into three stages based on the relationship between Total Product (TP), Average Product (AP), and Marginal Product (MP).

### Stage 1

$$
MP > AP
$$

- AP is rising.
- MP is greater than AP.
- TP is increasing.

### Stage 2

$$
0 < MP < AP
$$

- MP is positive.
- MP is lower than AP.
- TP continues to increase.
- This is the economically relevant stage of production.

### Stage 3

$$
MP < 0
$$

- MP is negative.
- TP decreases.
- Additional units of the variable input reduce total output.

## Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns

The **Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns** states that the marginal product of the variable input will eventually decline as more units of the variable input are added while the fixed input remains constant.

There is a limit to the output that can be produced in a productive facility of a given size and technology.

### Key Point

As more units of the variable input are added to a fixed input:

$$
MP \text{ eventually decreases}
$$

This is a **short-run** concept because at least one input is fixed.

## Marginal Revenue Product

**Marginal Revenue Product (MRP)** is the additional revenue generated by using one more unit of an input.

For labour:

$$
MRP_L = MP_L \times P
$$

Where:

- `MRP_L` = Marginal Revenue Product of Labour
- `MP_L` = Marginal Product of Labour
- `P` = Price of output

## Marginal Resource Cost

**Marginal Resource Cost (MRC)** is the additional cost of using one more unit of a resource.

For labour:

$$
MRC_L = Wage
$$

## Optimal Use of Labour

A firm should increase its use of labour when the additional revenue generated is greater than the additional cost.

The optimal level occurs when:

$$
MRP_L = MRC_L
$$

Therefore:

- `MRP > MRC` → increase labour.
- `MRP < MRC` → decrease labour.
- `MRP = MRC` → optimal level of labour.

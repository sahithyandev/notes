## Evaluation Using Scenarios

**Scenario-based assessment** evaluates an architecture by examining scenarios relevant to the system.

Scenarios help assess whether architectural decisions support the required quality attributes.

## Scenario-Based Assessment Approaches

| Approach | Full Name                                  |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **ATAM** | Architecture Trade-off Analysis Method     |
| **CBAM** | Cost Benefits Analysis Method              |
| **SAAM** | Software Architecture Analysis Method      |
| **SBAR** | Scenario Based Architecture Re-Engineering |

## Architecture Trade-Off Analysis Method (ATAM)

**ATAM** is an architecture evaluation method used to analyze architectural decisions and their effects on quality attributes.

It helps identify:

- Trade-offs
- Sensitivity points
- Non-risks
- Risks
- Risk themes

## ATAM Process

The ATAM process illustrated connects business requirements, quality attributes, scenarios, architectural decisions, and analysis.

### Step 1: Identify Business Drivers

**Business drivers** provide the business context and goals that influence the architecture.

They help establish what the system needs to achieve.

### Step 2: Identify Quality Attributes

Identify the quality attributes that matter to the system.

These attributes provide criteria for examining the architecture.

### Step 3: Develop Scenarios

Scenarios describe situations that help evaluate whether the architecture satisfies the required quality attributes.

### Step 4: Examine the Software Architecture

The process considers the software architecture and the architectural approaches used to realize it.

### Step 5: Identify Architectural Decisions

Identify the architectural decisions that influence the system's quality attributes.

### Step 6: Analyze the Architecture

Analyze the relationship between:

- Business drivers
- Quality attributes
- Scenarios
- Architectural approaches
- Architectural decisions

The analysis identifies the consequences of architectural decisions.

### Step 7: Identify Analysis Results

The ATAM process identifies several important results.

| Result                 | Meaning                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade-offs**         | Decisions that affect multiple quality attributes, potentially improving one while negatively affecting another. |
| **Sensitivity Points** | Architectural decisions or properties that strongly affect a quality attribute.                                  |
| **Non-risks**          | Architectural decisions that have been identified as not posing a risk in the evaluated context.                 |
| **Risks**              | Architectural decisions or conditions that may prevent the system from achieving its required qualities.         |
| **Risk Themes**        | Groups or patterns of related risks identified during analysis.                                                  |

## Understanding ATAM Results

### Trade-Offs

A trade-off occurs when an architectural decision affects multiple quality attributes in different ways.

For example, improving one quality attribute may negatively affect another.

### Sensitivity Points

A sensitivity point is an architectural decision or property that has a significant effect on a quality attribute.

Changes to sensitivity points can have important consequences for the system.

### Risks and Risk Themes

A **risk** is an architectural concern that may prevent the system from achieving its quality requirements.

**Risk themes** summarize related risks to help stakeholders understand broader architectural concerns.

## Importance of ATAM

ATAM helps stakeholders understand how architectural decisions affect quality attributes.

It provides a structured approach to identifying trade-offs, sensitivity points, and risks before they become costly problems.

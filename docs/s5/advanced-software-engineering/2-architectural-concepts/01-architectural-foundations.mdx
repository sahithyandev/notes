## Software Architecture

A software system's architecture is the set of **principal design decisions** about the system.

Software architecture acts as a **blueprint** for the construction and evolution of a software system.

Architectural design decisions can cover:

- **Structure**
- **Behavior**
- **Interaction**
- **Non-functional properties**

## What Is "Principal"?

**Principal** means that a design decision has enough importance to give it **architectural status**.

Therefore, not every design decision is architectural or necessarily affects the system's architecture.

## Fundamental Understanding

There are three fundamental understandings of software architecture:

- Every application has an architecture.
- Every application has at least one architect.
- Architecture is not a phase in development.

## Temporal Aspect

Architecture has a **temporal aspect** because design decisions are made and unmade throughout a system's lifetime.

- At any given point in time, a system has one architecture.
- A system's architecture changes over time.

## Where Do Architectures Come From?

Architecture results from the interaction of:

- Stakeholders
- Development organization
- Technical environment
- Architect's experience
- Requirements
- Architect(s)

These influences lead to the architecture of the system.

## Architecture Business Cycle

The **Architecture Business Cycle (ABC)** describes the continuous relationship between architecture and its surrounding environment.

- Architectures are influenced by stakeholders, organizations, technical environments, and architect experience.
- These influences affect requirements and architectural decisions.
- The resulting architecture affects the system and its environment.
- The cycle continues through feedback.

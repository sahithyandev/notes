## How Do You Design?

Architectures can come from two main approaches:

### Method

A method provides a structured way of designing.

- Efficient in familiar situations.
- Produces more predictable outcomes.
- May not always be successful.
- The quality of methods can vary.

### Creativity

Creativity can provide new solutions.

- Can be enjoyable.
- Can involve uncertainty.
- May be unnecessary when an established method is sufficient.
- May produce the best solution.

## Design Objectives

The design process aims to:

- Enhance creativity and the designer's skillset.
- Provide new design tools.
- Focus on highly effective methods.
- Develop judgment about when to create novel solutions and when to follow established methods.

## Engineering Design Process

The engineering design process consists of four stages:

### Feasibility Stage

Identifying a set of feasible concepts for the design as a whole.

### Preliminary Design Stage

Selecting and developing the best concept.

### Detailed Design Stage

Developing detailed engineering descriptions of the selected concept.

### Planning Stage

Evaluating and modifying the concept to satisfy the requirements of:

- Production
- Distribution
- Consumption
- Product retirement

## Issues with the Linear Approach

The linear approach has limitations:

- If no feasible concepts can be produced, progress stops.
- As system size and complexity increase, it becomes harder for one individual to successfully perform the initial design steps.
- It does not directly address situations where the relationships among a set of products are important.
- Increasing complexity or insufficient designer experience may require alternative design approaches.

## Design Strategies

### Standard

Uses the linear design model.

### Cyclic

The process can return to an earlier stage.

### Parallel

Independent alternatives are explored in parallel.

### Adaptive

The next design strategy is decided at the end of each stage.

### Incremental

Each development stage incrementally improves the existing design.

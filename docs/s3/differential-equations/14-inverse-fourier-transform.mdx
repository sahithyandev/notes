Used to recover original function from its Fourier transform.

$$
f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\omega) e^{i\omega t} \, \text{d}\omega
$$

Provided that the integral converges.

## Convergence Conditions

The inverse Fourier transform exists when $F(\omega)$ is absolutely integrable:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |F(\omega)| \, \text{d}\omega < \infty
$$

## Properties

- Linearity  
  $\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{aF(\omega) + bG(\omega)\} = af(t) + bg(t)$
- Time shift  
  $\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{F(\omega) e^{-i\omega t_0}\} = f(t - t_0)$
- Frequency shift  
  $\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{F(\omega - \omega_0)\} = e^{i\omega_0 t} f(t)$
- Duality  
  $\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{F(t)\} = \frac{1}{2\pi} f(-\omega)$

## Comparison with Inverse Laplace Transform

| Property    | Inverse Fourier                 | Inverse Laplace                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --- | ----------- |
| Integration | Along the real $\omega$-axis    | Along a vertical line $\text{Re}(s) = c$      |
| Domain      | Bilateral (all $t$)             | Unilateral ($t \geq 0$)                       |
| Convergence | Requires absolute integrability | Requires $F(s)$ to decay as $                 | s   | \to \infty$ |
| Typical use | Signal analysis, filters        | System response, ODEs with initial conditions |

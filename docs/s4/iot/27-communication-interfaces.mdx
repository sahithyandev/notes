## Local

Short-range, board-level or chip-to-chip protocols for connecting an MCU to sensors, actuators, and peripherals.

### UART

Universal Asynchronous Receiver/Transmitter. Asynchronous: no shared clock. Both ends must be configured to the same baud rate.

Pins: TX (transmit) and RX (receive).

Properties:

- Full duplex
- Point-to-point only (no bus topology)
- Common baud rates: 9600, 115200 bps

Used for: serial debug monitor, GPS modules, Bluetooth adapter modules, GSM modems.

### SPI

Serial Peripheral Interface. Synchronous: master generates the clock.

Pins: MOSI (master out, slave in), MISO (master in, slave out), SCK (clock), CS (chip select, one per slave).

Properties:

- Full duplex
- Single master, multiple slaves (each slave requires a dedicated CS line)
- Fast: up to tens of MHz depending on hardware
- No addressing — CS line selects the active slave

Used for: displays, SD cards, external ADC/DAC chips, flash memory.

### I²C

Inter-Integrated Circuit. Synchronous. Two-wire open-drain bus requiring pull-up resistors on both lines.

Pins: SDA (bidirectional data), SCL (clock).

Properties:

- Half duplex
- Multi-master, multi-slave on the same two wires
- Each device has a unique 7-bit (or 10-bit) address sent at the start of each transaction
- Standard mode: 100 kHz. Fast mode: 400 kHz. Fast-plus: 1 MHz

Used for: IMUs, temperature and pressure sensors, EEPROMs, real-time clocks.

### 1-Wire

Single data line (plus ground). Semi-duplex. Supports multiple devices on the same wire, each identified by a unique 64-bit ROM code.

Very low speed. Used where minimal pin count matters: temperature sensors (DS18B20), iButton authentication devices.

### Parallel Slave Port

Aka. PSP. A parallel interface that allows an external master processor to read and write the MCU's internal registers directly over a multi-bit data bus.

Unlike serial interfaces (UART, SPI, I²C), a PSP transfers a full byte (or word) simultaneously across multiple data lines.

Pin groups:

- Data bus  
  8 (or 16) bidirectional data lines carrying one byte per transfer.
- Address lines  
  Select which internal register or memory location the master is targeting.
- Control lines  
  CS (chip select), RD (read strobe), WR (write strobe). The master asserts these to initiate a transfer.

Operation: the external master places an address on the address lines, asserts CS and RD or WR, and the MCU responds by placing data on the bus or latching the incoming byte.

Use cases:

- MCU acting as a peripheral co-processor alongside a more powerful host CPU
- Display controllers and memory-mapped peripherals on embedded boards
- High-speed sensor data transfer where serial overhead is too slow

<Note>

PSP requires many pins (8 data + address + control). It is only practical when pin count is not constrained and transfer speed justifies it. Most modern IoT designs prefer SPI or I²C.

</Note>

## Network

Protocols for connecting IoT devices to local networks or wide-area infrastructure.

### Wi-Fi

IEEE 802.11. Operates at 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz. High bandwidth and low latency. High power consumption relative to other wireless IoT protocols.

Used for cloud connectivity, over-the-air updates, and applications requiring high data rates.

### Bluetooth and BLE

Operates at 2.4 GHz.

- Bluetooth Classic  
  Higher throughput. Used for audio streaming and file transfer.
- Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)  
  Very low power. Short bursts of small data. Used for sensor beacons, wearables, and proximity detection.

Range: approximately 10–100 m depending on environment.

### Zigbee

IEEE 802.15.4. Operates at 2.4 GHz. Very low power. Supports mesh networking: devices relay packets for each other, extending range without infrastructure.

Low bandwidth (~250 kbps). Used for home automation, lighting control, and dense sensor networks.

### LoRa / LoRaWAN

Operates in sub-GHz ISM bands (868 MHz in Europe, 915 MHz in the Americas). Spread-spectrum modulation enables kilometre-range links at very low transmit power.

Very low bandwidth (< 50 kbps). Used for wide-area sensor deployments: agriculture, smart metering, asset tracking.

### Ethernet

IEEE 802.3. Wired. High reliability and deterministic latency. Not affected by wireless interference.

Used in industrial IoT, building automation, and SBC-based devices (Raspberry Pi).

### Cellular

NB-IoT and LTE-M are cellular standards designed for IoT:

- Low bandwidth, low power, wide area coverage
- Devices connect directly to cellular infrastructure without a local gateway
- Used for mobile asset tracking, remote metering, and deployments in areas without Wi-Fi

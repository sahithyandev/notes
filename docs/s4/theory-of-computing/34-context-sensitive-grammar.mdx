Aka. CSG. A grammar $G = (V, \Sigma, S, P)$ where every production $\alpha \to \beta$ satisfies $|\beta| \geq |\alpha|$.

Equivalent form: every production has the form

$$
\alpha A \beta \to \alpha X \beta
$$

where:

- $A \in V$ (single non-terminal on LHS surrounded by context)
- $X \in (V \cup \Sigma)^+$ (non-empty replacement)
- $\alpha, \beta \in (V \cup \Sigma)^*$ (arbitrary context strings)

The context $\alpha$ and $\beta$ must be identical on both sides. The non-terminal $A$ is replaced only in that specific context.

## Context-Sensitive Language

Aka. CSL. Languages generated by a CSG. Accepted by [linear-bounded automata](/s4/theory-of-computing/linear-bounded-automata).

Every context-free language is context-sensitive. Not every CSL is context-free.

$$
\text{CFL} \subsetneq \text{CSL}
$$

## Example

The language $L = \{a^n b^n c^n \mid n \ge 1\}$ is CSL but not CFL. A CSG generating $L$:

Productions:

- $S \to aSBC \mid aBC$
- $CB \to BC$
- $aB \to ab$
- $bB \to bb$
- $bC \to bc$
- $cC \to cc$

The key production $CB \to BC$ allows $B$s and $C$s to swap positions, a context-sensitive rewrite that no CFG production $A \to \alpha$ can express, because the LHS has 2 non-terminals.

Suppose $W$ is a subset of $V$. $W\perp$ is the set of all vectors that are orthogonal to all vectors of $W$. Denoted by $W^{\perp}$

$$
W\perp = \set { u \in V | \langle u,w \rangle = 0\; \forall w \in W }
$$

**If** $x \in W\cap W^\perp$ **then** $x = \underline{0}$. $W\cap W^\perp$ can either be a null set or $\set{ \underline{0} }$.

$W^\perp$ is a subspace of $V$ for any $W$.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- $W^\perp$ is non-empty ($\underline{0}$ for sure)
- $W^\perp \subseteq V$
- $W^\perp$ is closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication

</Note>

$(W^\perp)^\perp \supseteq W$. They are not equal though.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

Start with $\langle x,w \rangle = 0$ for all $x \in W^\perp$. And the proof is obvious.

</Note>

## Theorem

Assume $W$ be a subspace of $V$ over $F$ having an orthonormal basis $(w_i)$. Let:

- $u \in V$
- $P(u) = \sum \langle w_i, u \rangle w_i \in W$

Then:

- $u - P(u) = Q(u) \in W^\perp$
- $|| u - P(u)|| \le ||u - w|| $ for all $w \in W$

<Note title="Proof Hint">

For the first part:

- Start with $\langle w_i, u-P(u) \rangle$
- Substitute $P(u)$ and the expression becomes $0$
- For all $w \in W$, it can be written as a linear combination of $w_i$
- Which means $u-P(u) \in W^\perp$

Aka. countermeasure. Protective measure to remove or reduce 1 or more vulnerabilities. Must know as much as threats to establish control for each.

Control mechanism changes based on kind of threats, control types and what they protects.

## Effectiveness

Determined by:

### Awareness

People must know a control exists and why it matters. Controls that go unnoticed are ignored or bypassed.

### Usage Preference

Even aware users may avoid a control if it takes too much space, time, or effort. Adoption depends on how lightweight the control feels.

### Usage

A control applied incorrectly or inconsistently leaves gaps. Correct and consistent use is required for it to work.

### Periodic Review

Threats and systems change over time. Regular reassessment ensures controls stay relevant and effective.

### Overlapping Vulnerabilities

A single control may be applied to multiple vulnerabilities. The more, the better.

## Principle of Weakest Link

Security is only as strong as the weakest link.

## Types

### Physical

Preventing the physical systems from being reached. Locking the server room, for example.

### Technical

Can either be software or hardware.

Examples:

- Encryption
- Hardware
  Devices like encryption cards, locks, and firewalls.
- Software  
  Includes program security, access controls, and antivirus tools.

### Administrative

#### Policies & Procedures

Organizational practices like password changes and regular audits.

#### Access Control

Policy defines who can access what resources and how.

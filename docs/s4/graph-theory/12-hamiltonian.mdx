A graph is said to be Hamiltonian **iff** it contains a [Hamiltonian cycle](/s4/graph-theory/walk/#hamiltonian-cycle). Introduced by William Hamilton in 1857, while studying cycles in the dodecahedron graph.

## Dirac’s Theorem

A simple graph with $n (\ge 3)$ vertices is Hamiltonian **if** every vertex has degree at least $n/2$.

$$
\forall v\;\text{deg}(v) \ge \frac{n}{2}
$$

## Ore’s Theorem

A simple graph with $n \ge 3$ vertices is Hamiltonian **if** for every pair of non-adjacent vertices $u$ and $v$:

$$
\text{deg}(u) + \text{deg}(v) \ge n
$$

<Note>

There are Hamiltonian graphs that do not satisfy these conditions.

</Note>

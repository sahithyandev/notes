Aka. inverse. For a simple graph $G=(V,E)$, its complement is $G^c(V,E^c)$ where $E^c = \set{ \set{u,v}; \forall u,v \in V \land u \neq v \land \set{u,v} \not\in E } $. An edge between two vertices **iff** they are **not** adjacent in $G$.

Undefined for multigraphs.

$(G^c)^c = G$.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Suppose $G=(V,E)$
- $G^c(V,E^c)$ 
- For $(G^c)^c$, it has the same vertex set and $(E^c)^c = E$ based on the definition above.

OR:

- Suppose $\set{u,v} \in E$
- $E^c$ does not have this
- Hence it must exist in $(E^c)^c$
- Prove the other way as well

</Note>

For any graph $G$ with $n$ vertices, when merged (unioned) with its complement graph $G^c$, it produces $K_n$.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Suppose $G=(V,E)$ has $n$ vertices
- $G^c(V,E^c)$ is its complement
- For all pair of vertices $u$ and $v$:
  - If an edge exists between then in $G$, it exists in $G\cup G^c$. 
  - If not, it exists in $G^c$ and in $G\cup G^c$. 
- $G\cup G^c$ has edges between all pairs of vertices. Hence it is $K_n$.

</Note>

## Self-Complementary Graph

A graph satisfying $G = G^c$.

Has $\frac{n(n-1)}{4}$ edges.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- $G\cup G^c = K_n$ has $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ edges.
- As $G = G^c$, they both have the same number of edges.
- Hence $|E(G)| = \frac{n(n-1)}{4}$

</Note>

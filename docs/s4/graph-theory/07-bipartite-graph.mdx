A graph where vertices can be split into disjoint sets $V_1$ and $V_2$ such that:

- All edges go between $V_1$ and $V_2$,
- No edges exist inside $V_1$ or inside $V_2$.

Complete graphs are **not** bipartite.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

All vertices are connected to all remaining vertices. Hence there cannot be 2 sets. 

</Note>

If a graph contains a cycle of odd length, it is not bipartite.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Label the vertices in the cycle as $1,2,\dots,2k-1$
- Each $k$ vertex is directly connected to $k+1$. And $2k-1$ is connected to $1$.
- Odd numbers and even numbers cannot be in the same set. $1$ and $2k-1$ cannot be in the same set.

</Note>

### Bipartition Set

The disjoint sets ($V_1$ and $V_2$) in the above process.

## Complete Bipartite Graph

Every vertex in $V_1$ is connected to every vertex in $V_2$. Denoted by $K_{m,n}$. $m,n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Has $m \times n$ edges.

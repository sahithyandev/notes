A graph where vertices can be split into disjoint sets $V_1$ and $V_2$ such that:

- All edges go between $V_1$ and $V_2$,
- No edges exist inside $V_1$ or inside $V_2$.

Complete graphs are **not** bipartite.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

All vertices are connected to all remaining vertices. Hence there cannot be 2 sets.

</Note>

If a graph contains a cycle of odd length, it is not bipartite.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Label the vertices in the cycle as $1,2,\dots,2k-1$
- Each $k$ vertex is directly connected to $k+1$. And $2k-1$ is connected to $1$.
- Odd numbers and even numbers cannot be in the same set. $1$ and $2k-1$ cannot be in the same set.

</Note>

Subgraph of a bipartite graph is also bipartite.

<Note title="Proof Hint">

- Suppose $G$ has $G_1$ and $G_2$ biparitions. And $H$ be a subgraph of $G$.
- Every edge in $G$ has one endpoint in $G_1$ and other in $G_2$
- $V(H) \subseteq V(G)$
- Suppose $A = G_1 \cap V(H)$ and $B = G_2 \cap V(H)$
- $A$ and $B$ are a valid parition pair.

$$
A' = A \cap V(H), \qquad B' = B \cap V(H)
$$

**Claim:** $(A', B')$ is a valid bipartition of $H$.

**Verification**

**(1) They partition $V(H)$:**
Since every vertex of $H$ belongs to $V \subseteq A \cup B$, it lies in exactly one of $A'$ or $B'$. So $A' \cup B' = V(H)$ and $A' \cap B' = \emptyset$. ✓

**(2) Every edge of $H$ goes between $A'$ and $B'$:**

Take any edge $e = (u, v) \in E(H)$. Since $E(H) \subseteq E$, this edge also belongs to $G$. Since $G$ is bipartite with partition $(A, B)$, one endpoint lies in $A$ and the other in $B$ — say $u \in A$, $v \in B$.

Since $u \in V(H)$ and $u \in A$, we have $u \in A'$. Similarly, $v \in B'$.

So every edge of $H$ crosses between $A'$ and $B'$. ✓

**Conclusion**

$H$ admits the bipartition $(A', B')$, so $H$ is bipartite. $\blacksquare$

</Note>

### Bipartition Set

The disjoint sets ($V_1$ and $V_2$) in the above process.

## Complete Bipartite Graph

Every vertex in $V_1$ is connected to every vertex in $V_2$. Denoted by $K_{m,n}$. $m,n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Has $m \times n$ edges.
